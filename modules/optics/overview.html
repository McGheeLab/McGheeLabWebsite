Optics Foundations

How light works — from a single atom to a microscope

Five interactive lessons that build optics from the photon up. Every concept comes with a live simulation you drive yourself: tune a light wave through the rainbow, watch an atom swallow a color, trap light inside glass, bend it with a lens, make cloudy tissue turn clear, and split a laser into interference fringes. Each lesson opens at a high-school level and finishes with the real equations a second-year science or engineering student uses.

The five lessons

do them in order — each builds on the last
1

Light & Matter — Why Things Absorb Color

A red apple isn't "made of red" — its atoms swallowed every color except red. See light as an electromagnetic wave, watch an atom absorb only the photon that matches an energy gap, and learn why a solid absorbs a whole band of color.

Light is a wave (c = λf) → atoms have energy steps (E = hc/λ) → one atom to a solid → level up: band gaps set a material's color (λc = 1240/E_g)

Open lesson →
2

Color, Reflection & Trapped Light

The color you see is the light that bounced back — and the angle light makes with a surface decides whether it escapes or gets trapped by total internal reflection. Press a finger onto trapped light and it leaks out: that's a fingerprint scanner.

Color is what reflected → reflect vs refract (Snell) → total internal reflection → level up: critical angle & frustrated TIR (fingerprint sensors)

Open lesson →
3

Bending Light — Lenses

Curve a surface and light bends in a controlled way — that's a lens. Trace rays through converging and diverging lenses, watch an image form at the focal plane, and see how the spacing between two lenses turns them into a telescope.

A curved surface focuses → converging vs diverging → focal plane & image formation → level up: thin-lens & lensmaker equations, compound systems

Open lesson →
4

Scattering, Index Matching & Metamaterials

Tissue looks cloudy because light scatters at every index mismatch — but match the surrounding fluid and it turns clear, letting you see deep inside. Then engineer sub-wavelength surface features and you can bend light on purpose: the flat metamaterial lens.

Why things look cloudy → index matching clears them → layered scattering → level up: Rayleigh scattering (1/λ⁴) & metasurface lenses

Open lesson →
5

Coherence, Interference & the Microscope

The capstone. Ordinary light rarely interacts with itself, but coherent laser light interferes into crisp fringes — patterns that set the ultimate resolution limit. Trace light through a microscope with a lamp and with a laser, and see what decides how small you can see.

When light meets light → interference encodes detail → the microscope light path → level up: the resolution limit (0.61λ/NA), widefield vs confocal
