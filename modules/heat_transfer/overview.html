Heat Transfer Foundations

Why things heat up, cool down & stay warm

Six interactive lessons that build heat transfer from the atom up — no calculus required. Every concept comes with a live simulation you drive yourself: shake a lattice of atoms hot, race water against copper, watch heat crawl through a wall, trap air inside a jacket, cool a glowing heatsink, and bake a black car in the sun. Each lesson starts at a high-school level and finishes with the real equations engineers use.

The six lessons

do them in order — each builds on the last
1

Heat Is Jiggling Atoms

Heat isn't a substance — it's atoms vibrating, and temperature is how hard they shake. Warm a lattice, watch energy get handed neighbor to neighbor, and see why conduction, convection, and radiation are three ways the jiggle gets around.

Temperature is jiggle → passing the jiggle takes time → three ways heat travels → level up: the diffusion clock (τ ≈ L²/α)

2

Specific Heat — Holding the Jiggle

Put the same flame under water and under copper and they warm at wildly different rates. Specific heat is how much energy a material soaks up per degree — and it's why the beach sand burns your feet while the ocean stays cool.

Some materials hold more heat → Q = mcΔT → heating takes time (power is the slope) → level up: calorimetry & Dulong–Petit

Open lesson → Worksheet (print)
3

Crossing the Boundary

Put a wall between hot and cold — how fast does heat cross? Watch carrier dots stream through a wall as a torrent through copper and a trickle through air, and learn why thickness, area, and material each change the flow.

A wall between hot and cold → material matters (conductivity k) → thickness & area → level up: Fourier's law & thermal resistance

Open lesson → Worksheet (print)
4

Layers, Air Gaps & Insulation

Real walls are layers within layers. Air is a superb insulator — but only if it can't circulate. Stack boundaries, trap air in millions of tiny voids, and find out why a puffy jacket full of nothing keeps you warm.

Boundaries inside boundaries → air: great insulator only when still → millions of tiny voids → level up: R-value & resistance networks

Open lesson → Worksheet (print)
5

Fins & Flowing Air

Now move heat away on purpose. Blowing air strips heat off a hot surface, and adding fins multiplies the surface it can strip from. Blow on soup, then design a spiky heatsink and watch it shed watts.

Air steals heat from surfaces → blow on it (forced convection) → why heatsinks are spiky → level up: fin efficiency & the Biot number

Open lesson → Worksheet (print)
6

Radiation & Color

The capstone: heat that travels as light, needing nothing in between. Absorbed photons become jiggle, so color decides how hot you get — which is why the black car seat blisters and the white one stays merely warm.

Light is energy delivery → why the black car is an oven → everything glows → level up: Stefan–Boltzmann & Kirchhoff
