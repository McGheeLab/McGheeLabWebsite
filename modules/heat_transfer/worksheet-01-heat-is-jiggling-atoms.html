TAB 4The diffusion clock, τ = L²/α

Goal: use the diffusion clock to predict real soak times — and see why thick walls take days.

Predict

Making a slab 10× thicker makes heat take…  ☐ 10× longer   ☐ 100× longer   ☐ 1000× longer to soak through.

Do

  1. Click Copper and set Length scale L to 1 cm. Read τ from the highlighted stat.
  2. Click Wood, keep L = 1 cm, read τ. Then drag L to 10 cm and read τ again.
  3. Still on Wood, slide L until the dot on the log-log plot sits on the 1 day line. Read the L readout.

Big picture — put it together

1. The metal spoon and the wooden spoon are at the same temperature, but the metal one feels colder. In three sentences, explain why. Use the words jiggle, hand-off, and diffusivity.

2. Challenge (optional): a copper atom's rattle is about 25 pm at room temperature and the spacing between atoms is about 250 pm. Roughly what temperature would push the rattle to 30% of the spacing (about 75 pm)? Remember amplitude ∝ √T, so 3× the amplitude needs 9× the temperature. T ≈ K. Copper melts at 1358 K — is your answer above or below that? What does that tell you about the rule of thumb?
