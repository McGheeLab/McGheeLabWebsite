Surface Functionalization Foundations

How to make a surface do what you want — from the atoms up

Four interactive lessons that build a real understanding of surfaces from the atom up — no chemistry background required. Every concept comes with a live simulation you drive yourself: pull an atom out of a surface, strike a glowing plasma, graft silane molecules onto –OH groups, and texture a surface until water bounces off it. Each lesson starts at a high-school level and finishes at sophomore-undergrad depth with the real equations scientists use.

How to use this course

three steps
1

Understand why surfaces are hungry

Start with why atoms at a surface are reactive, why a surface is never truly clean, and what "surface energy" really measures.

2

Activate & coat the surface

Use plasma to wake a surface up with fresh –OH groups, then graft silane molecules onto them to install exactly the chemistry you want.

3

Engineer the texture

Add nano- and micro-scale roughness to trap air, make water bounce off, and design surfaces that resist wetting and fouling.

The four lessons

do them in order — each feeds the next
1

Surfaces Are Hungry: Surface Energy & Adsorbed Water

Why atoms at a surface are energetically unbalanced, how that becomes measurable surface energy, why ambient water films coat everything, and how vacuum (slowly) strips them off to give a bare, functionalization-ready surface.

Missing Neighbors → Surface Energy → Water Coats Everything → Level Up: Vacuum & Kinetics

Open lesson → Worksheet (print)
2

Plasma Treatment: Energizing Gas to Rewrite a Surface

Pump down, backfill process gas, use RF energy to ionize it into a glowing plasma, and let reactive species graft new chemical groups onto the surface. Includes the Paschen curve and why different gases give different chemistry.

Why Pump Down First → Striking a Plasma → Plasma Colors → Level Up: Gas → Chemistry

Open lesson → Worksheet (print)
3

Silanes: Molecular Paint for Surfaces

One universal head group condenses onto surface –OH; the swappable tail sets the new surface chemistry — from adhesion-promoting amines to fluorinated ultra-low-energy coatings. Ends at Young's equation for contact angle.

The Common Head → The Goldilocks Reaction → The Tail Decides → Level Up: Young's Equation

Open lesson → Worksheet (print)
4

Roughness: How Texture Makes Water Bounce

Nano/micro texture traps air pockets so water can't wet in — the lotus effect. Explore Wenzel vs Cassie–Baxter states, Cassie collapse, and Sharklet anti-bacterial textures.

The Missing Ingredient → Wenzel: Roughness Amplifies → Cassie–Baxter: Trapped Air → Level Up: Lotus, Collapse & Sharklet

Open lesson → Worksheet (print)

For teachers: the four lessons feed each other in order — a clean surface, then plasma to make –OH groups, then silanes that graft onto those –OH, then texture for extreme repellency — so work them front to back. Each lesson starts at a high-school level and its final tab escalates toward sophomore-undergraduate depth, and can be treated as extension material. The companion worksheets follow a predict → experiment → record → explain cycle and every question is answerable directly from the on-screen simulations.
