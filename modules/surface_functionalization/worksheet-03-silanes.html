TAB 1The Common Head

Goal: see that the silane head can only weld where the surface offers an –OH anchor — so graft density tracks –OH density. No –OH, no silane.

Predict

Do

  1. Click the Plasma-activated (OH-rich) scenario. Watch silanes drift down, dock on an –OH stud, and puff off a leaving-group molecule. Fill in row A from the stats panel.
  2. Drag Surface –OH density (10–100%) down to about 20% (or click Barely activated (OH-poor)) and fill in row B. Notice how patchy the carpet gets.
  3. Return –OH density to 80%, then drag Leaving group from methoxy to chloro (or click Chlorosilane (fast)). Fill in row C. Watch the Byproduct stat change.

TAB 2The Goldilocks Reaction

Goal: find the narrow water window where a clean monolayer forms — too dry means no reaction, too wet means silanes self-polymerize into clumps.

Predict

Monolayer quality will be highest when the water content is…  ☐ ~0% (bone dry)   ☐ ~15% (a trace)   ☐ ~70% (soaked)

Do

  1. Set Silane concentration to 1.0% and leave it there. Sweep Water content (0–100%) to each value in the table and read Monolayer quality (%) and Clumping (%) from the stats. (Scenario shortcuts: Too dry, Trace water, Too wet.)
  2. Watch the verdict box flip: no-reaction → ideal monolayer → cauliflower clumps.
  3. Now set water to a wet value (~70%) and raise Silane concentration (0.2–5%). Does quality get better or worse? Who is the silane reacting with?

TAB 3The Tail Decides

TAB 4Level Up: Young's Equation

Goal: turn "it beads" into a number. Compute the contact angle from three surface tensions, and discover the flat-surface ceiling near 120°.

Predict

On a flat surface, the largest water contact angle θ you can reach with the best chemistry is about…  ☐ 90°   ☐ 120°   ☐ 150°   ☐ 180°

Do

  1. First compute by hand using \(\cos\theta = (\gamma_{sv}-\gamma_{sl})/\gamma_{lv}\), then set the three sliders (Solid–vapor γ_sv, Solid–liquid γ_sl, Liquid γ_lv) to match and check the cos θ and Contact angle θ stats.
  2. Case 1: γ_sv = 40, γ_sl = 30, γ_lv = 72.8 (the APTMS scenario).
  3. Case 2: γ_sv = 20, γ_sl = 50, γ_lv = 72.8.
  4. Slide γ_sv from 300 down to 5 (others fixed) and note where θ crosses 90°. Then switch the liquid to oil with the Fluorosilane + oil scenario (γ_lv → 27).
  5. Try to reach θ = 150° with any combination that keeps γ_sl ≤ 80. Record the highest θ you can hit.
