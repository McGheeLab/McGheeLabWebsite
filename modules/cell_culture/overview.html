Cell Culture Foundations

Everything that goes wrong is a physical process

Cell culture is taught as a list of rules, and the rules are correct — but a rule you cannot derive is a rule you will break the first time it is inconvenient. This series takes the four things that actually kill cultures — the cabinet, the incubator, your hands, and the bottle everyone shares — and works out what is physically happening in each, so the rules stop being arbitrary. Every claim is measured in a simulation on the page before it is explained.

How to use this series

four lessons, in order
1

Drive the sim first

Each section opens with something to measure. Move the sliders and read the number before you read the prose — the derivation is there to explain a result you already have.

2

Do the "Try this" steps

Every simulation has two to five numbered things to do with it. They are the difference between watching a picture move and finding out what it means.

3

Then take it to the bench

Work through all four, then take the lab's Cell culture & sterile technique training on the RM hub, which ends in a bench sign-off.

A note on honesty. Every model in this series states where it stops being true. Toy simulations are labelled as such, vendor rules of thumb are checked against the physics rather than repeated, and sections written from this lab's own practice — rather than from a published source — are marked McGhee Lab practice. When your bench disagrees with a number here, your bench is right; go and find out why.
