Equipment Foundations

Every instrument is telling you something slightly different from what you asked

A balance does not measure mass, it measures a current. A pipette does not move liquid, it moves an air spring. A pH meter does not measure acidity, it measures a voltage across a piece of glass. Each of those gaps is where the errors live — and every one of them is calculable, which means every one of them can be designed around instead of guessed at. This series takes the lab's instruments one at a time and asks the same five questions of each: how does it work, how do you use it, what will bite you, how is it kept honest, and what does all of that add up to on a real job?

How to use this series

1

Drive the sim first

Each section opens with something to measure. Move the sliders and read the number before you read the prose — the derivation is there to explain a result you already have.

2

Do the "Try this" steps

Every simulation has two to five numbered things to do with it. Several of them ask you to check the sim against the instrument's own manual, and they agree — that is the point.

3

Then go and look at the real one

Every lesson is built from the manual in Data/equipment/, but written for the general model. Check the badge on the instrument you actually use before you copy a number into a notebook.

These lessons are not a substitute for being shown the instrument. They exist so that when someone does show you, you already know what the machine is doing and which of its numbers you are allowed to trust. Every performance figure quoted comes from the manufacturer's manual and is cited; anything that is McGhee Lab house practice is marked as such, in a box, so you can tell the difference.

Lessons 1 to 4 are available now; the rest are being written in batches. If you want the technique of sterile work rather than the instruments themselves, start with Cell Culture Foundations instead — the two series are designed to be read together, and lessons 7 and 8 here are the machine-side companions to its lessons 1 and 2. In the same way, lesson 4 owns the plasma machine while Surface Functionalization lesson 2 owns what the plasma does to a surface.
