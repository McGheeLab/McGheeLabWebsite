## Course Content

BME 295C is a one-week summer course designed to introduce rising high school juniors and seniors to the field of Biomedical Engineering (BME). Through interactive lectures, laboratory tours, and networking with world-class faculty, students will explore diverse BME topics and gain exposure to ongoing research at both the Tucson and Phoenix University of Arizona (UA) campuses.

## Course Format

- **Dates:** June 8th – June 12th, 2026
- **Duration:** 5 days (Monday – Friday)
- **Times:** 8:30 AM – 11:30 AM daily, optional laboratory tours from 11:30 AM – 12:00 PM
- **Locations:**
  - Tucson: University of Arizona Main Campus, Building: Health Sciences Innovation Building (HSIB), room: **880** (Mon Jun 8 & Wed Jun 10) · **444** (Tue Jun 9, Thu Jun 11, Fri Jun 12)
  - Phoenix: Phoenix Bioscience Core, Building: Biomedical Sciences Partnership Building (BSPB), room: E115

Classes will involve lectures and other activities. Activities will mainly consist of interactive sessions involving class discussions on specific topics. The speakers will consist of local university faculty.

## Course Prerequisites

No prior biomedical engineering experience is required.

## Course Objectives

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- Define key concepts and career pathways in BME.
- Describe real-world applications of BME research and technologies.
- Engage with ongoing biomedical research through laboratory visits and discussions with faculty.
- Learn about opportunities and facilities at the Tucson and Phoenix UA campuses.

## Grading

This course is graded based on participation. Students are expected to actively participate in class lectures and complete the required worksheets for each session.

## Course Materials

All course materials will be provided on-site (no textbook required). Some recommended student supplies are listed below.

- Notebook or journal – for taking additional notes during lectures, tours, and discussions
- Pens and pencils – bring several in case of loss or sharing
- Highlighter – for marking important concepts or handouts
- Folder or binder – to organize printed materials and handouts
- Reusable water bottle – to stay hydrated during the day
- Closed-toe shoes – required for laboratory tours
- Comfortable clothing – appropriate for walking tours and indoor/outdoor activities
- Backpack or tote bag – to carry personal items and supplies

## Attendance Policy

Attendance is required for all scheduled sessions. This short, one-week course requires full participation to get the most out of the experience.

- Daily attendance is mandatory. Students are expected to arrive on time and remain for the full day.
- If a student must miss a session due to illness or emergency, they or their parent/guardian must notify the program coordinator as soon as possible. Students must either join virtually or watch the class recording for the day(s) missed.
- More than one unexcused absence may result in dismissal from the program and ineligibility for a certificate of completion.

This policy helps ensure that all participants benefit fully from faculty interactions, hands-on activities, and group experiences.

## Class Recordings

Lectures will be recorded. Access will be given to students following the completion of the course or on a case-by-case basis. Students may not modify content or re-use content for any purpose other than personal educational reasons. When a class is recorded, all recordings are subject to government and university regulations. Students accessing unauthorized recordings or using them in a manner inconsistent with University of Arizona values and educational policies are subject to suspension or civil action.

## Classroom Behavior and Classroom Policies Regarding Effective Learning

Students are highly encouraged to freely discuss the course material and their intellectual views. At the same time, we ask that the students be respectful and considerate of each other, to support an environment in the classroom conducive to effective learning. This includes refraining from conversation that is not geared toward the class topics, engaging in respectful healthy interactions during discussion sections, and arriving and leaving the class in a timely manner. Students should also silence any cell phones and use laptops only for class related activities.
