Solid Mechanics Foundations

Why things stretch, bend, twist & break

Five interactive lessons that build solid mechanics from the atom up — no calculus required. Every concept comes with a live simulation you drive yourself: pull two atoms apart, freeze a puddle of metal, poke honey and diamond, and morph a steel square into an I-beam. Each lesson starts at a high-school level and finishes with the real equations engineers use.

The five lessons

do them in order — each builds on the last
1

Atoms as Springs — Why Materials Stretch

Every solid is a crowd of atoms joined by bonds that behave like tiny springs. Pull a single bond, catch Hooke's law being born, then wire billions of atomic springs together into a real one.

Two atoms, one bond → the bond is a spring → from bonds to a real spring → level up: the Lennard-Jones energy valley

Open lesson → Worksheet (print)
2

Grains — Why Metals Bend Before They Break

Metals aren't one perfect crystal — they're a mosaic of grains. Watch liquid metal freeze in real time, control the grain size with the cooling rate, and discover why flaws focus force and small grains make strong metal.

A lattice of springs → what is a grain (watch it freeze!) → boundaries block slip → how cracks pick their path → level up: Hall–Petch

Open lesson → Worksheet (print)
3

Honey, Cheese, Metal, Diamond

Poke four very different materials and get four very different stories. Some care how far you push, some how fast — and two cartoon parts, a spring and a dashpot, are enough to explain them all.

Poke four materials → speed matters → springs & dashpots wired together → level up: stress relaxation in real units

Open lesson → Worksheet (print)
4

Stress and Strain

Two rods of the same steel give two different force curves — so force and displacement can't describe a material. Two clever divisions — stress and strain — fix it, and a hidden "bulb" of stress explains thumbtacks and snowshoes.

Same material, different curve → divide out the geometry → contact and stress bulbs → level up: Young's modulus & Poisson's ratio

Open lesson → Worksheet (print)
5

Loading Modes — Pull, Squash, Bend, Twist

The capstone: every complicated load breaks into a few basic modes, and geometry decides where the stress goes. Bend a beam, twist a shaft, then reshape 400 mm² of steel into an I-beam and win 20× the stiffness for free.

Tension & compression → bending (σ = My/I) → torsion (τ = Tr/J) → level up: combined loading & the shape game
