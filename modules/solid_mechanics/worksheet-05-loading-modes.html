TAB 1Tension and compression — the fair modes

Goal: verify that axial stress is uniform (every fiber carries the same share), and catch compression cheating.

Predict

A long skinny strut loaded in compression will fail by…  ☐ crushing the material   ☐ bowing out sideways first.

Do

  1. Set force to +15 kN (tension) and diameter to 10 mm. Record σ and the safety margin. Look at the stress-across-the-width plot: what shape is it?
  2. Flip the force to −15 kN (compression). What changed — the size of σ, its sign, or both?
  3. Click Slender strut (buckles first). Now slowly INCREASE the diameter and find the smallest d where the buckling warning disappears.

TAB 2Bending — tension on one face, compression on the other

Goal: map where bending stress lives, and discover that DEPTH is the magic ingredient.

Predict

Doubling a beam's depth h will change the peak stress by…  ☐ ÷2   ☐ ÷4   ☐ ÷8.

Do

  1. Set F = 100 N, depth h = 10 mm, width b = 20 mm. Record M, I, σmax, and the tip deflection δ.
  2. Look at the beam's colors near the wall: which face is red (tension) — top or bottom? Where is the white "dead zone"?
  3. Double the DEPTH to h = 20 mm (keep b = 20). Record σmax and δ.
  4. Reset h = 10 and instead double the WIDTH to b = 40. Record σmax.

TAB 3Torsion — shear in circles

Goal: show that a twisted shaft works only at its skin — so hollow shafts are almost free strength.

Predict

Boring a hole through a shaft's center (removing 36% of its weight) will raise the peak stress by…  ☐ ~15%   ☐ ~50%   ☐ ~100%.

Do

  1. Click Solid shaft: R = 10 mm, T = 100 N·m. Record τmax and the twist angle. Look at the cross-section colors: where is the shear stress zero?

TAB 4The shape game — same steel, 20× stiffer

Goal: morph 400 mm² of steel from a solid square into an I-beam and watch the bending numbers transform — then find the I-beam's weakness.

Do
