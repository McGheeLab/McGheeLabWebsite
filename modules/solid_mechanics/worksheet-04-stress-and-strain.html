TAB 1Same steel, different curve

Goal: prove that a force–displacement curve describes an OBJECT, not a material.

Predict

Two rods of the SAME steel, one thick & short, one thin & long, pulled by the same force, will stretch…  ☐ the same amount   ☐ differently.

Do

  1. Click Rod A (thick, short) (d = 20 mm, L = 1 m). Set the pull to 15 kN. Record the stiffness k and the stretch ΔL from the stats.
  2. Click Rod B (thin, long) (d = 10 mm, L = 2 m) at the same 15 kN. Record both.
  3. From Rod B, double ONLY the diameter (10 → 20 mm). Record k. Then set d back to 10 and halve ONLY the length (2 → 1 m). Record k.

TAB 2Two divisions fix everything

Goal: show that stress σ = F ÷ A and strain ε = ΔL ÷ L₀ collapse every rod onto ONE material line.

Do

  1. At F = 15 kN, set three wildly different rods (table below). For each, record stress σ, strain ε, and the ratio σ ÷ ε from the stats panel.
  2. Watch the two plots as you change geometry: the F–ΔL line moves, but where does your dot land on the σ–ε plot every single time?
  3. Slowly raise F until the verdict warns you about yield. Record that force for d = 10 mm.

TAB 3Contact: the thumbtack principle

Goal: same force, different contact area → wildly different stress, and a "bulb" of stress hiding under the surface.

Predict

Halving the contact width will multiply the pressure by…  ☐ ½   ☐ 2   ☐ 4.

Do

  1. Set the force to 500 N. Record the mean pressure at contact widths 80, 40, 8, and 2 mm.
