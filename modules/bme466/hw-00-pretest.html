Pretest — Where You're Starting From

HW-00 · Baseline check, completion credit only · BME/AME 466·566

What this is (and what it is not)

This pretest takes about 30 minutes and shows us — and you — where you are starting from. The course brings together people from very different backgrounds (BME, mechanical, pre-med, CS, applied math…), so nobody is expected to know all of this. We will ask some of these questions again at the end of the semester to measure what the course actually taught.

  • It is not graded for correctness. Credit is for completing it and uploading your results file to D2L. The module never tells you whether an answer is right.
  • "I'm not sure" is a real answer. Every question has that option, and choosing it is more useful to us than a guess. Please don't look things up — a blank spot on the map is exactly what this instrument is for.
  • You may use the calculator in the top bar for arithmetic.

How to do it

  1. Sign in with your UA NetID (top bar — it's your @arizona.edu Google account). That's the only setup: your personal dataset is assigned from your NetID automatically, and your progress autosaves to the course cloud, so you can stop and continue on any computer.
  2. Work through the two question tabs, in any order. Skipping is fine.
  3. On the Finish tab, export your results file and upload it to the D2L dropbox. That upload is what earns the completion credit.
Privacy. Signing in stores your progress (answers and time-on-page — never a grade) in the course's own database, readable only by you and the instructor. Until you sign in, the page shows demo numbers and nothing you do leaves this browser.

Part 1 — Mechanics & Math

Statics, materials, fluids, and one differential equation — the engineering you've met in your first three years. The numbers in these questions are your own, assigned automatically when you sign in.

Part 2 — Biology & Scales

Cells, molecules, randomness, and orders of magnitude — the other half of biomechanics. Same rules: answer what you know, record "I'm not sure" for the rest.
