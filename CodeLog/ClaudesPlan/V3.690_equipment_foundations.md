## Goal

Alex: *"Make a learning module for each piece of equipment in this folder
`Data/equipment/` … we need to talk about 1) what the equipment does and how it
fundamentally operates 2) how to use the equipment 3) equipment precautions
4) equipment maintenance 5) equipment recap and overview."*

`Data/equipment/` holds seven vendor manuals. Nothing in the repo taught those
instruments. The V3.686 Cell Culture Foundations track teaches *technique* — it
uses the cabinet, incubator and pipette as the setting for sterile practice and
deliberately stops there. This track teaches the *machines*.

## The five-tab mapping, and the one documented deviation

Alex named five sections; the house arc has five tabs, so they map one-for-one:
**1 How It Works · 2 How to Use It · 3 Precautions · 4 Maintenance ·
5 Recap & the Real Job**.

The BME466 spec (`LEARNING-MODULE-SPEC.md` §2) puts the capstone on tab 4 with
`cap-tab` and a next-week teaser on tab 5 with `next-tab`. Here **the capstone
moves to tab 5** and carries `cap-tab`; `next-tab` is unused and the bridge to
the next instrument is a `.handoff` at the end of tab 5. Everything else in the
spec holds — including the governing rule that every tab opens from a
fundamental lens *as a sim* and then moves into synthesis, and that the capstone
is a worked problem with interactives rather than a summary.

### The findings the sims reproduce from the manuals

These are the moments where a student computes something and it lands exactly on
a published number, which is the point of the whole exercise:

- **`m_min = 2s/RE` reproduces the XSR303S's published minimum weights exactly** —
  1 g at 0.10% and 100 mg at 1%, from one repeatability figure. The manual's own
  footnote (*"determined at 5% load, k = 2"*) confirms the coverage factor.
- **The lab's 0.1 g agarose weigh-out sits precisely on the 1% minimum weight** —
  no margin. Under the manual's *limit* repeatability of 0.9 mg it is under it.
- **One degree of temperature change at nominal load (1.5 mg) exceeds the residual
  an internal adjustment leaves behind (1.2 mg)** — which is the whole arithmetic
  behind the single word "Daily" in the maintenance table.
- **ISO 8655 tolerances are constant in microlitres across each pipette's range**,
  so a P1000 at 100 µL is permitted ±8.0 µL (8%) where a P100 is permitted
  ±0.80 µL (0.8%) — a tenfold penalty for reaching for the wrong one.
- **A 1 mg balance can verify a P1000 and cannot verify a P10** — eq. 1.3.4 from
  Lesson 1, and the manual agrees from the other direction by demanding a
  0.001 mg graduation below 10 µL.
- **eq. 3.4.2's z ≈ 90 °C, derived from Corning's two published depyrogenation
  cycles, reproduces both of them at exactly 100%** — and puts a standard
  121 °C/15 min autoclave at **1.84%** of a depyrogenation treatment, quantifying
  Corning's *"little effect on endotoxin levels"*.

## Durable build lessons

1. **Reading the screenshots caught what numeric QA could not, again.** Three
   defects passed every numeric assertion and were visible only in the pixels: a
   display axis printing kilograms as grams (`0.10 g` for a 100 g load), a legend
   drawn straight through a curve, and — worst — a widget that called a standard
   121 °C/15 min autoclave cycle *"not even sterile"* because D₁₂₁ had been set
   to 1.5 min instead of the F₀ convention's 1 min.
2. **A behavioural harness must reset to authored defaults between controls, not
   just between widgets.** Driving each slider to an extreme and leaving it there
   compounds state: a plate left at 14 days and 50% RH is already bone dry, so
   its volume slider correctly does nothing, and the harness reports a bug that
   is not there. Restoring every `defaultValue`/`defaultChecked` on the widget
   before each control fixed it.
3. **Some controls are correctly inert in the opening state, and silencing that
   check is the wrong fix.** Four sliders across L1 and L2 do nothing at their
   authored defaults — load with the balance level, load with the sample centred,
   humidity with a metal vessel, dead volume with everything equilibrated —
   because each lesson's "Try this" list opens on that state deliberately
   ("confirm the error reads zero"). The harness now carries a precondition per
   case and **asserts the control works once the precondition holds**, so the
   check keeps its teeth.
4. **Floating point silently picked the wrong pipette.** `1000^(-1/3)` returns
   `0.10000000000000002`, so a nominal 100 µL transfer computed as
   `100.00000000000003` fell *outside* the 10–100 µL model's range and the sim
   quietly recommended a worse pipette — teaching the opposite of the lesson.
   Range containment tests on computed volumes need an epsilon.
5. **Check which column of a spec table you are reading.** Mettler Toledo
   publishes every performance figure twice, as a **limit value** and a **typical
   value**, and the two differ by ~2×. The first draft had them reversed
   throughout; the tell was that the minimum-weight arithmetic only reproduced
   the published numbers with the *typical* repeatability.
6. **Physical models need calibrating against reality, not just against
   dimensional correctness.** Three shipped-in-draft models were quantitatively
   absurd and had to be retuned: a linear τ(µ) gave glycerol an 89% pipetting
   error and water 8%; an evaporation coefficient 40× too small made the
   edge-effect widget read 0% at its own defaults; and extrapolating moist-heat
   F₀ to 250 °C claimed dry heat was 10¹³× more lethal than an autoclave, when it
   is in fact less lethal per unit time. Each was found by asking "is this number
   one I would believe at the bench?".
7. **A scenario chip that is a *mode* rather than a preset makes its sliders read
   dead.** The gamma-irradiation chip has no temperature or hold time, so moving
   either slider had to exit the mode. `setScen` calls `SCENFN` and then `RESET`
   back to back, so the naive exit fired during the chip's own click and the mode
   could never be entered — a one-shot flag set in `scenfn` and consumed by
   `reset` is the fix.
