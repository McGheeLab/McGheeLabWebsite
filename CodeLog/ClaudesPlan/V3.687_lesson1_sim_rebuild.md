# V3.687 — Cell Culture Lesson 1: honest views, real animation, a cabinet you lay out yourself

## Why

V3.686 shipped the Cell Culture Foundations track. Alex then drove
[01-the-cabinet.html](../../modules/cell_culture/01-the-cabinet.html) as a
student would, and found several sims unreadable or actively wrong — not
numerically, but *as pictures*. Reading a canvas is the one thing an automated
harness cannot do, so every item below was found only by a human using it.

| § | Reported | Cause found in the code |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 `flow` | particles arrive through the glass | entry height was a fixed normalized `p.y` with no relation to the sash geometry; ~half the particles were seeded above `sashY`. **The sash was also inverted** — raising it drew a *smaller* opening |
| 2.2 `hepa` | curves pile up into a mess | **`Chart` never cleared the canvas.** `setupCanvas` clears once, then `getCv` caches — so every redraw painted over the last |
| 3.1 `crowd` | not animated; drawing not understood | one static front view carrying a side-view idea (a tall item interrupting the downflow column), with no view label |
| 3.2 `wake` | not animated; wants two views | static; recirculation was a red rectangle rather than air going round |
| 3.3 `sweep` | "very odd, I don't understand it at all" | **the widget contradicted itself** — a *side* view in which "parallel to the sash" was animated as horizontal motion, which in that view *is* the through-the-curtain direction. The two scenarios were the same animation, separated only by a bare `×0.08` multiplier |
| 4.1 `layout` | wants real placement + a simulated run | it was a four-preset picker with three sliders; the student never placed anything |

Two findings were wider than what was reported:

- **The chart-clear bug hit 9 widgets across all four lessons**, not just §2.2:
  `hepa`, `door`, `therm`, `buff`, `week`, `entry`, `blast`, `decay`, `opt`.
  (`ring` and `coin` happened to clear by hand.) One line, four files.
- **§3.3 was a modelling error, not a drawing error.** Fixing the picture alone
  would have left "parallel motion is safe" asserted by a coefficient.

## What was built

### 1 · The chart-clear fix (all four lessons)

One line in the shared `class Chart` constructor. Verified safe: of the 11 chart
widgets only `ring` and `coin` paint before constructing their `Chart`, and what
they paint *is* this same clear.

### 2 · §2.1 `flow`

Particles now carry `p.s`, a fractional height **within the opening**, mapped at
draw time between `sashY` and the work surface; in the room they are steered
down into that band over the last stretch. The sash mapping was inverted so a
taller setting opens a taller aperture, and the opening is dimensioned on the
room side — it is the area *A* of eq. 1.2.2 and the whole point is that it moves.

### 3 · §3.1 `crowd` — two labelled canvases, animated

`cv-crowd` (front) and `cv-crowd-side` (side) in a `.chart-row`, so they stack
full-width on a phone instead of shrinking to thumbnails. Tracers are **streaks
with heads**, not dots, so a still screenshot shows which way the air is going.
Tracer speed scales with the local velocity, so blocking the grille visibly
*accelerates* what is left (eq. 1.3.1). Over a covered span, tracers walk
straight in and reach the work surface — the section's central claim, previously
only asserted in prose.

### 4 · §3.2 `wake` — side + **top**, animated

The side view shows tracers falling, then being **captured into a closed orbit**
at the base of the object. The second panel is a **top view, not the front view
first sketched**: the model computes a circle-on-circle overlap of two
*footprints* on the work surface, so "% of the dish inside the wake" has nothing
to point at in a front view. The top view also carries the width a front view
would have shown. Flagged to Alex as a deliberate change.

### 5 · §3.3 `sweep` — top view, and a structural fix

Top view (primary) with the sash line drawn across the front, plus a side view.
Particles are now released **by counted crossings** of that line. Parallel motion
carries nothing because it never crosses — the `×0.08` fudge is gone. In the
side view, parallel motion is drawn as an **arm cross-section with the
into-the-page mark**, because drawing it as horizontal motion is what made the
old version incomprehensible.

### 6 · §4.1 — a builder, then a simulated run

Drag-and-drop top-view builder over a 120 × 55 cm surface, reusing the pointer
pattern proven in `modules/tfm/02-hydrogel-network-formation.html`. A 12-item
palette, presets kept as loadable starting points, and a 1-or-2-operator control.
**Run the media change** plays a 16-step task list built from what is actually
placed; a hand and a wider **sleeve** walk it, laying down a persistent debris
layer, and the verdict names the step that forced each bad reach.

### 7 · §4.2 (new) — the full concept check

Three stages, one verdict: drag the sash to a height that is both in
specification and workable; lay the surface out; then **drag the hand** through
the task list while the sim measures the real pointer speed against the 0.50 m/s
curtain. Six criteria, each one a number measured earlier in the lesson. The old
§4.2 (the McGarrity account) is now §4.3, unchanged.

## Model bugs the run exposed, and their fixes

These are the reason §4 took the longest, and all four are the same shape as the
V3.686 overcrowding bug: **a model that contradicted its own lesson.**

1. **The reach-over test treated a 24 × 14 cm flask rack as a 12 cm-radius disc**,
   so paths clearing its back edge by 5 cm scored as reach-overs. Now the sleeve
   band is sampled against the real rectangle.
2. **Transiting the front edge counted as resting on it.** Your forearm always
   spans the front edge while your hand is in the cabinet — flagging that flags
   every reach ever made. The error is routing the *hand* along the front lip
   between two things that are both inside.
3. **The sleeve trailed backwards along the path** instead of towards the
   operator. This inverted the whole exercise. An arm runs from the hand back out
   through the opening, so "never reach over an open vessel" is what it actually
   is: *never put an open vessel between yourself and something you have to
   fetch.*
4. **The shoulder was nailed to one spot**, which punished every traverse across
   the cabinet and made *no* arrangement passable. People turn to face what they
   are reaching for; the shoulder now tracks the hand.
5. **The task list let the hand teleport** between legs. Written as independent
   (from, to) pairs, the automatic run scored a layout clean that the same layout
   failed the moment a student dragged the hand through it. It is now a waypoint
   list, so leg N+1 starts where leg N ended.

The four presets were then re-derived against a Python port of the finished
model rather than guessed: Textbook 100/100 with zero reach-overs, Friday
afternoon 34, two cell lines 48, cabinet-as-a-shelf 23 — each failing for its
intended reason.

The `.rules` text in §3.2 and §4.1 moved with the model: §3.2 now states the
distance rule the sim actually computes (3 + 1.5*h* cm) instead of the "at the
back" proxy, and §4.1 gains **open vessels sit deepest** and **nothing behind an
open vessel**.

## Framework changes (apply to any future module on this base)

- **`Chart` clears its canvas.** See above.
- **`redraw(key)`** resets the stats rate limiter before drawing. `statTick`
  throttles animated widgets to ~6 Hz, so two interactions inside one window left
  the second one's verdict stale. This is the same failure as the V3.686
  `statTick` bug, in the other direction: rate-limiting is right for *frames* and
  wrong for *clicks*.
- **QA hooks go on `dataset`, not `window`.** The browser harness evaluates in an
  isolated world where page globals are invisible. `cv-flow` and `cv-check` now
  publish their geometry and state as JSON on the element, which is what made it
  possible to drive stage 3 the way a student does.

  canvases; the paired canvases stack full-width at 420 px.
