Source: `Data/Grants/ONR Technical Proposal.docx` — the submitted technical volume for
ONR BAA N00014-25-S-B001, *"Towards the Development of a Periodic Table of Quasistatic
Tissue Mechanical Properties"*, now awarded (award 3062920).

## What was actually wrong

The awarded grant existed on the research map as an **orphan**: `level: grant`,
`status: awarded`, real `grant_meta`, and `links` that were empty in every field. It
hung off no concept, had no aims, no bibliography, and no pointer to the document it was
won with. Meanwhile the proposal's own content — 33 references, 5 figures, 9 numbered
equations, 97 inline expressions, 2 tables — existed only inside a `.docx`.

## The decisions that mattered

**Read the XML, not the converter.** `pandoc file.docx -o out.md` loses exactly the
three things this job is about. The 33 references live as base64(zlib(JSON)) Paperpile
payloads inside each citation field's `w:instrText` — split across runs, 89 nodes for 32
fields, so they only reassemble in document order. The Gantt chart's bars are
`w:shd/@w:fill=FFC000` on otherwise-empty cells. And Figure 4 is an anchored
`w:drawing` living *inside* the body paragraph before its caption, not in a paragraph of
its own. A naive walker finds four figures and no bibliography, and looks like it worked.

Maths is the one thing delegated: pandoc's OMML→LaTeX beats anything worth hand-writing.
Its AST's 97 `Math` nodes align 1:1, in document order, with the 97 `m:oMath` elements.
(Counting `'<m:oMath'` textually gives 106 — nine of those are `<m:oMathPara` prefixes.
That off-by-nine is the kind of thing that silently shifts every equation by one.)

**One heading level is promoted, not flattened.** The docx nests H1→H5; an RM
`subheading` is level 2 or 3 only (`MAX_SUBHEADING_LEVEL`). Every H2 under "Technical
Approach" and every Aim (an H3) opens its own section, after which H4→2 and H5→3 fits
exactly. 16 sections, no lost outline.

**Look up the missing DOIs; never invent them.** Six references reached us with no
identifier. CrossRef resolved all six, accepted only on an exact normalized-title match —
including, usefully, the fact that entry 29 (an author-less Paperpile duplicate) resolves
to the *same* DOI as entry 26, so `dedupeAndKey` now collapses them by DOI rather than by
title heuristic. Both markers still point at one library item and the document's own
numbering is untouched.

**Number things once.** The docx labels *both* tables "Table 1". Captions lose their
literal `Fig. N.` / `Table N.` prefix, the prose references become `[!fig:collab]`,
`[!tab:ecm]`, `[!tab:schedule]`, `[!eqr:eq3]`, and `sectionNumbering` is set **off**
because the proposal numbers its own headings ("2.1", "3.1.1", "Subtask 1.1)"). RM
numbers the floats; the document keeps its own section numbers; nothing is numbered twice.

## The census, and why it is a hard failure

`build_onr_bundle.py` refuses to emit a bundle whose counts disagree with the docx:
5 figures, 9 equations, 2 tables, 33 references, 16 sections, and every marker 1..33
used (ranges expanded the way `REFERENCE_IMPORT.rewriteNumericMarkers` expands them).
Every failure mode this pipeline has is a *silent drop* — a figure that never got
placed, an equation consumed by the wrong block, a citation range counted as two
references instead of seven. A count that has to match is the only thing that catches
those before upload rather than after.

## Verified

- `build_bundle.py --strict` → **0 errors, 0 warnings**.
- All **97 maths expressions render under KaTeX 0.16**, the exact build
  `paper-editor.js` loads from jsDelivr — zero failures. That check is what surfaced
  Word's Greek confusables (`\mu_{ο}` with U+03BF omicron; `k_{Β}Τ` with Beta and Tau)
  and pandoc's trailing `\` before a closing `$`, both of which would have compiled to
  garbage or eaten the rest of a paragraph. `pdflatex` is not installed here, and KaTeX
  is the renderer that actually matters.
- **All 6 seed cards compile byte-identically** to the live
  `research-composer.js compileDescription()`, and every prompt in every level's template
  has an answer — so a card opens in the guided editor complete, and re-compiling in the
  browser shifts nothing.
- Bundle: 1.9 MB, 8 files, 185 KB manifest.

## The thing that happened mid-session

The ONR grant card was on the map when this work started (read at 15:05: awarded, award
number 3062920, period 2026-04-01 → 2029-04-02) and **had been deleted from Firestore an
hour later**. Not renamed — gone. `seed_onr_associations.js` therefore carries a restore
path built from the content recorded off the live doc, prints a loud warning when it
fires, and asks before assuming. If the card is present it is only *patched* — links and
`ref_source` — because its `grant_meta` holds the **real award** period (April 2026 →
April 2029), which is not the proposal's stated period of performance (Jan 2026 → Dec
2028), and its description was hand-written.

## The trap worth remembering

The paper importer stamps `meta.library.linked_drafts`. The grant's bibliography reads
`meta.library.cited_in_drafts` — a different field, written only by `CITED_SYNC`'s
debounced diff loop about 16 seconds after somebody opens the draft in the editor. So an
import that succeeds completely still leaves the References section showing **zero rows**
until the draft happens to be opened. V3.718's plan said the field was "stamped when the
paper bundle was imported"; it never was. Step 5 of the seed script stamps it directly,
which is safe because `CITED_SYNC` re-scans before removing anything and the body cites
all 33.
