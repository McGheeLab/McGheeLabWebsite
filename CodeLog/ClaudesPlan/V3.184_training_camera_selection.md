## Goal

Teach a lab member to read a camera datasheet, match camera specs to a
specific imaging modality (epi-fluorescence, brightfield, phase / DIC,
TIRF, live-cell time-lapse, spinning-disk, single-molecule), and pick a
sensible camera for the Nikon Ti-E / Ti2-U at a stated budget.

Concrete trigger: Alex was asked which camera to buy for a Nikon Ti-E
comparable to a Photometrics Iris 15 (~$15k). The answer is rarely "the
Iris 15" — it depends on whether you care about FOV, sensitivity, or
speed. This module makes that decision interactive instead of asking
Alex every time.

Lives in the **Microscopy** category, after the existing 4 microscopy

## Tabs

### Tab 1 — Sensor Architectures
Compare the four sensor families a buyer will actually encounter:

- **sCMOS** (front-illuminated) — workhorse: ~70-82% peak QE, ~1.5 e-
  read noise, fast, $10-20k. Examples: Hamamatsu Flash 4.0, Andor Zyla,
  Photometrics Iris.
- **sCMOS / BSI** (back-illuminated) — premium QE: ~95% QE, similar read
  noise + dynamic range to front-illum, $20-30k. Examples: Photometrics
  Prime BSI Express / Kinetix, Andor Sona, Hamamatsu Fusion BT.
- **qCMOS** — newest: photon-number-resolving, ~85% QE BSI, sub-0.3 e-
  read noise, $25-30k. Examples: Hamamatsu ORCA-Quest 2.
- **EMCCD** — legacy ultra-low-light: ~95% QE BSI but slow + small +
  excess noise factor; mostly displaced by qCMOS. Examples: Andor iXon
  Ultra 888.
- **Industrial CMOS** — color or mono, ~$1-3k; fine for brightfield /
  phase / DIC where SNR isn't the bottleneck.

Interactive: 5 cards on a scenario bar. Click any card → schematic of
that sensor's pixel architecture + ranked strengths/weaknesses. The
read-the-paper "what is BSI" insight is on the BSI card.

### Tab 2 — Camera Specs Deciphered
Six specs that actually matter, what each one trades against, and where
it bites in real imaging:

1. **Quantum efficiency (QE)** — % of photons → electrons. Front-illum
   ~70%, BSI ~95%. Matters most when photons are scarce.
2. **Read noise** — RMS electrons added per pixel per readout. sCMOS
   1.0-1.6 e-, qCMOS <0.3 e-. Matters most for short exposures where
   read noise dominates the noise budget.
3. **Pixel size** — physical pixel pitch (µm). 4.25 µm (Iris/Quest),
   6.5 µm (Flash/Fusion), 11 µm (Prime 95B). Drives Nyquist sampling
   together with system magnification.
4. **Sensor size** — sensor diagonal in mm + total megapixels. Drives
   field of view at the sample (FOV = sensor_diagonal / M_sys).
5. **Dynamic range / bit depth** — full-well / read-noise (e- ratio).
   12-bit, 14-bit, 16-bit. Matters when you have both very dim + very
   bright structures in the same frame.
6. **Frame rate** — fps at full frame, faster at ROIs. Matters for live
   imaging, calcium imaging, particle tracking.

Interactive: 6-row stats table with per-row sliders. Slide any spec →
the "where it bites" verdict updates with a concrete example failure
mode.

### Tab 3 — Modality Matcher
Pick an imaging modality → page weighs each of the 6 specs from Tab 2
on a 0-3 priority scale and shows what to optimize for. Modalities:

- **Brightfield** — dynamic range high; QE / read noise irrelevant.
  Industrial CMOS fine. $1-3k.
- **Phase contrast / DIC** — same as brightfield. Add good gray-scale
  rendering (12-bit fine, 16-bit not needed).
- **Epi-fluorescence (well-stained / live)** — moderate QE, moderate
  read noise, want decent FOV. sCMOS front-illum fits. $10-15k.
- **Epi-fluorescence (dim / fixed / quantitative)** — high QE BSI, low
  read noise, cooled. sCMOS BSI / qCMOS. $20-30k.
- **TIRF** — highest QE, lowest read noise, small FOV is fine. qCMOS or
  EMCCD. $25-30k.
- **Spinning-disk confocal** — high QE BSI + fast frame rate. sCMOS BSI
  with global shutter. $20-30k.
- **Live-cell time-lapse** — low read noise + low photo-toxicity (use
  shorter exposures + higher QE). sCMOS BSI. $20-25k.
- **Calcium / fast tracking** — highest frame rate, moderate QE.
  Hamamatsu Lightning / Kinetix subarray. $25-30k.
- **Single-molecule (STORM / SMLM)** — lowest noise + highest QE +
  cooled. qCMOS or EMCCD. $25-40k.
- **TFM bead tracking** — low read noise (bead localization) + large
  FOV (more cells per frame). sCMOS BSI ideal. $20-25k.

Interactive: scenario buttons swap the priority weights + the recommended
sensor family + a "good camera for this" callout.

### Tab 4 — SNR Calculator
The single equation that ties all the specs together:

```
SNR = (S·QE) / sqrt(S·QE + B·QE + N_read² + D·t)
```

Sliders: signal photons (S), background photons (B), QE, read noise
(N_read), dark current (D), exposure time (t). Output: numerical SNR +
a "what's dominating your noise" verdict (shot-noise / read-noise /
dark-current / background limited) + a simulated noisy image preview.

Pedagogical payoff: students see that read noise matters only at low
signal, dark current only at long exposure, QE always matters
proportionally — i.e. when high QE is worth paying for and when it isn't.

### Tab 5 — Capstone: pick a camera
Set your spec, get a scored shortlist from a real catalog of ~14 cameras
spanning $2k–$40k. Inputs:

- Budget (slider, $2k → $40k)
- Primary modality (dropdown — same list as Tab 3)
- Need large FOV? (toggle)
- Need fast frame rate? (toggle)
- Need lowest-possible noise? (toggle)

Scoring weights each catalog camera against the modality's spec priorities
from Tab 3 + the toggles. Top match named with a one-paragraph trade-off
explanation. Top 5 printed as a purchase shortlist with prices and key
specs — printable like the V3.164 + V3.167 sign-off sheets.
