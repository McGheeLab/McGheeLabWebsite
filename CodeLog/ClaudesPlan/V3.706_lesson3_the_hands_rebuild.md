## 2. Approach

### 2.1 The named fix — a new §2.2 that runs concept → equation → simulation

The track's default arc is sim-first (AUTHORING §9: *"every tab opens from a
fundamental lens — as a simulation, not prose"*). That arc works when the sim is a
**measurement the student can interpret cold**. It fails here, because "how much
film does a withdrawn surface carry" is not readable off a pipette-tip cartoon. So
§2.2 deliberately inverts the default — precedent exists in the track already
(L1 §1.2 and L2 §5.1 both put a displayed equation ahead of the panel).

New §2.2, **"Everything comes out wet"**:

1. the everyday picture — a spoon out of water, out of honey, and out of water
   *fast*; the film is not a property of the liquid alone;
2. the tug-of-war at the meniscus — viscous drag `μU` carries liquid up, surface
   tension `γ` pulls it back; the outcome is a **ratio**;
3. `Ca = μU/γ` as that ratio, displayed;
4. **eq. 3.2.1** (Landau–Levich, unchanged number) with `r` finally given a
   meaning — capillary length `ℓ_c = √(γ/ρg)` for a plate, bore radius for a tube;
5. **eq. 3.2.2** (new) — thickness → volume, `V_film = π d L t`, which is what
   actually costs microlitres and is what §2.3 and the worked example need;
6. **then** the `film` widget: a flat plate rising out of a bath (animated), beside
   a log–log `t` vs `U` chart where the 2/3 slope is visible and measured;
7. a worked example — a 25 mL serological pipette withdrawn at 20 cm/s vs 2 cm/s.

Old §2.2 becomes **§2.3**, unchanged in substance: it is now the *application* of a
concept the student already has, and its trailing prose cross-references §2.2
instead of introducing Landau–Levich for the first time.

**Equation numbering:** `eq. 3.2.1` keeps its identity (the film thickness). The
new number is `eq. 3.2.2`. Nothing is renumbered — a number carries the meaning it
was first written with, per the V3.703 convention.

### 2.2 Show every equation the file references

  eq. 1.5.1 the same way.
- **eq. 1.1.4** (`N = C v_s A t`, deposition onto an open surface) displayed in the
  new §3.3, where the tip-rack sim actually evaluates it.
- §3.4 then refers back to both rather than citing invisible equations.
- Constants-bar equation index gains `1.1.4` and `3.2.2`.

### 2.3 §3.3 gets the sim it was missing — `path`

**"What passed over the rack"**: a 96-tip rack seen from above, with the pipettor
shaft's swept corridor drawn across it, and the same rack seen in section with its
lid. It computes two independent contributions and adds them:

- **crossed tips** — geometry: the shaft sweeps a corridor of its own width along
  the reach; every tip mouth under that corridor has had a non-sterile surface pass
  over it (this is exactly the claim §3.3's prose makes);
- **settled particles** — eq. 1.1.1 into eq. 1.1.4 evaluated per tip mouth over the
  time the rack stands open, with the lid state setting the exposed area.

Scenarios are the three choreographies the prose already describes: *straight down,
lid on its hinge* / *swept across the open rack* / *lid off on the bench, open all
session*. Output is "compromised tips per rack" and "one bad tip every N days",
which is the honest form of the argument — the same form §3.4 uses for reach-overs.

### 2.4 Tab 1 gets §1.2 — `cushion`, and eq. 3.1.2 stops being an assertion

An animated widget: the air column between piston and liquid, warming toward the
hand with a thermal time constant, the meniscus creeping down the tip as it
expands. Sliders: pipettor range (sets the cushion), body temperature, seconds
held. This **earns** eq. 3.1.2 before §2.1 spends it, and it derives the `0.25`
effective-fraction constant that `errState()` has been using unexplained — only the
part of the column your hand is wrapped around reaches your hand's temperature.

## 3. Verified numbers

Landau–Levich, `t = 0.94 r Ca^(2/3)`, `Ca = μU/γ`; medium + 10% serum
(μ = 4 mPa·s, γ = 0.050 N/m); 25 mL serological pipette, bore d = 4.6 mm
(r = 2.3 mm), wetted length 25 cm:

| U | Ca | Ca^(2/3) | t | V = πdLt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 cm/s | 0.01600 | 0.06350 | 137 µm | 496 µL |
| 2 cm/s | 0.00160 | 0.01368 | 29.6 µm | 107 µL |

Ratio 4.64 = 10^(2/3) ✓. Doubling U or μ: ×1.587 (+59%). Halving U: −37%.
Capillary length of medium (γ = 0.055): 2.37 mm.

Caveat carried into the prose and the sources note: the 0.94 prefactor assumes a
clean Newtonian liquid and a thin film. Matrigel on ice at 20 cm/s returns a 383 µm
film in a 750 µm bore — half the bore — which is **outside** the thin-film
assumption, and the sim says so rather than printing the number straight.
