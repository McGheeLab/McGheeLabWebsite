# V3.856 — 📖 Paper cards with a reader, coloured nodes, typed connections, and 📑 Collections / Claims

**Date:** 2026-10-23
**Alex:** *"in the lab notebook, i want a paper reader directly in the notebook, users can expand the paper to make it
so they can read it just like they would read it in the paper reader. highlighting and figure snipping options should
be useable and we should make those options available as a side window that is expandable and collapsable. highlights
and figures each get a colored node to link to something. for example if a user makes a highlight in a paper or snipps
a figure, and wants to use that information in a collection they just add a link from the node to the collection card.
that highlight or figure gets added as a list to the collection."* — *"this works for any canvas labnotebook and
figureboard."* — *"i like the ability to switch the connection type, this allows any figure or highlight to have
multiple connection types depending on context; these types inherit from the set of types from the paper reader. if a
user drags a connection from a node it can support or refute something, or the connection can be information only for
example. then they can make another connection from the same node that does something else of another block.
collections can be a claims node. claims are already a system we use in the paper writer."*

## Goal

A paper from the library becomes a **card on the canvas** (lab notebook and figure board alike). The card lists the
paper's highlights and snipped figures, each with a **coloured node**. Press **📖 Read** and the paper opens full
screen on top of the canvas, read exactly as in the paper reader (same PDF viewer, same text selection → colour
popover, same click-to-snip hotspots and ⬚ Snip marquee), with a **collapsible side window** holding the highlighting
and snipping tools and the live list of highlights and figures. Dragging a node onto a **📑 Collection** card adds that
highlight or figure to the collection's list. A wire from a node carries a **type** from the reader's vocabulary
(supports · refutes · informs · method · question · quote), one node may have several wires of different types to
different cards, and a Collection can be made a **Claim** of a paper draft — then every *supports* / *refutes* wire is
written into the paper writer's claims system as evidence for or against.

## What exists and is reused

- Both hosts already mount the whiteboard engine with `wires: { enabled: true }` and a per-card side port
  (`[data-wb-port]`, V3.841). The engine stores wires at `…/wires/{id}` with `from, to, rel, fromSide, toSide`.
- The reader stack is modular: `PDF_VIEWER.create` (both canvas pages already load it for their paper picker),
  `GEOM_SELECTION` (drag-select text / marquee), `ANNOTATION_OVERLAY` (paints `.ann-rect`s, shows the colour popover,
  calls `onCreate`), `ANNOTATION_ANCHOR`, `ANNOTATION_COLORS` (colour IS the category), `PAPER_FIGURES.uploadImage`
  (Storage `paper-figures/{paperId}/…`). Highlights and snips are `papers/{paperId}/annotations` docs; rules let any lab
  member create (`creator.uid == request.auth.uid`, visibility lab|private).
- Claims: `drafts/{draftPaperId}/claims/{claimId}` (`claims.js`: `CLAIMS.create / addEvidence / removeEvidence`, both
  sides in one transaction; the paper builder shows them as bullet pills via `paper-bullet-claims.js`). Rules let a
  lab member create a claim and update any claim's evidence arrays.
- `FIGURE_CAPTURE` is NOT reused: its dialog `alert()`s on failure (the no-popups rule) and depends on the reader
  page's `ANNOTATION_PANEL`. Its 30-line snip-from-canvas routine is re-implemented in the module.

## Approach

### One shared module: `rm/js/canvas-paper-reader.js` (`window.CANVAS_PAPER_READER`)

`registerKinds({ hostKind, group, getInst, toast, draftId })` registers two kinds on `CANVAS_WIDGETS`, so the notebook
and the figure board get identical cards:

**📖 `paperreader`** — `data: { paperId, title, cite, journal, pdf, year, first, second, nAuthors }`.
- Empty card: an inline library search (`items where type == 'paper'`, library entries only; session-cached), one tap
  picks the paper and names the card after it.
- With a paper: title · citation, **📖 Read**, the counts, then the **node list** — every highlight (colour bar in its
  reader colour + the quote) and every snipped figure (teal bar + thumbnail + name), live from two `onSnapshot`
  listeners (lab-visible + my private), merged like `ANNOTATION_SYNC.start`. Each row ends in a round **node**
  (`span.cpr-port[data-wb-port="e"][data-wb-node=<annId>][data-wb-color=<hex>]`) the engine recognises as a port, and a
  **⤳** button that opens an anchored "Connect to…" menu (the board's collections and claims, ＋ New collection,
  ＋ New claim) for phones and for anyone who prefers tapping to dragging. Clicking a row opens the reader at that page.
- ⋯ menu: Read the paper · Open in the paper reader ↗ · Change the paper… (hands over to ✎ Edit on a phone).
- On a phone the card is inert like every card; the sheet's ✎ Edit shows the same list with ⤳ buttons and 📖 Read.

**The reader** (`openReader(item, api, { focus })`): `div.cpr-reader` fixed over the page (z 2540, under the engine's
popovers at 2600). Top bar: title · citation · page n / N · − + Fit · **⬚ Snip** (toggle: `GEOM_SELECTION.setMarquee`)
· **☰ Highlights & figures** (toggle the side window) · Open in the paper reader ↗ · ✕ Close (Escape too). Main: the
PDF pages (`PDF_VIEWER.create(…, { fitToContainer, disableRange, disableStream, onPageReady → redraw + hotspots })`)
and the **side window** (`aside.cpr-side`, 340 px, collapses to a 28 px tab; under 700 px it overlays the pages): the
tools strip (how to highlight / snip, the colour legend), the snip form (preview · name · note · Save) that appears
when a region is snipped, then **Highlights (n)** and **Figures (n)** — each row with its colour bar, text, page,
comment, the chips of its existing connections (type → target; a chip opens the type menu), ⤳ connect, and × delete
(own annotations, two taps). Wiring is the reader page's recipe: `ANNOTATION_OVERLAY.init({ viewer, container,
getColors, onCreate, onAnnotationClick, onCaptureFigure })`, `GEOM_SELECTION.init({ viewer, viewerEl, onCommit })`;
a marquee commit (Snip mode, Alt-drag, or a hotspot click) goes straight to the snip form instead of the colour popover.
`onCreate` writes the annotation doc with the same shape as `ANNOTATION_SYNC.create` (through `firebridge.db()`, so
the stub harness can drive it) and records the reading `ActivitySignals` event. Everything is torn down on close
(`GEOM_SELECTION.destroy`, `ANNOTATION_OVERLAY.destroy`, `viewer.destroy`, listeners).

**📑 `collection`** — `data: { items: [entry…], claim: null | { draftId, draftTitle, claimId, statement } }`.
- An entry is materialised when a wire lands: `{ id, wireId, fromId, rel, type: highlight|figure|card, paperId,
  annId, quote, name, url, thumb, page, cite, hex, colorId, paperTitle | cardId, kind, glyph }` — so the list reads,
  searches and exports without the source card, and other viewers get it through the item's own sync.
- Rows are grouped by connection type (supports · refutes · informs · method · question · quote): colour bar, thumbnail
  for figures, the quote / name / card title, citation · page, a **type chip** (tap → switch the type = `updateWire`),
  × (unlink: removes the entry and its wire).
- **Make this a claim…** (⋯, or the button on an empty card): pick the draft (on a figure board the board's own paper
  is preselected; in the notebook, my drafts = `papers where authorUids contains me, not library entries`), then either
  **use an existing claim** of that draft or type a statement and **Create the claim** (`CLAIMS.create(draftId,
  { statement })`). The card's title becomes the statement, the head shows ◆ Claim · draft · status, and every entry
  with a stance is pushed into the claim (`CLAIMS.addEvidence(draftId, claimId, paperId, annId, 'for'|'against')`).
- A claim card's ⋯ offers Open the claim's draft ↗ and Detach from the claim (keeps the list).

**Connection types** (`NODE_RELS`): `supports` (stance for, green) · `refutes` (stance against, red) · `informs`
(information only, slate) · `method` (blue) · `question` (violet) · `quote` (orange). A new wire from a node defaults
from the highlight's reader category (`annotation_type`: evidence → supports, limitation → refutes, method → method,
question → question, quote → quote, note / figure → informs); the user switches it from the wire's menu (hosts hand
node wires to `CANVAS_PAPER_READER.wireMenu`), from the chip on the collection row, or from the chip in the reader's
side list. Wires from the same node to different cards are independent (the engine's duplicate check now includes
`fromNode`). Changing a type on a claim moves the evidence between the claim's for / against lists, or removes it when
the new type has no stance; removing the wire removes the evidence.

### Engine (`whiteboard.js`, `whiteboard-board.css`)
- `startPortDrag` reads `data-wb-node` / `data-wb-color` from the port and passes `{ fromNode, color }` to `addWire`;
  the draft line and the finished wire start at the **node's own position** (`nodePoint`: the row's right-middle,
  measured through the card's rect and the lens scale; a collapsed card falls back to its side port) and take the
  node's colour (per-colour arrow markers). `addWire(from, to, rel, extra)` stores `fromNode`, `color`; its duplicate
  check includes the node. New mount options `onWireAdded(id, w)`, `onWireUpdated(id, w)`, `onWireRemoved(id, w)`
  fire for LOCAL changes (the collection entry is written once, by whoever made the connection). Handle:
  `addWire(from, to, rel, extra)`, `redrawWires()`.
- Node ports hide on a phone with the other ports (`.wb-m .cpr-port`), and the body stays inert there.

### Hosts (`notebook-canvas.js`, `figure-board-canvas.js`, their pages)
- `registerKinds()` calls `CANVAS_PAPER_READER.registerKinds({ hostKind, group: 'Reading' | 'Figures', getInst,
  toast, draftId })`; mount options pass `onWireAdded / onWireUpdated / onWireRemoved` to the module; `wireMenu`
  delegates wires that carry `fromNode`.
- Pages load `annotation-colors.js`, `annotation-anchor.js`, `annotation-overlay.js`, `geom-selection.js`,
  `paper-figures.js` (notebook), `claims.js`, `canvas-paper-reader.js` and `canvas-paper-reader.css`. The overlay's
  CSS (`.ann-rect`, `.ann-popover`, `.pv-*`) is in `style.css`, already on both pages.

## Decisions

- **The reader is an overlay, not an in-place giant card.** Reading needs the viewport; the card on the canvas is the
  paper's *handle* (its nodes), the overlay is where you read. The side window lives in the overlay because that is
  where highlights are made; the card's node list is the same data for wiring on the canvas.
- **Entries are materialised**, not derived from wires at render time: the collection must read without fetching
  every annotation, must survive the paper card's removal, and must export as text.
- **Types default from colour** so a green (Evidence) highlight dragged onto a claim is already "supports"; switching
  is one tap. Types are per wire, not per node, which is what lets one highlight support one claim and merely inform
  another card.
- **Claims reuse `drafts/{id}/claims`** exactly as the Comments page and the paper builder do, so a claim made on the
  canvas shows up as a bullet pill in the writer with the same evidence.
- Equations (`kind: 'equation'`) are skipped in the node list; they have their own flow in the reader.
