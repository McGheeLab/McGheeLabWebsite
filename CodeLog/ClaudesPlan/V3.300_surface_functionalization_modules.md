# V3.300 — Surface Functionalization Foundations (4-lesson interactive course)

## Goal

Build a new standalone learning-module series, `modules/surface_functionalization/`,
matching the existing `modules/solid_mechanics/` pattern: each lesson starts at a
high-school level and escalates to a sophomore-undergraduate "level up" final tab,
with interactive Canvas 2D simulations of every concept. The series teaches surface
functionalization from the atoms up, in an order where each lesson feeds the next:
clean surface → plasma makes –OH groups → silanes graft onto those –OH → texture
gives extreme repellency.

## Approach

Each lesson is a single self-contained HTML file. The house CSS and the shared JS
helper library (COL palette, `setupCanvas`, `Chart`, `arrow`/`springGlyph`/`drawAtom`,
`fmt`/`niceTicks`, `setStat`/`setVerdict`, `val`/`num`, `mulberry32`, the control
scaffold: `activeTab`/`scen`/`FMT`/`SCEN`/`DRAW`/`wireAll`/`presetSlider`/`setScen`/
`refreshActiveTab`, tab-button handler, resize handler) are copied verbatim from
`modules/solid_mechanics/01-atoms-as-springs.html`. Animated tabs use the TFM
teardown pattern (`rafId`/`animClock`/`animLoop` guarded on `activeTab`,
`startAnim()`/`stopAnim()` called from the tab handler and on boot). ID convention
`s-/v-/st-/vd-/cv-{tab}`. MathJax 3 CDN only; no SPA header/Firebase (standalone).

## Key decisions

- **Authoring split (per user):** the 4 interactive lessons were authored directly
  (Fable); the overview page and the 4 worksheets were delegated to Opus 4.8
  subagents after the lessons existed, so worksheet steps match the real sims.
- **Physics kept qualitatively correct with honest caveats in prose:** vacuum does
  not strip water instantly (desorption is kinetically limited; bake-out helps);
  broken-bond γ overestimates real values; plasma color is representative line
  emission, not blackbody; plasma grafts a distribution of groups (named group is
  dominant) and hydrophobic recovery fades the effect; silanization needs trace
  water but excess self-polymerizes; Young's equation assumes an ideal smooth
  surface and flat chemistry caps ~120°; Wenzel/Cassie are area-averaged and Cassie
  is often metastable; Sharklet works by topography, not by killing bacteria.
- **Real cited sources with DOIs** at the end of every lesson (Israelachvili;
  BET 1938; Lieberman & Lichtenberg; Paschen 1889; Sagiv 1980; Young 1805;
  Wenzel 1936; Cassie & Baxter 1944; Barthlott & Neinhuis 1997; Chung 2007; etc.).
