# V3.704 — Lesson 2, Tab 5: the drawer model corrected against how the drawer is actually used

## What prompted it

Alex drove tab 5 and reported two things that could not both be true of a
working model:

1. *"the current version of the simulation is showing that plates sitting on an
   empty shelf have the best stability in temperature and CO2. this seems
   wrong."*
2. *"our CO2 cylinder currently lasts many months, so something else is wrong.
   We need to tripple check that all of this is making sense."*

He also struck out a leftover comparison to the earlier carried-box concept
("*That is a much cleaner arrangement than a box you carry…*"), and then, mid-
round, described the sequence the model was supposed to be representing:

> If we take the drawer out when the door is open, the drawer and its contents
> leaves and the inner part of the [chamber] without a drawer equilibrates. we
> put the drawer with the culture back in, and yes it comes with its own air
> etc, but that amount of air is insignificant with respect to the chambers
> environment and thus the air equilibrates quickly. Also when the door is
> opened, the contents inside the drawer should be shielded to changes. The
> incubator as a whole equilibrates much faster since less air is being replaced
> or heated up.
>
> **How many of these factors are actually in your model.**

Answer at the time: **two of four, and one of the missing two was modelled
backwards.**

| factor | in the model? |
|---|---|
| the drawer *leaves* the chamber during your access | **no** — §5.1 did the opposite: `drawerNow → Cbx=Cch`, i.e. the drawer was opened *in place* and filled with the chamber's worst air, then held it for its own τ. That single assumption produced the whole "the better it damps, the worse it gets" verdict Alex quoted back. |
| the air it brings back is insignificant | **no** — there was no drawer↔chamber mass balance anywhere; §5.2's coupling was one-way and non-conserving. |
| a closed drawer shields its contents during somebody else's opening | **yes**, and correctly — τ_gas 6 min, τ_therm 20 min against an 8-second disturbance. |
| the whole box equilibrates faster with less air to replace and re-heat | **half** — thermally it was backwards; on gas, Q_door was held constant as the chamber filled with metal. |

## The five model defects, and what each was doing

### 1 · An 8-second opening was integrated as 15 seconds
`dt = 0.25 min` with `dooring` true for one whole step. Every dip and the whole
CO₂ bill were inflated by ~40%. `stepsOf()` now sub-steps the opening at
`dur/16` and adds a fine settling window (`SETTLE = 0.6 min` at 1 s) after each
door shuts, because the thermal recovery is seconds long and was otherwise
drawn as a straight line where the model has an exponential.

### 2 · The chamber lost heat with no way to get it back
While the door was open the air relaxed toward 22 °C *and nothing else*, so more
metal in the box made the dip **deeper**. That is precisely the "an empty shelf
is the most stable place to be" artefact Alex saw. The incoming air is re-warmed
by every warm surface *while* it arrives, so the two act together: the air heads
for a mixture of 22 and 37 weighted by the two time constants, and gets there
fast.

    τ_mix = τ_x·τ_air/(τ_x+τ_air)      T_mix = (22·τ_air + 37·τ_x)/(τ_x+τ_air)

### 3 · Q_door was held constant as the chamber filled with metal
So a box packed solid with sealed drawers still admitted 66 L of room air
through the same doorway. Wrong in its own limit. Q now scales with the free
volume — right at **both** ends (empty → Q₀, packed → 0) — so τ_x stays 20 s,
the *volume* admitted falls with loading, and the concentration dip no longer
gets worse for the people who never built a drawer. This is the one real
modelling choice in the section and the honesty aside now says so, names the
opposite limit, and says what it would change (~20% deeper dips at four
converted shelves; no sign changes anywhere).

### 4 · The drawers were a one-way sink
Eight drawers hold 37 L of 5% CO₂ — a quarter of the chamber. The coupling is
now conserving in both directions, so what the drawers hold helps the chamber
recover and the cylinder pays less.

### 5 · The CO₂ bill counted gas it never bought
Every rise in chamber concentration was charged to the cylinder, including
rises fed back by the drawers. The controller is now explicit: it injects only
what it commands, and only while the door is shut — which is also what the
manual says the hardware does (the glass-door switch cuts gas and heating).

Plus: `PER_SHELF` was **4**, which is geometrically impossible — two
300 × 220 mm drawers is what fits a 423 × 465 mm insert. Now 2.

## The cylinder

With (1)–(5) fixed and the dwell finally integrated as 8 seconds, the lab's
traffic (4 openings a visit, a visit every 4 h) costs **2.1 L an opening,
50 L a day, 2.8 kg a month**. The old model reported a 9 kg cylinder without
saying so, which is a small one; the default is now the **50 lb (22.7 kg)**
cylinder labs actually put on an incubator, and the stats give both. That reads
**8.1 months**, which is Alex's "many months". A new **dwell slider** (2–60 s)
carries the section's real lesson: thirty seconds instead of eight takes the
same cylinder from eight months to under four, and nothing on the shelves comes
close to that.

## §5.1 rebuilt around the trip, not the door

The sequence Alex described is now the model, and it changes what the section is
about. Someone else's door opening moves a T-25 by **0.12 °C**. Five minutes on
the cabinet work surface moves it by **9.8**. Everything else is a footnote.

Controls: `box-draw` ("drawer opened N min after the door" — the scenario that
does not happen) is replaced by **`box-out`**, minutes out of the incubator, plus
two checkboxes: **37 °C warm plate** and **the lid stays on**. Five scenarios:
our way / work through the lid / no warm plate / no drawer / it never comes out.

What falls out, all measured rather than asserted:

| where the culture spends 5 minutes out | worst CO₂ | worst T | back to 5% |
|---|---|---|---|
| it never comes out | 1.13% | 0.07 °C | 74 min |
| drawer on the warm plate, lid on | 1.14% | 0.04 °C | 67 min |
| plate onto the 37 °C warm plate | 1.91% | 0.04 °C | 70 min |
| plate onto the 22 °C bench | 1.91% | 9.76 °C | 70 min |
| no drawer at all | 1.28% | 9.76 °C | 60 min |

- **The warm plate is the whole temperature answer** and the aluminium is barely
  involved, because the plate is out of the drawer while you work.
- **The lid is the gas answer.**
- **The drawer's one real cost** is that it goes back full of cabinet air: 70 min
  to climb back within 0.2% of 5% against 60 for a bare plate. That is why the
  CO₂ dip reads *worse* with a drawer, and it replaces the deleted
  "damper-injected-downstream" paragraph with something that actually happens.
- **5 mm is over-built for damping**: on the warm plate, 0.5 mm and 10 mm both
  give 0.04 °C. Off it, with the lid on, the same slider runs 7.7 → 1.5 °C. The
  thickness is bought for the walk and for a hundred autoclave cycles.
- **The filter count is set by how you work**: more filters for a drawer that
  comes out weekly (8 → 65 min and 1.63%; 1 → 111 min and 2.75%), fewer for a
  stock that never moves.

## Drawing

- §5.1's illustration was an empty rectangle. It now draws the wall thickness as
  a caliper, the lid on or lifted with its filters, the plate where the controls
  say it is (inside the drawer, or out on the surface), and the surface itself
  in red at 37 °C or grey at 22.
- **Both temperature panels were flat lines.** An 8-second excursion on a
  four-hour axis is narrower than a pixel. §5.2's lower panel now **zooms one
  opening**, x-axis in seconds, and shows the chamber plunging 2.3 °C and
  re-warming while both culture traces stay at 37. §5.1's keeps the full cycle
  (the trip needs it) and, when the culture really is flat, says so on the chart
  instead of looking broken.
- Chart y-ranges are derived rather than hard-coded, so a small excursion is
  visible and a large one is not clipped.
- The purple "in a drawer" trace and its legend are suppressed when there are no
  drawers — it was drawing a flat 5.0% line implying a perfect population that
  did not exist.
- "air back to 37 in 5.9 s" was τ, not the recovery. Relabelled everywhere.
- "…from someone else's door alone" was computed from the not-away window of the
  same run, so it was polluted by the post-trip recovery tail. It now comes from
  a separate `tout = 0` run.
- The warm-plate verdict compared against `dTno`, the no-drawer control — which
  **inherits the warm plate**, so the treatment was being scored against itself.
  It now compares against the same trip with the warm plate switched off.
