## Approach
- **One query language, the lab's.** `rm/js/library.js` `_parseQuery` (field:value, quotes, -word, added:) is the grammar lab members already know; Scientia's search speaks it and adds what a notebook knows about a paper: the notes that cite it (`IX.citations`), its highlights (the literature note's anchored components), the quotes taken from its embedded PDF (`block.quote.src` → the pdf block's `media` = `library.pdf`), the notes on its highlights and the structures from it, its PDF's words (`RD2.texts`, once a session), its shelves and foci, its reading state. The lab's parser lives inside a closure tied to Firestore records, so it is followed rather than imported; the field names and syntax match.
- **The search box is the one source of truth.** Facets, the tree and the map put words in it; chips show each part. A smart focus is that search kept.
- **Shelves you can see.** The shelf map shows every focus as spines on its shelf's board, so the organisation (and where a search's results stand in it) is visible at once.
- **Keep the lab layer's Library working.** `LGROUP`'s API is unchanged; its tree gains colours, order, smart foci and drag there too. The new page is `LibraryView` (the standalone app and builds without the lab layer).
- **Settings.** Four features with switches, settings and previews (the V3.995 rule).
