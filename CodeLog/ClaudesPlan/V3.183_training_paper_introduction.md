## Goal

Teach a student to write the section that reviewers (and casual
readers) judge a paper by. The Introduction's job is to make the
reader want to keep reading by the end of the third paragraph, while
giving them everything they need to understand what the paper claims.

## Format

Lecture-style (same as V3.182). Three interactive widgets:

1. **The funnel diagram** — interactive 3-paragraph funnel visual:
   broad context → specific gap → claim + approach. User can click
   each pane to see good / mediocre examples (using V3.163's
   compare-bar widget).
2. **Gap-statement generator** — form: the contested observation +
   what's known + what's missing + your contribution → renders a
   first-pass gap statement paragraph.
3. **Last-paragraph builder** — the introduction's last paragraph IS
   the paper's elevator pitch. Form-based builder with claim +
   approach + key finding inputs; output is a 3-sentence elevator.

## Sections (9 → finalized in build)

1. The Introduction's job — make them care + invest by paragraph 3.
2. The 3-paragraph funnel: broad → gap → claim.
3. The opening sentence — what to never use.
4. Paragraph 1 — the broad context (with audience calibration).
5. Paragraph 2 — the gap, the contested literature, the missing piece.
6. Paragraph 3 — what you did + what you found, in one sentence.
7. Voice: Storyteller (per V3.182).
8. Common Introduction failure modes.
9. Capstone — write your last paragraph first.
