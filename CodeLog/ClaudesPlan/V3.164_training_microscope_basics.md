## Goal

Ground-floor microscope concepts that students need before they touch a
specific scope. The lab uses microscopyu.com heavily as the authoritative
external resource — this module curates and motivates that material with
in-context interactive demos using lab-relevant sample geometry (tumor
spheroids, TFM gel beads, microfluidic channels).

## Required-training role

This module is the **lab-mandated prerequisite** a student must complete
before their first session on the Nikon AX-R confocal. The capstone tab
(see below) needs to function as a real competency check — the student
should leave the module able to justify NA, sampling rate, modality, and
contrast-mechanism choices well enough that Alex (or the senior member
running their first AX-R session) trusts them not to waste a session or
damage a sample.

**Design implications driven by the gating role:**
- The capstone tab is a graded worksheet, not a recap. It must explicitly
  flag "not ready" when the student picks an unsafe / nonsensical
  combination, and it should print a checklist suitable for Alex to sign.
- Cross-link prominently to V3.166 ("when you've completed this, request
  AX-R training").
- When the (deferred) `userData/{uid}/trainingProgress/{moduleId}` schema
  lands, V3.164 completion is the first one to persist — V3.166 / AX-R
  booking eligibility reads from it.

## Format

Full interactive notebook (MODULE_TEMPLATE.md pattern). 5 tabs.

## Tabs

### Tab 1 — Resolution & Diffraction Limit
**Bio context:** counting nuclei in a tightly packed spheroid edge —
how close can two DAPI-stained nuclei be before they merge into one blob?

- Background: Abbe limit (`d = λ / (2·NA)`), Rayleigh criterion
- Interactive: two point-source Airy patterns whose separation the
  student drags; canvas renders the diffraction pattern; verdict says
  "resolvable" / "unresolved" with the current λ + NA
- Scenarios: 488 nm laser + NA 0.75, 488 nm + NA 1.4, 405 nm + NA 1.4
- Worked example: at NA 1.4, 488 nm light → ~174 nm resolution; two
  nuclei <200 nm apart will blend
- External: [microscopyu.com — Resolution](https://www.microscopyu.com/microscopy-basics/resolution)

### Tab 2 — Numerical Aperture, Magnification, Working Distance
- Background: NA = n·sin(θ); how NA, mag, and WD trade off for
  off-the-shelf objectives
- Interactive: slider for NA → live-update of (a) resolution, (b)
  depth of field, (c) light-gathering area, (d) typical WD; spec
  sheet excerpt for the lab's actual objectives
- Scenarios: 4×/0.13 (slide overview), 20×/0.75 (workhorse air),
  40×/1.3 (oil immersion), 60×/1.4 (oil), 25×/1.1 (water — AX-R)

### Tab 3 — Köhler Illumination
- Background: why a uniform image field requires the lamp filament to
  be defocused at the sample; the field + aperture diaphragms
- Interactive: side-by-side rays (correct Köhler vs. mis-aligned);
  click to step through the alignment procedure
- Worked example: when the field iris edges go fuzzy, the field iris
  isn't conjugate to the sample → re-collimate

### Tab 4 — Contrast Mechanisms
- Background: brightfield, phase contrast, DIC, fluorescence — what
  each is sensitive to and what it costs you (light efficiency, optical
  complexity, sample prep)
- Interactive: same field of unstained cells rendered four ways; the
  student toggles modalities
- Scenarios: live-cell time-lapse (favors phase / DIC, photodamage
  matters), fixed-cell endpoint (favors fluorescence, signal-noise
  matters), label-free thickness mapping (DIC wins)

### Tab 5 — Capstone: choose-your-scope worksheet (gating competency check)
Given a hypothetical experiment (e.g. "image actin dynamics in a
500 μm spheroid"), the student picks: modality, objective, light source,
camera. The page evaluates the choice with a binary verdict — "ready
for AX-R training" or "review tabs N, M before retrying" — and prints
a sign-off checklist that the student carries to Alex / senior lab
member for AX-R session approval. This worksheet is the lab's gate
between V3.164 and V3.166.
