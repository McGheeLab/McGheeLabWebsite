## Goal

Operational fluency on the lab's **Nikon AX-R** confocal. Covers the
*why* of confocal optical sectioning before the *how* of the AX-R's
specific scan modes (resonant 30 fps vs. galvo high-quality).

## Prerequisite gating
V3.164 (microscope basics) is the lab-mandated entry gate. This module
assumes the student already understands resolution, NA, Köhler, and
contrast-mechanism choices — V3.166 builds on those, it does not
re-teach them. The module opener should remind students that AX-R
session approval comes from Alex after V3.164's capstone sign-off, not
from completing this module.

## Format

Full interactive notebook (MODULE_TEMPLATE.md pattern). 6 tabs.

## Tabs

### Tab 1 — Why Confocal: the Pinhole
**Bio context:** imaging into a 200 μm spheroid — widefield blurs every
optical section together; confocal rejects the out-of-plane signal.

- Background: pinhole rejects light from out-of-focus planes; Airy unit
  (AU) as the natural unit; sectioning thickness as a function of
  pinhole, NA, λ
- Interactive: slider for pinhole (0.5 AU – 5 AU); side-by-side widefield
  vs. confocal volumes of a simulated 3-fluorophore spheroid; verdict
  trades SNR vs. axial resolution

### Tab 2 — Scan Modes: Resonant vs. Galvo (AX-R-specific)
- Background: galvo = high-fidelity slow scan, resonant = fast (30+ fps)
  but lower per-pixel dwell time → noisier; line / frame averaging as
  the recovery
- Interactive: simulated single cell scanned in each mode; sliders for
  averaging count and zoom; SNR + per-frame photobleach estimate update
- Decision rule for the AX-R: live-cell dynamics → resonant, archival
  high-NA volumes → galvo

### Tab 3 — Laser Lines + Detector Setup
- Background: AX-R laser turret (typical lines: 405 / 488 / 561 / 640);
  spectral detector configuration; gain (HV) vs. offset vs. laser power
  — which to push first
- Interactive: simulated spectral detector; the student picks lasers
  for a multi-color sample, sets emission ranges, and sees crosstalk
  warnings
- Worked example: DAPI + GFP + mCherry + AF647 on the AX-R — the
  standard 4-line excitation + emission split

### Tab 4 — Z-Stacks & 3D Acquisition
- Background: Nyquist sampling in Z (`dz ≈ λ_em / (2·NA²·n)`); when to
  oversample, when to under-sample
- Interactive: pick objective + λ_em → page computes the Nyquist Z step;
  preview of a stack at Nyquist vs. 2× under vs. 2× over (file size,
  acquisition time, bleach budget displayed)
- Worked example: 60×/1.4 oil + GFP → ~150 nm Z step for Nyquist; a
  20 μm spheroid needs ~135 slices

### Tab 5 — Photobleaching & Phototoxicity
- Background: dose = power × dwell × N_passes; phototoxicity ≠ bleach
  but tracks it; live-cell tells (membrane blebbing, retraction)
- Interactive: dose dial → simulated bleaching curve + viability flag
- Operational tips: scan a sacrificial corner first; favor lower laser
  + higher HV; never assume "the cells look fine right now" means safe

### Tab 6 — Capstone: AX-R session worksheet
Given a hypothetical experiment ("3-color 100 μm spheroid Z-stack on
AX-R"), the student picks: scan mode, objective, lasers, pinhole, Z
step, averaging, total budget. The page critiques the plan, flags
phototoxicity risk, and provides a printable session sheet.
