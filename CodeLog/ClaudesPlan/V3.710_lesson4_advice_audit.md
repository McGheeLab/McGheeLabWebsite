# V3.710 — "we don't autoclave media": an advice audit of Lesson 4

`modules/cell_culture/04-aliquoting-and-stewardship.html`. FTP-only; no Firebase deploy.

## The call

Alex, on §4.2's sterility check:

> Turbidity means stop and autoclave the batch. […]
> this is bad advice, we dont autoclave media.
> Ensure we dont have any other bad advice in this module

He is right, and the error is worse than a wrong verb. **Medium is
filter-sterilised precisely because it does not survive 121 °C** — glutamine,
vitamins and serum proteins are the whole reason nobody autoclaves it. So the
sentence told a student to do something that would destroy the material *and*
would not make it usable if it worked, because the bacteria would be dead and
the endotoxin they left would not be: LPS needs 250 °C dry heat, not a 121 °C
wet cycle. The lesson had no failure branch at all — it told you how to run the
check and nothing about what to do when it fails.

Then I read the whole module for anything of the same shape: **a sentence a
student could act on that would produce a worse outcome than doing nothing.**

## What the audit found

Seven, in three families.

### Actionable errors (would cause harm or waste)

1. **"Turbidity means stop and autoclave the batch."** Replaced with the failure
   branch the section never had: the batch is contaminated, *every tube of it* is, it
   goes through the lab's biohazardous liquid-waste route, and whatever it stood in gets
   decontaminated. Two sentences. The word "autoclave" no longer appears anywhere in the
   module — see **The correction that needed correcting** below, because my first version
   of this fix was three times longer and was the wrong shape.

2. **The sterility check was being incubated in the cell-culture incubator.**
   "…two weeks of shelf space in an incubator you are opening anyway", and the aside's
   "write the date on the incubator log". That directs a student to deliberately grow a
   suspected contaminant in the CO₂ box, next to their cultures — and **Lesson 2 of
   this same track spends a section establishing that a CO₂ incubator is a warm, humid
   box with a fan that distributes whatever grows in it.** The module was contradicting
   its own physics. The check needs no CO₂ (it is a capped broth); it goes in a plain
   30 °C and 37 °C incubator or oven.

3. **"Split further, or store colder, or buy smaller."** Colder is not free advice in a
   section whose *other* half is about freeze–thaw loss, and complete medium and
   serum-containing solutions are damaged by freezing. Now qualified: colder only where
   the certificate of analysis for that stock says it takes it.

4. **"how much goes down the sink"** (×3). Two were about unused sterile leftover, one
   about a contamination event — and contaminated medium is not sink waste. The module
   should not teach a disposal route in passing; the language is now neutral
   ("discarded", "you lose") and the one place that *does* specify a route is the new
   aside, marked as house practice.

5. **Matrigel "gels irreversibly above about 10 °C"** (×2). The handling instruction was
   right; the mechanism was overstated. Corning's figure is 22–35 °C — **10 °C is the
   working margin, not the transition.** Restated so the number is the margin and the
   instruction is unchanged.

### Over-claims that the new §4.1 refutes

6. **"you cannot really get it wrong"** — in the §3.2 insight and in the `opt` verdict.
   V3.705 softened the takeaway and the handoff and **missed these two.** With §4.1 in
   the module they are now flatly false: 120 aliquots of a $70 bottle is $42 of plastic
   for under a dollar of contamination saving. Both now say the minimum is flat *as far
   as this equation can see*, and name what is coming.

7. **"costs you nothing but a few more tubes"** — same family, in the `plan` verdict.

### A stale reference the DOM canary could not reach

The `fit` verdict for "on a tooth, but m ≥ 2" still said "§3.4 will tell you". V3.709's
canary read `document.body.innerText`, so it only ever saw the verdicts for the states
the page happened to be in — **a string that only renders in one widget state is
invisible to a DOM scan.** Found by grepping the source instead.

## Sourcing

The published sterility method is Corning CLS-AN-020, and the bullet list is introduced
as "the published method" — so **the house reasoning had to come out of that list.**
Where to incubate and what to do with a failed batch are not in the bulletin; they are
now in an aside marked *McGhee Lab practice — agree the details with Alex*, which is
the marker the sources footnote already explains. Putting an unpublished caveat inside
a list attributed to a source is its own kind of bad advice.

## Verification

Two passes. First a browser pass that walks all five tabs and force-opens every
`<details>` so worked examples and check-yourself answers are in the DOM too. Then the
one that is worth keeping: **`scripts/tests/module-advice.test.mjs`**, wired into layer 1
of `run-all.sh`, which asserts over the module *source*:

- seven banned strings are **absent**, each carrying *why* it was wrong, so the next
  person to read the file learns the reason and not just the rule;
- five corrected strings are **present**, so a future edit cannot quietly drop the
  disposal route, the incubator caveat, the filter-sterilised reason, the endotoxin
  point or the failure takeaway;
- a stale-`§3.4` check that **exempts `L3 §3.4`** — a cross-lesson reference to another
  lesson's section 3.4 is legitimate and the crude version flagged it;
- the new multi-paragraph aside keeps one computed colour, because `.background-section
  p` and `.aside p` have equal specificity and the later rule wins — a `<p>` added to an
  aside without that rule silently loses the cyan.

14 checks, all passing — and **proven to fail**: reintroducing each defect (including
one deliberately hard-wrapped across a line break, since the sources are hard-wrapped and
a literal match would miss it — hence the whitespace collapse) turns the run red. V3.709's
structural harness and the 40 offline model assertions both still pass unchanged.

## What to take from this

The autoclave line had survived several passes because **every check I had run was about
whether the module was internally consistent and numerically right, and it was both.**
It read fluently, the equations were correct, the sim agreed with them. The defect was
in the one register nothing was testing: *would a student who did this be worse off?*
That question needs its own pass, and it is the only one of my checks that cannot be
automated from the inside — which is why item 2 is the interesting one. The module
contradicted its own Lesson 2 in a way that only shows up if you ask what the reader
will physically go and do.

## The correction that needed correcting

The first version of this fix answered the autoclave line the way I had worked it out:
medium is filter-sterilised *because* it does not survive 121 °C; glutamine, vitamins and
serum proteins do not come through; and sterile or not it would still be unusable because
endotoxin outlives the bacteria. Three sentences, all true, all in the aside.

Alex:

> dont say all this […] its not needed. You are putting ideas students never had into
> their minds

He is right, and the failure is specific enough to be worth naming. **A reader had not
proposed autoclaving the medium — I had.** So the paragraph was not teaching, it was me
arguing with my own mistake in public, and it named the wrong action three more times on
the way past. A student who arrives at that aside with no autoclave in their head leaves
with one.

The general form: *say what to do, not why the wrong thing is wrong.* The reasoning
belongs here, in the plan doc, where it explains the change to us. It does not belong on
the page, where it only introduces the error to someone who did not have it.

Once named, the same pattern turned out to be in three other places I had just written:

- **§3.2's insight** literally quoted "you cannot get this wrong" in order to deny it —
  planting the phrase in the sentence that corrects it. It now just states what is true:
  the bottom is flat for the bottle because the only rising term in *that* equation is
  negligible at that size.
- **The decay verdict** had grown a lecture on why colder is not automatically the
  answer. It is now one qualifying clause on the option itself.
- **The incubator note** spent three sentences justifying one instruction, including a
  callback to Lesson 2. One line of why is enough.

**And it changed what the guard should pin.** `module-advice.test.mjs`'s required list
originally held `<em>filter</em>-sterilised`, `endotoxin they left behind` and
`waste treatment</em>, not recovery` — it was guarding *the essay*, which is exactly the
text that had to come out. A register like this pins the **instruction**, never the
reasoning behind it. `endotoxin they left behind` moved to the **banned** list, carrying
this note as its reason.

Two of the revised entries failed on their first run, both correctly: `store colder,` was
too crude a banned string and fired on the *corrected* text (the defect was the
unqualified triple `or store colder, or buy smaller`, not the words themselves), and a
required string had the wrong case. All thirteen were then re-proven by reintroducing
each defect.
