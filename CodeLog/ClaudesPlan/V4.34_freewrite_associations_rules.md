# V4.34 — Freewrite: your own associations by highlight, and the rule editor in Settings

## Goal
Gabe (18 Nov 2026), the end of his Freewrite message (V4.33 has the rest): "The user may also add freewrite components via highlights, and once the highlight is done, will be asked to associate it to a new or existing component. That association will be asked if it is temporary (i.e., just for that freewrite) or permanent (i.e., for all future freewrites). Additional freewrite association rules can be added, modified, or deleted in Settings."

## Approach
- **Highlight → asked.** In the freewrite's page, selecting words with the pointer (two letters or more) puts a small button at the end of the selection: "Make these words a component…". It is the asking Gabe describes, kept small (nothing opens by itself; typing or a click elsewhere takes it away; a keyboard selection does not raise it).
- **The question** (a dialog over the page): *These words become* — a new component (what it is: a component, a task, a Result to add, a bridge from a note, any component kind, any structure) or an existing component (a searchable list: this note's components first, then every other note's); then *For* — just this freewrite (temporary) or every freewrite (permanent: a rule).
- **Temporary:** `fw.assoc = [{ text, key, as, kind, struct, block }]` on the freewrite. **Permanent:** a rule of yours in the notebook's settings (`settings().fwRules.own`, synced like every setting, so it follows the person across devices): its pattern is the words, `phrase` (just those words), `A` what they become, its example the sentence they were in.
- **Reading them** (`FREEW.convert`): after the sentences are read, every occurrence of a temporary association or a phrase rule (word-bounded, any case) becomes its part — its own component where nothing else claimed those words; where a rule found exactly those words, it changes what they become; inside a whole sentence a word rule (or "every sentence a component") coloured, the words are taken out of it as their own part; inside a part a two- or one-part rule found, they stay part of it.
- **An existing component:** the part stands for it (`p.block`): coloured green with "In the note: …" or "Bridged: …". On Apply no new component: links go to that component when it is in this note, else to a bridge of it made in the note (a `ref` block, as `M.pullInto` makes), in the same undo group.
- **The rule editor (Settings → Freewrite → Rules):** every rule, built in and yours, with its switch, its patterns, what it makes ("[A] a component → supports → [B] a component") and its example; Edit opens a form in place: patterns (one per line; `[A]`, `[B]` the parts; none: a word anywhere, making the whole sentence or just those words), what [A] and [B] become, the link (relation and direction), the example, with the example read live by that rule below it; Save, Cancel, Delete. + Add a rule. Restore the built-in rules (when one was changed or deleted).
- **Storage:** `settings().fwRules = { own: [rule], edit: { builtinId: changed fields }, off: [deleted builtin ids] }` — built-in rules stay in the code, so a later version can improve them while your changes to them are kept. `FREEW.rules()` merges them.
- **The live preview** shows a rule at work on its example: the rule being edited (or added) as you type, else the default sentence.

## Decisions where the brief was silent
- The asking is a small button at the selection, not a dialog that opens by itself (Gabe: nothing opens uninvited).
- A permanent association to an existing component in another note is bridged into the note on Apply.
- A built-in rule deleted can come back (Restore the built-in rules); yours deleted are gone.
- Rules apply in their order: yours after the built-in ones of the same shape; two-part rules always before one-part ones.
