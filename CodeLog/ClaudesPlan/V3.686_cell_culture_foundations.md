## The governing principle, and why every sim exists

The spec's rule is *every tab opens from a fundamental lens AS A SIM, then moves
into synthesis* — the derivation must confirm a number the reader already
watched appear. That is not decoration here; it is what makes the rules
derivable instead of arbitrary. Two examples of the shape:

- **L1 §1.1** asks the reader to find the particle diameter that settles at
  1 ft/min. It is 13 µm — dead centre of the 4–28 µm band Corning quotes. The
  vendor's rule of thumb is Stokes' law evaluated at the middle of the size range
  that carries microbes, and the reader derives that rather than being told it.
- **L2 §2.2** shows the pH holding flat and then falling off a cliff, *then*
  explains that buffer capacity is proportional to the bicarbonate that is left —
  which is the answer to "but it looked fine yesterday".

## Findings that changed the content

Building the sims produced results that contradicted what I had planned to write,
and the content follows the numbers:

- **The weekend flask goes acid before it is confluent, and while there is still
  ~14 mM glucose left.** Cells poison themselves before they starve. That makes
  **volume**, not seeding density, the lever for a long hold — growth is limited
  by surface area, acid is diluted by volume, and those are independent.
- **A reach-over is 10–100× baseline, not infinite.** Room-settling into an open
  dish over a few seconds is *hundredths* of a particle (eq. 1.1.4), so the honest
  model is a hand as a local source at 10 cm, not the room at a metre. The rule
  survives on repetition, not on any single event — and the lesson says so,
  because overclaiming is what makes students stop believing rules.
- **Aliquoting's improvement floors on the master transfer**, which reaches every
  tube identically. Aliquoting does not remove risk so much as concentrate it
  into one moment you can prepare for — which is the argument for the sterility
  check, and it only became visible once eq. 4.2.1 was plotted.
- **The small-aliquot limit is the Landau–Levich film from Lesson 3.** A fixed
  few µL per tube is nothing out of 25 mL and a sixth of a 20 µL aliquot, so
  "split it further" is free for a bottle of medium and wrong for an antibody.

## Three corrections the QA harness forced

The harness asserts on *behaviour* (does a readout change when an input does),
never on console silence. All three of these passed a console check:

1. **`statTick` was silently freezing static widgets.** Rate-limiting readouts to
   ~6 Hz is safe for an animated widget — the next frame repaints — but a static
   widget draws once per input, so an input landing inside the window left the
   readouts stale permanently. `statTick` now bypasses for widgets with no
   `registerAnim`.
2. **A clear grille scored "transitional".** The first `crowd` model put the slot
   Reynolds number at 1757 for a *correctly certified* cabinet — the model
   contradicted the thing it existed to teach. Two fixes: Re now uses the
   hydraulic diameter of one grille slot (≈ 840, laminar, as it must be), and the
   verdict keys on the real dominant failure — **a covered span of the face has no
   inward velocity at all**, so room air crosses there unopposed. The prose claim
   moved with it, from "a quarter of the grille" to "about a third".
3. **Two dead controls.** L3's "right pipettor" checkbox had identical branches;
   the range slider *is* that decision, so the checkbox is gone. L2's
   "stacked" toggle redrew the canvas without touching any readout.

## Deliberate omissions

- **No presenter mode / `#rail`.** Alex declined it. The
  `defWidget`/`registerAnim`/geometric `syncVis()` animation gating from
  AUTHORING.md §9 is kept — it is what stops five sims per tab all running — and
  is independent of the rail. Dropping `#rail` also means `class-builder.js`
  keeps scrollHeight-sizing the iframe, which is right for a page with no
  `position:fixed`.
- **No printable worksheets.** Alex chose the case files instead.
- **The V3.684 module whiteboard panel is not adopted.** It mounts into
  `.rail-head`, which this track has no rail to provide, and it is opt-in. A
  classroom-projector track would want it; a self-paced lab track does not.
- **No training-completion persistence.** `userData/{uid}/trainingProgress/` still
  does not exist (sketched in V3.164, never built), and this release does not
  build it. The gate is the printed sheet.

## Making the gate real — zero code, Alex's call

The only *enforced* gate in RM is `canUserBook()` in `rm/js/equipment.js:453`,
which checks `equipmentTraining/{uid}.certifications` (rules: admin-write only)
against `equipment.training.certifications[]`, with cert definitions in
`equipmentSettings/config`. **All three are admin-UI data.** So after FTP, if
Alex wants the sign-off to actually gate something: Equipment → Settings → define
a `cell-culture` certification; edit the BSC/incubator to require it; tick it per
student on sign-off. No code, no rules deploy.

## Source integrity

Every lesson carries a `.sources` block. Vendor literature anchors the narrative;
the numbers that drive sims are cited to **NSF/ANSI 49** and the **CDC/NIH BMBL**
(cabinet velocities), **Hinds** (settling, filtration), **Landau–Levich** (films),
**Jurin** (capillary rise) and standard physiology for Henderson–Hasselbalch.

Two honesty items worth keeping:

- **The corpus contradicts itself on UV.** `CLS-AN-724` says the hood's UV lamp
  keeps the environment aseptic; `CLS-AN-020` states NIH/CDC/NSF/ABSA do **not**
  recommend UV in Class II cabinets, and NSF 49 provides no performance criteria
  for it. BMBL and NSF 49 side with CLS-AN-020; L1's sources say so.
- **Three topics have no source in the corpus** and are authored as lab SOP,
  labelled `McGhee Lab practice` in a visually distinct callout: opening tip
  boxes / pipette wrappers / caps (L3 §3.3), the BSC spatial workflow, and the
  aliquoting procedure. **Alex should confirm the specifics against the lab's
  actual cabinet before this is used for a real sign-off** — the reasoning is
  physics and transfers anywhere, but clean-to-dirty direction is a handedness
  decision and the choreography depends on where the grilles are.

## Verification

- **Structural** (`scripts/`-equivalent checker run per lesson): 5 tabs, every
  widget has its `cv-`/`st-`/`vd-` triple, every slider has a readout and an FMT
  entry, every `.equation-box` is numbered and indexed, no duplicate equation
  numbers, balanced tags, and no `prompt`/`alert`/`confirm` anywhere.
- **Runtime** (headless, `browser-automation`): every tab, every scenario chip,
  every slider and every checkbox driven on all four lessons and the RM page.
  Zero console errors and zero failed requests throughout; every canvas paints;
  MathJax typesets all 35 equation boxes and **no raw TeX reaches the ~6 Hz
  stats/verdict DOM**; every scenario chip produces a distinct verdict.
- **RM page specifically**: the lab-member gate is confirmed working (tab bar in
  the DOM but zero-sized, sign-in panel shown). `firebridge` is a *lexical* const,
  not `window.firebridge`, so it cannot be stubbed — the harness instead serves
  the page with the auth `<script>` intercepted out, which takes the renderer's
  own `else { init(); }` branch. The renderer under test is byte-for-byte
  production. All 10 case files and 6 branch points score, all-best answers
  produce a PASS with the sheet shown and the date pre-filled, and breaking one
  critical item withdraws the pass and labels it CRITICAL.
- **Screenshots read**, not just captured — they caught a wake-circle overlay that
  swamped the layout diagram, a verdict that claimed "no wakes" while the stats
  said 28%, ellipsized stat labels, a cabinet drawing with an invisible sash, and
  a stray leading full stop in the sign-off verdict.
