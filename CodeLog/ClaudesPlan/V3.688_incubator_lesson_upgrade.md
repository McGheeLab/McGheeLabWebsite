# V3.688 — Cell Culture Lesson 2: our incubator, the competing lesson, and a tab on keeping it clean

## Why

Alex read the V3.686 incubator lesson against the box the lab actually owns and
found four things it either got wrong or left out. All four are the same kind of
gap: the lesson was right about the physics and silent about the *practice*.

| Reported | What it meant |
|---|---|
| "our incubator is a Heracell VIOS 160i and we want that to be a pill on the preselects" | the lesson taught three generic designs and none of them was ours |
| "we have a glass door inside not just the outside door — open the main door, find your stuff, open the glass door, grab as quick as safely possible" | **the lesson's whole door model was wrong**: one door, one time constant, so hunting and grabbing cost the same |
| "as we reach in over other cell cultures to get to the stuff in the back we may introduce contamination — the competing lesson is grab your stuff for temperature but we also want to reduce contamination" | §1.2 taught "mission-critical goes at the back" with no mention that the back is only reachable *through* the front |
| "a student could take away that they should just put more media in, but this has the cost of reduced gas exchange" | tab 2 made a trap: every argument in it points at more medium, and nothing said what that costs |
| "maybe we should have a full tab for contamination avoidance — change the water pan, steri run, don't let the door stay open, open the door slow" | contamination was a subsection of the water-pan tab, not a topic |

## What was built

### 1 · §1.1 — the VIOS 160i, and the second door

New scenario chip **our Heracell VIOS 160i**, now the default, alongside the
three generic designs. More importantly, the widget models **two doors**: an
inner glass door does not stop heat leaving, it stops the *air* leaving, and
bulk exchange carries almost all of the loss. The two phases run eq. 1.5.1 in
series, so the exponentials multiply and the exponents add:

> **eq. 2.1.2** — ΔT = ΔT_max (1 − e^−(t₁/τ_outer + t₂/τ_glass))

with τ_outer ≈ 9 τ_glass. A second of hunting therefore costs about a ninth of a
second of grabbing, which turns Alex's instruction into a number and inverts the
usual advice: **be unhurried with the outer door and quick with the glass.**
Rushing the wrong one is how people drop flasks.

The chart draws the two phases as separate shaded bands and ghosts the same
opening with the glass left open. Measured in the sim: 40 s / 6 s → −6.0 °C;
the same 40 s with the glass open throughout → −13.2 °C; two full minutes of
hunting with the glass shut → −9.4 °C.

New worked example derives the factor of nine. The lab-practice note is now the
four-step routine, and states plainly that the numbers for our unit are
representative of a 165 L direct-heat box rather than measured on ours — a
datalogger morning would fix that and would make a good first project.

### 2 · §1.2 — two maps that want opposite things

The same six positions now carry a second map. Fill is temperature; **border is
how often a sleeve crosses that position**. Every trip to a back position takes
an arm over whatever is in front of it, so the thermally-best position is the
one that charges its cost to somebody else. New control: *reaches to the back*
per day, and a checkbox for **each project has its own pull-out rack**.

This is the only intervention on the page that takes a number to *zero* rather
than making it smaller, and the verdict says so: being careful, going slower and
agreeing to be tidy all fail. The rules block was rewritten — "mission-critical
goes at the back" is now true *once the reach is gone*, and is preceded by
**nobody reaches over anybody**.

### 3 · §2.3 (new) — the fix that is not a fix

The section tab 2 needed. Medium is not only a chemical reservoir; it is the
thing oxygen has to get through, and nothing stirs a flask.

> **eq. 2.2.5** — C_cells = C_surface − qh/D ⟹ h_crit = D·C_surface/q
> **eq. 2.2.6** — t_eq ≈ h²/2D

Numbers that fall out, and they are the whole section: 5 mL in a T-25 is 2.0 mm
and leaves the monolayer at **76%** of surface oxygen; 10 mL "so it lasts the
weekend" doubles the pH headroom and drops oxygen to **52%** while quadrupling
the CO₂ lag; 25 mL puts a **hypoxic layer at the bottom of the flask** while the
phenol red stays reassuringly pink. And h_crit scales inversely with density, so
a sparse flask can afford depth and a confluent one cannot — which matters
because density is the thing that goes up on its own while you are away.

The worked example lands the anchor: **every recommended volume in every
catalogue is ≈ 2 mm of depth** — T-25 5 mL, T-75 15 mL, 6-well 2 mL — and none
of them say why. It is a diffusion length with a safety factor, and doubling a
volume "to be safe" spends that factor on the wrong risk.

### 4 · Tab 4 (new) — Keeping It Clean

**§4.1 · The door is an inlet.** An incubator door has no filter. Two mechanisms,
two habits, and people reliably swap the instructions:

- the **swing** is a piston — eq. 1.3.2 from Lesson 1, unchanged, with the
  incubator's exchange velocity in the denominator instead of the cabinet's
  curtain. Same ratio, same Π = 1 threshold, different door.
- the **dwell** is buoyancy — eq. 1.5.1 again, and it *saturates*, so a propped
  door is a full charge of room air rather than a proportional one.

The two compose multiplicatively on what is left, the same way the two doors did
in eq. 2.1.2. Opened smoothly and shut in 8 s replaces 33% of the chamber;
propped for 75 s replaces 98%, and flinging it as well adds essentially nothing
on top — because the dwell had already replaced everything. That *is* the lesson.

**§4.2 · Three rungs, and only one of them prevents anything.** A year simulated
a day at a time. Bioburden regrows on §3.2's 22-day constant, so a knock-down
faster than that holds the year flat and a knock-down slower than that is a
notch in a curve that spends most of its time high. Water change + wipe (weekly,
no downtime) · full clean (monthly, 2 h out of service) · **Steri-Run** (the
unit's own high-temperature cycle, the best part of a day, everything comes out).

Policies grade: nobody's job → 95% average; **Steri-Run only** → 83% average (24 h of downtime for it)
despite two sterilizations, because the tray comes back regardless; our schedule
→ 15%; belt and braces → 10%. The point the sim exists to make: **Steri-Run is
a reset, not a schedule.** It reaches the sensor, plenum and ducting that nothing
else does, and it is useless as your only control.

The good verdict closes the loop to §3.2: this percentage is a *fraction of
saturation*, and what is in the tray sets what saturation means — 15% of sterile
distilled water is housekeeping, 15% of tap water is an outbreak.

## Decisions worth recording

- **Six tabs, not five.** `LEARNING-MODULE-SPEC.md` §2 gives five, but its hard
  rules are about *roles and order* — "the capstone is the last content tab; the
  teaser is the last tab" — and both are preserved. Tab 4 is a second synthesis
  tab. The tab bar, the section `data-nav` numbers and the section comments all
  renumbered (capstone 4.1 → 5.1, teaser 5.1 → 6.1).
- **Equation numbers did not renumber, and that is deliberate.** `eq. 2.5.1` is
  referenced from Lesson 3 in five places and the spec says reuse keeps a number
  *forever*. So equation numbers carry the tab an equation was **first written
  in**: the capstone keeps 2.4.x and the teaser keeps 2.5.1. Tab 4 adds **no new
  equations** — it is built entirely from eq. 1.3.2 and eq. 1.5.1 — so there is
  no collision to resolve. A note in the equation index says all of this.
- **A reused equation is boxed once per file.** The release checker forbids two
  `equation-box`es with the same number, and it is right to. Tab 4's two reuses
  are presented as `.step-eq` with the number named in the line.

## Model corrections found by driving it

- **The inlet model let a 75 s opening put 202 L of room air into a 165 L box.**
  The swing and the dwell were added; they compose multiplicatively on what is
  left, exactly like the two doors in eq. 2.1.2.
- **A water change was being charged as downtime.** The rung table says the box
  stays in service; the model did not. Split into "time it costs you" and
  "of which out of service" — which is also the better teaching point, because
  the cheap rung costs labour and not availability.
- **§2.3's `dbl` scenario — the exact trap the section is about — reached the
  reassuring verdict.** Threshold too high (4.5 mm) and the sparse branch tested
  after the depth branch, so a sparse-but-deep flask got warned about nothing.
- **§1.1's x-axis printed "1 1 2 2 3 3"** — a pre-existing bug, exposed because
  the two-door model makes the time axis a few minutes instead of ten.
