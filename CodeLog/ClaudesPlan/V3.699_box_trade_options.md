# V3.699 — five ways out of §3.3's stability-vs-recovery trade

## What Alex asked

> Ok so we have a trade off here with stability and recovery. Suggest some options for
> how we get around this. Better box design, heat plate in the BSC to keep the box
> warm? or should we do a plastic box and accept worse damping of the temperature and
> CO2 per incubator opening

## The framing that makes them comparable

The trade only exists because **the box gets cold**. Every candidate is therefore an
attack on the same single quantity, which means they go on one table rather than into
an argument. All are now scenarios in the sim, modelled first in `options.py`.

| change | plate at end of work | water on the plate after | CO₂ back |
|---|---|---|---|
| nothing — the box today | 30.4 °C | 0.54 g | 17 min |
| no box at all | 25.7 °C | — | — |
| **be quick — 4 min** | **33.8 °C** | 0.28 g | 17 min |
| **warming plate, 45 W** | 31.5 °C | **0.13 g** | 17 min |
| **double-walled** | **34.1 °C** | 0.34 g | 17 min |
| 2 filters instead of 4 | 30.4 °C | 0.21 g | 34 min |
| polycarbonate | 28.8 °C | 0.42 g | 17 min |
| anodised black | 29.2 °C | 0.44 g | 17 min |
| **double-walled + matched 20 W** | **34.8 °C** | **0.02 g** | 17 min |

## Recommendations

1. **A warming plate is the cleanest fix for the condensation, and it has a size.**
   The box loses `hAΔT` = 14 × 0.215 × 15 ≈ **45 W** in the downflow, so 45 W under it
   is break-even. 0.54 g → 0.13 g; at 60 W, essentially nothing. It is a powered object
   in a cabinet: sealed and wipeable, low surface temperature (there is ethanol about),
   cord clear of the sash.
2. **A double wall is the best fix for the culture** — it changes *h*, not the mass, so
   the box barely cools. It only halves the water, because insulation also slows the
   re-warming; pair it with a matched plate and the problem disappears.
3. **Be quick** — free, and still the biggest single gain for the culture.
4. **Do not go plastic.** It gives up 1.6 °C of the only thing the box is for and
   removes barely a fifth of the water. (The Cell Locker is polycarbonate but is not
   doing this job — it is a segmented compartment, not a carrier.)
5. **Do not bother anodising.** The extra radiation that helps it re-warm helps it cool
   in the cabinet by the same mechanism.
6. **Fewer filters** only after the temperature is fixed — it doubles CO₂ recovery to
   34 min, and the medium has just spent the media change outgassing.

## The finding worth keeping

With the lid off, the plate's own exposed top loses about **1.5 W** to the downflow
while the box can feed it only about **0.1 W** through its base and sides — fifteen
times less. **While you are actually working, the box is barely holding the culture up
at all**; most of the five degrees it buys are bought while the lid is *shut*, in
transit and on the bench. So the two things that matter most for the culture are how
long the lid is off and whether the plate's own lid is on between steps, and no
hardware substitutes for either.

## Three test-integrity bugs, all one family

Each was found by sweeping the new controls against the standalone model, not by the
page failing. All three are cases of **a check sharing a mechanism with the thing it
checks**:

- **The control group inherited the treatment.** The bare-plate comparison used the
  box's own heat-transfer coefficient, so insulating the box silently insulated the
  control — `insul` reported +2.4 °C when the real figure is **+8.5**.
- **A soaked-filter box was congratulated.** The "both ends fixed" verdict fired on
  zero condensation without checking the CO₂ ever returned, so the failure case read as
  the ideal.
- **The overheat guard watched the wrong body.** With the lid off the plate's open top
  holds the culture down while the metal runs away, so an over-powered plate reads
  45–50 °C in the *box* while the culture still says 36 — and the culture is shut inside
  that box a minute later. It now watches both, and the chart draws the ceiling.
