# V3.692 — §2.1's cabinet becomes the engine, and all of tab 3 runs on it

## What Alex asked for

> "all the simulations on tab 3 should use the simulation from 2.1 since it is
> the best and gives the most information for how the entire system is working."

## What that changes

Tab 3 had four widgets and **seven** canvases, each an independent cartoon with
its own private geometry: a front view of a grille, an abstract side view of a
bottle, a top view of a hand path, a 44 cm strip of bench. Each was correct
about its own effect and told you nothing about the machine it was happening
inside. §2.1's section is the only picture in the lesson that shows the whole
circuit, so it is now an **engine** and every section on tab 3 draws on it.

Tab 3 is now four widgets and **four** canvases, each laid out exactly like
§2.1: the cabinet in section on the left, and on the right the one thing a side
section cannot show.

| § | What it puts in the cabinet | Right-hand panel |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | grille coverage (front **and** rear) + a tall item | the tray from above: which span is covered |
| 3.2 | one object, and the region around its base | the two footprints, and whether they touch |
| 3.3 | a forearm through the opening, and the slug it drags | does this path cross the sash line? |
| 3.4 | a shedding cuff, and where the plume lands | the two routes, side by side |

## The engine

Everything above `/* widget: flow */` is now shared: `CAB`, `vIn`, `qExhaust`,
`sashOK`, `pCross`, `splitRaw`/`splitFrac`, **`cabAir()`**, `secField`, `legOf`,
`stepField`, `plLen`/`plAt`, `secGeom`, `sweepPath`, `underTray`, `deflect`,
`secPath`, `warpU`, `secBox`, `secGauge`, `secTracers`, `secSplit`,
`secSplitLabel`, `secLabels`, `secWake`, `secObstacle`, `planGeom`, `streak`.

Two new pieces make it reusable:

- **`cabAir(o)`** — one airflow state for every section. Takes
  `{sash, down, blockF, blockR, fixedQ, off, vrms, sc}` and returns the state
  §2.1 already used. Every tab-3 widget now gets its airflow from the same
  function, so they cannot drift apart.
- **`env`** — what a section puts *in* the cabinet: `obstacles[]`, `blockF`,
  `blockR`, `spill`. `secPath` consults it, so a streamline that would run into
  something goes around it (`deflect`), and inflow the front grille cannot catch
  continues over the tray instead of vanishing.

## The payoff: §3.1 stops asserting and starts deriving

Covering a grille is no longer a separate effect bolted onto a cartoon. It
shrinks that grille's open area in **the same equation §2.1 uses to place the
split**:

```
(qEx + f·qDown)/A_front = ((1-f)·qDown)/A_rear      A_front = 1.5·(1-blockF)
```

So covering the front grille runs the split forward until it jams (`starved`),
and covering the **rear** grille — a new slider, and the failure nobody looks
for — runs it backward, which means the downflow that used to drop straight out
at the back now crosses the entire work surface to reach the front. §2.1's prose
already claimed that. Now you can watch it, and the front of the cabinet looks
perfect the whole time.

## Everything else is preserved

Every model, stat and verdict on tab 3 is the one that was there —
`crowdState`'s eq. 1.3.1 velocity and slot Reynolds number, `wakeState`'s
circle–circle overlap, `sweepState`'s Π and counted crossings, `shedState`'s
Gaussian `shedFrac`. No equation numbers change; no new equations are added.
§3.1 gains one slider (`crowd-rear`) and one stat row; §3.2 and §3.4 gain a stat
row each.

## Bugs found on the way

1. **`streak()` was collateral in the first splice** — it had been defined
   inside the old crowd block. Every tab-3 sim drew its furniture and then
   silently stopped. **The `console.error` from the rAF loop's catch never
   reached the harness**, and no page error was raised: a widget can fail
   completely and leave a page that reports itself clean. The fix that found it
   was a `dataset` probe, which the isolated world *can* read.
2. **The wake rectangle was drawn unclipped** and covered the room, the ledger
   and the panel — a 34 cm object's disturbed zone really is ~54 cm, which is
   the lesson, but it has to be clipped to the chamber.
3. **Recirculating air was the same amber as room air.** They are different
   things; the recirculation is now dark amber.
4. **Landed plume dots were drawn under the dish**, so the ones that went *in* —
   the only ones that matter — were invisible. Split into two passes.
5. **10 cm of fall is only ~60 px at true scale**, so §3.4's plume was
   technically present (48 particles, 441 purple pixels) and unreadable. Added
   the plume **envelope**, which carries the mechanism at any size.
6. **A `\\'` from a shell heredoc** became a literal backslash in JS and broke
   the whole script block. Same class as the recorded `→` hazard.
