# V3.852 — The notebook on a phone: inert cards, tap-to-edit sheet

**Date:** 2026-10-23
**Alex:** *"all of the items in the notebook need to be made to work well with a mobile version. clicking
on one of the items should pull up a edit field from the bottom. do not edit fields directly on the item
in the mobile version. i still want to be able to move the item around the canvas. check for all the
options in the item works on mobile"*

## Goal

On a phone, a notebook card is a thing you move and a thing you open — never a thing you type into
on the sheet. A tap opens a bottom sheet with every field and every option the card has; a drag moves
the card. Every kind of card, and every action its ⋯ menu offers on a desktop, works from the sheet.

## Approach

**Mobile mode lives in the engine** (`whiteboard.js`), because the engine owns the card chrome, the
drag, the colour, the width, the wires and the regions; the host supplies the editors and the menu.

- **Detection** — `opts.mobile`: `true` / `false` / `'auto'` (default). Auto = a host board
  (`notebookBoards` / `paperBoards`) with a coarse pointer and the short side of the viewport under
  700 px (phones in either orientation; an iPad stays a desktop). Auto re-evaluates on `resize`. The
  hosts pass `?mobile=1|0` through for testing. `S.mobile` → class `wb-m` on the board root.
- **Inert cards** (CSS under `.wb-m`) — a card's body and title are `pointer-events: none`, the corner
  grip and the connection dots are hidden, the header buttons grow to 2 em, and the whole card is
  `touch-action: none` so a finger on it never scrolls the page.
- **Whole-card drag, tap to open** — `bindItemHandlers.onDown` accepts a pointer anywhere on a card in
  mobile mode (buttons and the colour popover excepted). The drag arms only after 8 px of travel, so a
  tap is never a one-pixel move. On a motionless pointer-up the sheet opens
  (`d.tap` → `openItemSheet`). The ⋯ button opens the sheet too — on a phone the sheet IS the menu.
- **The sheet** (`openItemSheet` / `closeItemSheet`, appended to `document.body`, z 2550 under the
  `cw-pop` popovers at 2600): grip, kind label, **Done**; the **title** input; the **body**, rendered by
  the host's `widgets.renderSheet(item, body, api)` when it exists, else `widgets.render` — the same
  editor the card shows on a desktop, with an `api` whose `remove` closes the sheet and whose
  `refresh` re-renders both the sheet and the card; the **colour** swatches (`ITEM_TINTS`, or
  `REGION_COLORS` for a region); the **width** S / M / L / XL (0.30 / 0.46 / 0.62 / 0.85); the
  **connections** list (each with ×, `removeWire`) and a "Connect →" picker over the other cards
  (`addWire`); the **actions**: Collapse / Expand, Bring to front, then everything the host's
  `widgets.menuItems(item, api)` returns (the exact list the desktop ⋯ menu shows — Duplicate, Remove,
  Replace the image, Unload the protocol, Pick a different action…), with the same two-tap confirm
  for dangerous ones (`armTwoTap`). A region's sheet has its name, its six colours, Open / Collapse,
  Fit around its cards, Remove; an instance's sheet has the source link and Remove. Nothing in the
  sheet reaches the board (pointer / key events stop at the backdrop); the backdrop tap, Done or
  Escape close it; a card deleted elsewhere closes it; closing re-renders the card's body, so a
  protocol's or calculator's live widgets return to the card after the sheet borrowed them.
- **Hosts** — `notebook-canvas.js` and `figure-board-canvas.js` factor their ⋯ menu lists into
  `widgetMenuItems(item, api)` and pass `menuItems` + `renderSheet` + `mobile`. The HTML frame kind gets
  a `sheet()` renderer (file name + size; the frame keeps running on the card instead of a second copy
  in the sheet); every other kind renders its normal editor in the sheet.

## Decisions

- The sheet reuses each kind's editor rather than a second mobile form per kind: one source of truth
  for every field, and new kinds are mobile-ready by default (`renderSheet` is the escape hatch).
- Threshold 700 px on the short side: phones are mobile in both orientations, iPads are not (Alex
  draws on the iPad with inline editing and a keyboard).
- Width presets instead of the corner grip: a grip is too small for a finger; four sizes cover the
  cases and the desktop keeps the grip.
- Connections via a picker in the sheet: the port dots are hidden on a phone (too small, and a drag
  from a card is now a move).

## Two modes per card (same day)

**Alex:** *"every card needs two modes, 1 change a cards settings, 2 change the actual thing the card does.
settings do stuff like change name, color, etc. Please go through and ensure all cards work and have been
tested with an opus agent."*

The sheet became two panes with a segmented switch: **✎ Edit** hosts the card's editor; **⚙ Settings** holds
the name, colour, width presets, connections and the actions. A tap opens Edit (you tapped the thing), the
⋯ opens Settings (you asked about the card). Regions and instances have only Settings, so they get no tabs.
On a computer the ⋯ popover's first entry, "Card settings…", opens the same sheet as a centred panel
(`widgetApi.openSettings`); inside the sheet that entry is suppressed (`api.inSheet`).

The harness grew to every kind — note, checklist, figure, HTML frame, quote, calculator, protocol,
inventory, action, lit review, linked board — plus a region and an instance of a region from another board
(a source board is seeded and the instance mirrors its card): each kind opens in Edit with its own controls
and in Settings with its own actions; 56 checks. `scripts/tests/v3852-notebook-mobile-sandbox.html` is the
same page without the checks, for hands-on testing (`?mobile=0` for the desktop); an Opus QA agent was
briefed on it to try every card in both modes independently of the harness. `sw.js` → 834.

Lessons: synthetic taps must be dispatched on the element the browser would hit (pointer-events honoured),
but after a test has moved or duplicated cards they overlap, so later taps go to the target node directly;
a tap must never snap the card (an off-grid card counted as moved); the runner's `page.evaluate` is an
isolated world, so a harness reports its own diagnostics into the page.
