# V3.695 — §5.2 stage 1 moves onto the section engine, and its first criterion was stale

> "the cabinet solved 5.2 should use the same side view simulaiton as tab 2 and 3"

## What changed

§5.2's stage 1 ("open the cabinet to the right height") was the last hand-drawn
side view in the lesson — its own frame, its own sash, its own three decorative
downflow arrows, and no circuit at all. It is now the **shared section engine**:
the same cabinet as §2.1 and every section on tab 3, with the real split, the
real drain-pan and back-duct circuit, live tracers, the sash gauge on the frame
with its mark and its tolerance band, and the work-zone limit. Canvas 640×392 →
980×500, matching the rest.

The drag survives unchanged. `secGeom`'s `yOf(inch) = surfY − span·(inch/20)`
inverts to exactly the mapping the existing pointer handler already used, so
publishing `CHK._surfY = g.surfY` and `CHK._inner = g.yOf(20)` was the whole
adaptation. Verified by driving the real handle: 14 in → 8 in and back.

`stepCheck` was an empty function on a 90 ms timer; it now advances the tracer
field at 33 ms, in stage 1 only.

## The part that was not cosmetic

Stage 1's first criterion still read:

```js
{ok: vIn(sash) >= CAB.vinMin && sash >= 6,
 t: 'Sash at a height that is both in specification and workable', …}
```

which is the **pre-V3.689 model**. On this cabinet the inflow is 0.53 m/s at
every opening from about 1 in to 13 in, so `vIn(sash) >= 0.51` is satisfied
almost everywhere and the criterion was passing the student at 10, 11, 12
inches — precisely the heights V3.689 established are *not certified*. The
capstone was grading against the physics the lesson had already corrected.

Now:

```js
{ok: sashOK(sash) && sash*2.54 >= 12,
 t: 'Sash on the marked work position, and workable', …}
```

and the right-hand panel is built around that distinction rather than hiding it.
It shows the two criteria as ticks, and then shows the airflow bar **as a
non-criterion**, with the line that only makes sense after §2.1:

> Green — and the sash is off the mark. This is the whole of §2.1: the cabinet
> holds 0.53 m/s by raising the exhaust volume, so the instrument you would
> naturally check is the one that cannot fail you. What you lose off the mark is
> certification, not velocity.

That is a better capstone moment than the one it replaces: the student's natural
check is demonstrated to be the one that will not catch them.

The second criterion is now a **drawn clearance** rather than a threshold — the
forearm is an 8 cm ellipse in an opening `2.54·sash` cm tall, so "can I work
through this" is a picture. The three stage-1 verdicts are rewritten to match:
on the mark; nearly shut (green airflow, no room for an arm, and uncertified);
and off the mark (green airflow, and that is the trap).
