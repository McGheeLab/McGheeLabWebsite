# V3.701 — §3.3 for the design as it now stands: a drawer that stays in the incubator

## The design Alex settled on

> Ok the box now stays in the incubator and its role is only to keep the temperature
> and CO2 variation damped between door openings. The box is still aluminum 5mm thick.
> the plate slides in and out like a drawer. The culture plate is moved to a 37C warm
> plate in the BSC or microsope for any feedings or bench work.

That is a cleaner arrangement than a carrier — the warm plate does the hard part, the
box never leaves 37 °C, so the whole condensation finding from V3.697 simply goes away.
It also leaves the box exactly one job, which makes it fair to ask how good at it the
box actually is.

## Model the whole chain, because the last link decides the answer

Four first-order lags between the room and the cells, three of them already in the
lesson:

| | |
|---|---|
| chamber recovery after an opening | ~6 min (§4.1) |
| box metal | `τ = ρct/h` = **20 min** (eq. 2.3.4) |
| box gas | `τ = VL/(D_eff A)` = 6 min at 4 filters (eq. 2.3.5) |
| **the medium itself** | `τ = h²/2D` = **18 min** for a T-25, **171 min** for a 200 µL well (eq. 2.2.6) |

**The medium got there first.** A door opening is over in 30 seconds and the culture is
already behind a filter thirty times longer than that, before the box does anything.

## What the box actually buys (T-25, 4 openings/hour)

| | worst dip at the cells |
|---|---|
| CO₂, no box | 0.78% |
| CO₂, in the box | 0.74% — **5% removed** |
| temperature, no box | 0.99 °C |
| temperature, in the box | 0.72 °C — **28% removed** |

The asymmetry is the point: 5 mm of aluminium is 20 minutes of lag against a
half-minute disturbance and damps temperature usefully; on the gas side the medium had
already done the work, and no filter count changes that (1 filter → 7%, 8 → 4%).

## Two further results

**A filter cannot fix an average.** At 12 openings/hour the chamber stops recovering
between them and the culture sits at a standing depression of ~1.6% CO₂ — the box
removes ~1% of it. A low-pass filter removes ripple, not offset. The fix there is fewer
openings, which is §1.2's rack argument, not a better box.

**The better it damps, the more the drawer timing matters.** One filter damps best —
and open the drawer while the incubator door is still open and the box fills with the
chamber at its worst and *holds* it for its own 23-minute τ. The dip comes out **1.02%
against 0.78% with no box at all**: the damping has become a liability, because the
disturbance was injected downstream of the filter.

## So the section's conclusion changes

Keep the box, for the two things it is genuinely excellent at, **neither of which is
damping**:

- **It is a lid.** §3.2 established that what reaches your cultures on this incubator is
  what entered *downstream* of the HEPA — the door and the shelves. A closed drawer
  takes that to zero for the plate inside it, and nothing about the medium does that.
- **It is a drawer.** §1.2's second map was traffic, and the only intervention that took
  the reach-over count to *zero* rather than merely reducing it was giving each project
  something it could be pulled out on. That is what this is.

The damping is a small bonus on top of a good containment and traffic decision.
