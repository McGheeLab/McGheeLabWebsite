# V3.693 — three things about tab 3 that were not true of a real cabinet

Alex, after driving the rebuilt tab 3:

> 3.1 the forearms are often covering the front grill and we should tell users this
> 3.2 the petri dish needs to be able to be moved back and fourth as well, and should stay behind the front section cutoff
> 3.4 the hand is shown all the way in the back of the cabinet, this is usually not the case

The third one is a modelling error I introduced in V3.692, not a drawing nit.

## §3.4 — I had the hand behind the dish

§3.4's offset slider has always meant *lateral* distance from the dish, which is
fine on a 44 cm strip of bench drawn face-on. When the section became the primary
view in V3.692 I mapped that offset onto **depth**, and picked the wrong sign:
`handCm = DISHCM + d` put the cuff at 48 cm — against the back wall — for the
default "beside" case. Hands do not go there. They come in from the front.

```js
const handCm=clamp(DISHCM-d,6,CABD-6), plumeCm=handCm-drift;
const sep=Math.abs(plumeCm-DISHCM);
```

That also **fixes a sign** that was wrong before the section existed. `drift`
carries the plume toward the front grille as it falls. The old code took
`|d − drift|`, which assumed the drift always closes the gap. With the hand in
front of the dish it *opens* it, so the separation is now computed from where
the plume actually lands rather than from where the hand is. The numbers barely
move (17.3 cm instead of 14.7 cm on the default) but the model is no longer
right by accident.

Consequences that fell out and are worth having:

- Pulling the hand all the way toward you (24 cm) puts the cuff at 8 cm — **on
  the front grille** — which is §3.1's problem. That earns its own verdict, and
  it makes the point that there is a *right answer between the two extremes*,
  about 10 cm wide, rather than a direction to push in.
- The scenario chips are renamed: "beside the dish" → **"in front of the dish,
  where hands actually go"**, "in and out fast, but beside it" → "…but not over
  it". "Approach from the side" becomes "approach from beside or in front,
  never from above", which is what the model now shows.

## §3.1 — the commonest blockage is your own arms

New `arms` scenario chip, a forearm drawn resting on the front grille in the
section (one — a side cut meets one arm; the pair is a *width*, so it goes in
the plan panel), an insight box, and a rewritten "nothing on either grille"
rule. Two forearms cover about 30% of the intake: velocity through what is left
goes to 0.76 m/s, the split runs forward to 24%, and 19.8% of room air reaches
the surface — numbers indistinguishable from the absorbent-pad case, which is
the point. Nobody counts their arms as clutter because arms are not objects.

The manual supplies the argument: armrests exist to *"minimize blockage of
airflow at the front grille"* — a sentence that only makes sense once you know
where arms end up by default.

## §3.2 — the dish is a control, and it stops at the work-zone limit

Both positions are now **absolute depths from the front edge** and independent:
`wake-d` is where the object sits (4–51 cm), new `wake-dish` is where the dish
sits (18–46 cm). Previously the offset dragged the dish along with it, so moving
one moved both and the separation never changed — the reason Alex could not
position the dish at all.

New engine constant **`WORKLIMIT = 18 cm`** and `secWorkLimit()`, drawn as a
faint band on the tray rather than a third dashed vertical (there are two
already and a third reads as air). The front grille is about 8 cm of the tray
and the standard rule adds 4 in behind it, so nothing open goes in front of
~18 cm — and the dish slider will not let you. The object is deliberately *not*
clamped, because being able to stand it on a grille and be told so is worth
more than being prevented; a new `objOnGrille` check gives that its own verdict.

## Verification

- Behaviour QA over all five tabs: zero dead sliders, zero dead chips, zero blank
  canvases, zero page errors.

as `2.69e-9 %` (now `< 0.01 %` / `effectively none` / `≈ 0`), and the cuff label
