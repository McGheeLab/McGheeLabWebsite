# V3.698 — §4.1 gets the same animation as §3.2

## What Alex said

> 4.1 should have the same style animation as 3.2

## The one design decision

§3.2's animation is a **steady state** — air going round a circuit forever. §4.1's
two mechanisms are not steady, and they are not even the same *kind* of event:

- the **swing** is a one-off slug, thrown in the second the panel is moving;
- the **dwell** is a saturating flow that dies away on eq. 1.5.1.

Animating both as one continuous stream would have made them look identical, which
is exactly the confusion the section exists to remove ("the two instructions are
opposites, and that is why they get confused"). So §4.1 plays **the real cycle on a
loop** instead:

```
0 shut → 1 swinging open → 2 open (the dwell) → 3 closing → 4 held shut
```

with tracers in the §3.2 style on both panels.

**Timing.** The swing runs at its **real** duration — 0.9 m of panel travel at the
door's own speed, clamped to 0.5–3.5 s — so flinging it is visibly quicker and a
slow open visibly takes a beat longer. The dwell is time-compressed onto 1.5–7 s of
wall clock with the **real clock printed beside it** ("open — 25 s of 75"), because
120 s of animation is not a thing anybody will watch.

**The chamber fills in real time inside that phase.** `fill = 1 − e^(−t_real/τₓ)` and
the tracer release rate goes as `e^(−t_real/τₓ)` — the flux, not the total. So a long
dwell visibly *stops exchanging* well before the door shuts: at 75 s the panel is
still open, the fill is at 85%, and almost nothing is moving. That is §4.1's whole
claim — the damage is done early, and the last minute is free only in the sense that
it is already too late — and it is now something you watch rather than read.

## What is drawn

| panel | tracers |
|---|---|
| PLAN | red slug particles released **as the panel sweeps**, only above Π = 1, count ∝ the swept volume; they disperse into the chamber and die |
| SECTION | amber leaving the top of the opening, cyan entering along the bottom and drifting up into the chamber, both at the decaying flux rate |

The red room-air wash in the plan and the blue fill in the section now track the
**live** cycle state rather than the end state, so the two panels agree with each
other and with the phase label.

## Follow-up, same session: the plan view was still wrong

> the door still opens weird. thats why we need the side view from 3.2. the door
> opening can just be visualized as a side view projection of a door opening.

Correct, and the reason is better than "it looks odd". **A door hinged on a vertical
edge is seen edge-on from the side**, so its projection onto that plane is a rectangle
growing out of the front face, of depth `W·sin θ` — **and that rectangle is exactly the
volume the panel has swept.** The picture no longer has to *assert* that the door is a
piston; the rate the rectangle grows *is* the speed of the panel face, which is
eq. 1.3.2's numerator. A side view is also the one projection that does not care which
edge the hinge is on, which retires the hinge question instead of answering it.

The plan panel is gone. §4.1 is one side view, matching §3.2 and §3.3, and the cycle
now separates the two doors: **the outer door swings and nothing happens**, because
the glass one is still holding the chamber — §1.1's point, finally drawn.
