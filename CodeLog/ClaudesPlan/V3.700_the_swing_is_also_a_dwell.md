# V3.700 — §4.1: the swing is also a dwell, so there is a best door speed

## What Alex said

> 4.1) while the door is being opened, there should also be some air exchange the
> animation makes it look like exchange only occurs when the door is fully open. This
> concept may be why people may favor opening too quick because the too slow has
> penalty as well.

Both halves are right, and the second half overturns the section's advice.

## The missing term

The model had exactly two mechanisms: a piston slug during the swing (only above
Π = 1) and a buoyancy exchange **once the door was open**. But the aperture is not shut
and then suddenly open — *it is open the whole time it is opening*, and the exchange
runs through it from the first crack.

Take the open fraction as sin θ and sweep θ from 0 to 90° in the time the panel needs
to travel its own width `W/v`:

```
∫ sin θ dt = (2/π)·(W/v)      ⇒   t_eff = (2/π)·W/v_door
```

The swing is worth that many seconds of a **fully open door**, before anybody has
reached in. A 0.9 m panel at a gentle 5 cm/s takes 18 s to travel, worth **11.5 s** of
open door. At 130 cm/s it is worth **0.4 s**.

## The consequence

Two penalties with opposite slopes — the piston grows with speed, the opening exchange
grows as you slow down — so there is a **minimum**, not a limit. With an 8 s dwell:

| door speed | Π | chamber replaced |
|---|---|---|
| 5 cm/s — as gently as you can | 0.17 | **62%** |
| 10 cm/s | 0.33 | 50% |
| **30 cm/s** | **1.00** | **39%** |
| 50 cm/s | 1.67 | 40% |
| 130 cm/s — flung | 4.33 | **51%** |

**Creeping is worse than flinging.** And the minimum is not an arbitrary number: it
sits at **Π = 1**. Move the door at about the speed the air can get out of its way —
below that you are simply holding the box open, above it you start throwing air in.
For this door, about **30 cm/s**, or **three seconds** for the panel.

That falls out of the model's own structure rather than being fitted: the piston term
is zero below Π = 1 by construction, so total-in is strictly falling there, and it
starts rising the moment the piston engages. The crossover *is* Π = 1.

**Alex's read on the behaviour is also right**: people fling doors because they have
correctly noticed that slow is not free, and the standard advice never gave them the
other half.

## What changed

- `inletSwingCost(v)` and `inletBestV()`; `inletState` now composes three terms
  multiplicatively (piston, opening, dwell) and reports each volume separately.
- The animation exchanges during the swing at a rate ∝ sin θ, with `INLETF.tEff`
  accumulating *effective open seconds* across the swing (real time) and the dwell
  (compressed, scaled back up). The fill rises from the first crack.
- Ledger gains **"drawn in WHILE it was opening"**.
- A new mini-chart: **swing cost against door speed**, with the Π > 1 region shaded,
  the minimum marked, and the current speed as a dot on the curve.
- Stats gain the swing duration, its effective open-seconds, and the best speed with a
  "← you are there" marker.
- New verdict for **too slow**; the "matched" verdict now outranks the Π > 1 one, which
  was calling 35 cm/s a fridge while the stats said it was optimal; the fridge branch
  moved to Π > 1.3.
- Prose: the rule "open it like a book, not like a fridge" is replaced with **"move the
  door at the speed of the air"**, and the closing insight now separates *match the
  swing* from *minimise the dwell*.
- No new numbered equation — `t_eff` is a derived relation and goes in a `step-eq`.
