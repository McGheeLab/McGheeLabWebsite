## Goal

Teach the optical components between the sample and the camera (or
eyepiece) — the *image train* — and how to read an objective spec sheet.
This is the module a student should be able to recite from when buying
an objective or troubleshooting an out-of-spec image.

## Format

Full interactive notebook (MODULE_TEMPLATE.md pattern). 5 tabs.

## Tabs

### Tab 1 — End-to-End Light Path
**Bio context:** the journey of a photon emitted by a GFP molecule in a
spheroid on its way to a pixel on the camera.

- Components in order: sample → coverslip → immersion medium →
  objective → tube lens → intermediate optics (filter cubes, beam
  splitters, scan optics for confocal) → port (camera or eyepiece)
- Interactive: a draggable photon traverses the schematic; click any
  element to see what it does + what changing it would cost

### Tab 2 — Decoding an Objective Spec
Front-of-lens markings explained:

```
Plan Apo λ 60× / 1.40 Oil ∞ / 0.17 WD 0.13
```

- `Plan` (field flatness), `Apo` (apochromatic chromatic correction),
  `λ` (extra UV/IR transmission), `60×` (magnification at the
  manufacturer's reference tube-lens focal length), `1.40` (NA),
  `Oil` (immersion), `∞` (infinity-corrected — needs a tube lens),
  `0.17` (coverslip thickness in mm), `WD 0.13` (working distance, mm)
- Interactive: a "spec decoder" — paste any objective marking, page
  parses and color-codes each token; show the trade-offs

### Tab 3 — Immersion, Coverslips & Correction Collars
- Background: refractive index match between objective, immersion
  medium, coverslip, mounting medium, and sample — spherical aberration
  when any link is off
- Interactive: dial the coverslip thickness (0.13–0.22 mm) + the
  correction-collar setting → simulated PSF shows the aberration; turn
  the collar to compensate
- Worked example: imaging deep into a 100 μm spheroid in 80% glycerol
  with an oil objective → severe spherical aberration; the fix is a
  water-immersion or glycerol-immersion lens, not the correction collar

### Tab 4 — Tube Lens, Field of View & Port Coupling
- Background: tube lens focal length × objective magnification = system
  magnification; modern Nikon uses 200 mm tube lens (Olympus 180,
  Zeiss 165) — mixing brands changes mag
- Interactive: pick objective + tube lens + sensor size → page computes
  field of view, pixel size at sample (μm/pixel), Nyquist match
- Worked example: 25×/1.1 + 200 mm tube + Hamamatsu Fusion BT (2304 ×
  2304, 6.5 μm pixel) → 0.26 μm/pixel; Nyquist needs 0.20 μm — close
  to but not quite at Nyquist

### Tab 5 — Capstone: objective purchase decision
Given an experimental need ("image actin filaments in fixed spheroids
at high resolution; ~50 μm depth"), the student fills in: target NA,
immersion type, WD constraint, mag, budget. The page recommends a
match from a curated catalog of objectives compatible with the Ti2-U /
AX-R, with line items justifying each spec.
