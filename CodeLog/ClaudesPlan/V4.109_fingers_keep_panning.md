# V4.109 — Scientia: fix — in pen mode, fingers keep panning the note

## Why
Gabe, 23 Nov: "another bug: after some time on a note canvas, while in pen mode, your fingers stop being able to pan on the note. You have to exit out of the note and re enter it to be able to pan again"

## The cause
Each note's paper keeps the touches it is following (13zf, V3.941's palm rules):
- `T.palm`: touches counted as a palm until they lift;
- `T.touch` and `P.ptrs`: fingers down.

A touch leaves those sets only when its lift (pointerup or pointercancel) reaches the paper. While anything is in `T.palm`, every new touch counts as part of the same hand, so it is a palm, and it neither pans nor pinches. A finger left in `P.ptrs` makes the next finger a pinch with it. Nothing else cleared them. A new paper does, which is why leaving the note and coming back fixed it.

**Where a lift gets lost:**
- The iPad sends a touch's lift to the element the touch began on, even after that element has left the page.
- A pen stroke draws the ink component's strokes again (a probe: the strokes are replaced, the component's box and the paper are kept).
- So a hand resting on earlier writing while the pen writes more loses its lift, and in pen mode the hand rests on the writing all the time.
- Chrome sends such a lift to what is under the finger instead, so on the desktop the same moves do not show it. The test therefore stops that lift itself before the paper hears it.

## What changed
- **`13zf-ink.js`**, the paper's touch handling:
  - when a touch comes down while no other touch is down (`isPrimary`, the first finger on the glass), every touch the paper still remembers has lifted;
  - `T.fresh()` forgets them (`T.palm`, `T.touch`, their entries in `P.ptrs`) and ends a pinch or finger pan left over from them;
  - `T.seen` holds every touch the paper heard, including ones on a button that the paper's own pointer handler counted.
- **A palm really resting still counts:** a finger put down beside it is not the first on the glass, so it is still a palm, and two fingers still pinch.
