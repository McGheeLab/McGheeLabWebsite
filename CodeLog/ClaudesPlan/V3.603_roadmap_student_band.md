# V3.603 — Research Roadmap R4: "how the lab moves forward"

## Goal

Alex's second ask:

> On the research overview page I want a section that is valuable for students
> to understand all of the things going on in the lab and what they need to do
> to help accomplish those goals.

The research hub showed the map but never said what a student should *do*
about it. R1–R3 built the substrate (experiments → analyses → figures, and
weekly focus areas that align to a specific analysis); R4 is the surface that
turns it into an invitation.

## Approach

One band at the top of the hub, above the three level bands, answering the
three questions a new student asks **in the order they ask them**:

1. **🎯 What we're driving toward** — every output card with a roadmap, its
   one-liner, and a progress bar (`N of M analyses done`).
2. **🙋 Open work you could pick up** — every analysis that is not `done` and
   has no claim *this week*, with its experiment context, the figure it feeds,
   and a **🎯 Make this my focus** button.
3. **👥 Who's on what this week** — claimed analyses and who claimed them.

Three columns side by side, so the answer to one question stays visible while
reading the next. At ≤1024px the third column drops to a full-width row
beneath (it's the least urgent of the three); at ≤640px they stack.

### Cost

Zero extra structural reads: roadmaps ride on the card docs that `listAll()`
already fetched. The only new read is one whole-collection get of
`roadmapClaims`.

### Claim staleness is a feature

Claim docs are keyed `{uid}__{analysisId}` and persist across weeks, so the
collection stays bounded (users × analyses) rather than growing forever. The
band filters on `week_id === thisWeek`, which means **last week's claim
automatically frees the analysis back into "open work"** — exactly right, since
an unrenewed claim is an abandoned one.

### "Make this my focus"

Creates a focus area on the signed-in user's current week titled after the
analysis, described with its experiment and target figure, carrying the
analysis-level `focus_ref`, then writes the claim mirror. It lands as
`proposed` — the mentor signs off from Team Focus, exactly as any other
student-originated focus does. The confirm dialog says so, because a student
should know their click is visible to the lab.

## Verification

`scratchpad/t3.js` renders the section and the band against a stubbed
`firebridge` and asserts: progress counts, analysis anchors, figure targets,
admin-only edit button, no add-ref button without `ROADMAP_ATTACH`, concept
cards get no section, the empty state, and for the band — goal progress, a
current-week claim landing under "who's on what", **a last-week claim freeing
its analysis back to open work**, done and claimed analyses excluded from open
work, blocked sorting above planned, and the band collapsing to nothing when no
card has a roadmap.

That harness caught a real bug: the open-work sort used
`(rank[status] || 3)`, and `blocked`'s rank is `0` — falsy — so blocked
analyses sorted **last** instead of first, burying exactly the rows the band
exists to surface. Fixed with an explicit `typeof === 'number'` check.
