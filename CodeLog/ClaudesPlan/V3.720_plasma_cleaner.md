### The signature fact the lesson is built on

Harrick's FAQ publishes the power settings for all three cleaners:

| Model | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic PDC-32G | 7 W | 11 W | 18 W |
| Expanded PDC-001/002 | 7 W | 11 W | 30 W |
| **High Power Expanded PDC-001-HP** | **30 W** | **38 W** | **45 W** |

**This lab's LOW is the standard model's HIGH** — Harrick's datasheet says so in
as many words. Every published recipe written for a PDC-001 at "HIGH" is this
machine's *lowest* setting, so following the words exactly delivers 1.5× the
intended dose. That is the lesson's title and the `power` widget exists to make
the conversion concrete.

### Two derivations that make the instrument legible

1. **Chamber pressure is set by the flowmeter, not the pump.** At steady state
   \(p = Q/S\). The PlasmaFlo's full-scale 50 mL/min of air at atmosphere is
   38,000 Torr·mL/min; against a 50 L/min oxygen-service pump that is
   **0.76 Torr** — landing at the top of Harrick's recommended 200–800 mTorr
   window. The flowmeter's full scale *is* the operating window.
2. **Pump-down.** The 6″ × 6.5″ chamber is 3.0 L, so \(V/S = 3.6\) s and
   reaching 200 mTorr takes about 30 s of ideal pumping — with the honest note
   that the manual's "few minutes" is the reality, because of hose conductance
   and a pump that slows near its ultimate. The useful output is not the time
   but the **floor**, \(Q/S\).

### Two non-obvious lessons the sims are built to deliver

- **The contact angle cannot see over-treatment.** Two minutes of plasma takes
  PDMS to ~6.8°; thirty minutes takes it to 13.7° — worse, but nowhere near
  enough to look like failure — while the oxide skin thickens the whole time and
  cracks. The `over` widget plots both curves so the divergence is visible.
- **Wettability and bondability do not decay together.** Bondability falls with
  a time constant of tens of minutes (Harrick's 15–60 min window); the contact
  angle recovers over tens of hours (the literature's ~72 h). An hour after
  treatment a surface still wets beautifully and will not weld. `recovery` draws
  both, which is the only way to make "it looked fine" stop being a defence.

## Sourcing discipline

Every operating number is Harrick's, per Alex's instruction. Where Harrick
publishes nothing, the page says so rather than filling the gap:

- **No per-gas glow-colour chart exists.** Harrick state only *purple /
  purple-pink for air*. The lesson uses the glow as a presence check and says
  explicitly that colour is not a gas identification.
- **No RF frequency is published for the HP unit** — only "MHz frequency". The
  8–12 MHz figure belongs to the *Basic* PDC-32G manual and is not attributed to
  this machine.
- **Nothing on ozone venting, UV, RF emissions, prohibited materials, chamber
  cleaning or any maintenance interval.** All marked "not stated by the
  manufacturer", with the defensible defaults labelled as house practice.

The PDC32G-M-03 manual is quoted throughout for safety and procedure, and is
flagged where its numbers are Basic-model-only.

## Durable build lessons

1. **A control that feeds nothing is a design bug, not a QA false positive.**
   The `window` widget shipped in draft with an RF slider that no stat depended
   on. The fix was to make power genuinely set the lower edge of the pressure
   window — which Harrick's own "lowest level that will light" instruction
   implies anyway — rather than to delete the control or silence the check.
2. **A widget whose default state makes its scenario chips identical reads as
   broken.** `recovery` opened at zero delay, where nothing has decayed, so all
   three storage scenarios were the same. `pumpdown` opened at 60 s, already
   fully pumped down, so both ends of the time slider read the floor. Both were
   fixed by moving the *default* onto the interesting part of the curve.
3. **The canvas draws in CSS pixels, so a fixed-pixel layout leaves the bottom
   of a wide canvas empty.** The drawing surface is `w × w·(attrH/attrW)`, which
   grows with the viewport while absolute y-offsets do not — the capstone was 40%
   empty at 950 px. Deriving the row pitch from the available height fixes it at
   every width. The framework's new `dataset.cvh` probe exists for exactly this.
4. **Reading the screenshot caught a clipped axis that every numeric check
   passed.** The mean-free-path chart was drawn with a y-range of 0.01–100 cm
   when the entire operating band is tens of *micrometres*, so the whole
   interesting region sat flat on the axis floor. Stats, verdicts, behaviour and
   drift all passed.
5. **A vendor's silence is not permission, and it is not an invitation to
   improvise.** Harrick publish no glow-colour chart, no HP frequency and no
   maintenance schedule. The temptation to fill those from general knowledge was
   strong and specific; naming the gap is more useful to a reader than a
   plausible number they cannot check.
