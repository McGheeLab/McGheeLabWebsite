## Goal

Teach a student to write a Discussion that does its four jobs —
interpret, situate, limit, project — instead of re-summarizing the
Results. Of all sections, this is where reviewers attack hardest, and
where Advocate voice (per V3.182) earns its name.

## Format

Lecture-style. Three interactive widgets:

1. **Four-jobs checklist** — interactive widget where the student
   pastes their draft Discussion (or just tracks mental coverage); the
   checklist marks whether each job (interpret / situate / limit /
   project) is addressed.
2. **Limitation honesty meter** — slider from "over-claimed" to
   "over-hedged"; shows example phrasings at each level. The lab's
   target is the middle 40% — claims that are bold but bounded.
3. **Discussion vs Results separator** — paste a paragraph; tool
   flags sentences that belong in Results (Reporter voice) vs.
   Discussion (Advocate voice).

## Sections (10)

1. The Discussion's four jobs: interpret, situate, limit, project.
2. The opening paragraph — what your results mean, in one sentence.
3. Situating against the literature — agreement, disagreement,
   extension.
4. Limitations — honest, specific, not boilerplate.
5. The implication ladder — your finding implies a slightly broader
   claim, which implies a broader one (per V3.182 narrative scope).
6. Future work — projecting forward without overpromising.
7. Voice: Advocate (per V3.182). Claim-making with humility.
8. The "summarize Results" trap.
9. The "over-claim generalization" trap.
10. Capstone — write the opening paragraph + close.
