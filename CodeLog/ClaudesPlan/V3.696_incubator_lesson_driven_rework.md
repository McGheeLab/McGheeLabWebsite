## What reading the manual changed

Per the standing lesson from V3.689 (*read the manual for equipment the lab owns
before modelling it*), I pulled the lab's own incubator manual out of
`Data/equipment/` before touching any drawing. It is a **Heracell VIOS 160i LK**.
Findings are recorded in
[CodeLog/References/Incubator_VIOS160i_verified_facts.md](CodeLog/References/Incubator_VIOS160i_verified_facts.md).
Three of them contradict what the lesson shipped:

1. **The humidity setpoint is 93%, not 95%.** The manual gives "approx 93 %rH"
   as the constant high-humidity value at 37 °C, and a **Low Humidity setting at
   approx 90%** whose stated purpose is condensation on culture containers.
   Alex's ">95% is difficult" question is answered by the manufacturer directly.
2. **The air from the water reservoir is HEPA-filtered before it reaches the
   workspace** — 99.998% at 0.3 µm, through an airbox that sits on the reservoir
   cover and feeds the rear-wall duct. §3.2 was drawing a mechanism (fan lifts
   spores off the tray onto your flasks) that **does not exist on this machine**.
   That is why the illustration explained nothing: it was a picture of a
   different incubator.
3. **The door switch is on the *glass* door**, it suspends gas supply *and*
   heating, and the box **alarms at 30 seconds**. §4.1 drew one door; the whole
   lesson's punchline is that there are two.

Also: the shipped §3.2 recommends "sterile distilled + approved additive" as the
*best* water. The manual voids the warranty for chlorine-containing additives and
explicitly does not recommend a constant disinfectant (or copper sulfate) in the
reservoir — and voids it again for ultrapure/DI, which corrodes the stainless,
copper and glass. The best answer is distilled water inside a **conductivity
band** (1–20 µS/cm, pH 7–9), with nothing added.

## The physics, checked before it was drawn

Verified numerically in the scratchpad (`model.py`, `week.py`) before writing any
canvas code.

**§3.1 — the plate.** Per-well evaporation is a **series resistance**: vertical
out of the well headspace, then lateral under the lid to the plate edge. That is
eq. 2.3.1 with δ made explicit, so it needs no new number.

```
R(c,r) = h_head/(D·A_well) + d_edge(c,r)/(D·g·pitch)
```

At the manufacturer's own 93% RH, 200 µL wells, 3 days: **corner 18.2 µL lost
→ 352 mOsm; centre 5.0 µL → 328 mOsm**, a rate ratio of **3.63×**. Nobody dried
out. Nothing looks wrong. The plate carries a 24 mOsm gradient that is
*correlated with position*, which is the worst possible kind. Drop to 80% (a pan
nobody filled) and the corner is at **432 mOsm** — past the stress line — while
the centre is still at 345. **Two different experiments on one plate.**

**§3.1 — the ceiling.** New eq. 2.3.3, the dew-point margin (Magnus):

```
ΔT_max = T − T_dew(RH);  condensation wherever a surface is colder than that
```

37 °C: 90% → 1.92 °C, **93% → 1.32 °C**, 95% → 0.94, 98% → 0.37. The box's own
spatial spread is ≤ ±0.3 °C and its walls, ceiling and outer door are *actively
heated* — so the chamber clears its own margin. **Your vessels are not heated.**
A plate arriving from the cabinet fogs for **27 minutes** at 93% (τ ≈ 11 min from
its own thermal mass), and Low Humidity mode buys only ~4 of those minutes back.
That is the honest answer to "why is >93% hard": not that the pan cannot supply
it, but that every degree of headroom you spend is headroom a cold vessel needs.

**§3.3 — the box.** Two new equations, and the first has a result worth the space:

```
eq. 2.3.4   τ_thermal = ρ c t / h        ← the AREA CANCELS
eq. 2.3.5   τ_gas     = V L / (D P g)
```

The thermal buffering of a metal box depends on **wall thickness alone**, not on
how big the box is: 1 mm → 4 min, 5 mm → 20 min, 10 mm → 40 min. The gas lag
depends on the lid gap: 0.3 mm → 88 min for CO₂, 1 mm → 26 min, 3 mm → 9 min.
Water vapour always moves 1.6× faster than CO₂ (D ratio), so **a box always
re-humidifies before it re-gasses** — which is why it does not rain inside while
it warms up.

So the answer to Alex's question is a genuine trade with a genuine resolution:

- **Temperature: yes, decisively.** A 20 s door opening moves the box interior by
  about 0.2 °C.
- **CO₂: it cuts both ways.** The same gap that buffers a door opening delays
  recovery from a bench trip. You cannot tune one without the other.
- **Contamination: it converts a continuous low-level exposure into a rare
  high-dose one** — better if and only if you control the rare event, which means
  wiping the rim every time and autoclaving on a schedule.
- **The resolution is not a gap at all.** The Cell Locker is a polycarbonate
  chamber with **two membrane filters, permeable to water vapour and gas,
  impermeable to microorganisms** — the vented flask cap from §3.1, at box scale.

**§5.1 — is 10 mL the answer?** No, and the lesson already contained the
refutation two tabs earlier. §2.3 establishes that oxygen reaches the monolayer
only by diffusion through the whole column and that the CO₂ lag goes as *h²*.
§5.1 then advocated 10 mL in a T-25 — **4 mm**, twice the depth §2.3 calls a
diffusion length — and said nothing about the bill.

Sweeping seed × volume over the slider ranges and scoring Monday 09:00:

| plan | conf | pH | O₂ at the cells | CO₂ lag | depth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25k / 5 mL — what most people do | 68% | 6.93 | 84% | 18 min | 2.0 mm |
| 25k / 10 mL — **the shipped advice** | 68% | 7.28 | **68%** | **70 min** | 4.0 mm |
| 25k / 14 mL | 68% | 7.34 | **55%** | **138 min** | 5.6 mm |
| **32k / 7.5 mL — a little of both** | **75%** | **7.12** | **73%** | 39 min | 3.0 mm |

**368** seed/volume combinations pass on pH and confluence alone. Only **51** also
keep oxygen at the monolayer above 70% of the surface value — and every one of
those 51 lies between **2.6 mm and 3.4 mm** of depth. Adding the third criterion
collapses the space and pins it to the band §2.3 already derived. The answer is
not "more medium", it is **a little of both levers**, and there is also a clean
algebraic result behind it (new eq. 2.4.3): buffer duration goes as *depth /
seeding density*, so **the flask size cancels exactly** — a bigger flask does not
help.

The hypoxic-glycolysis feedback (cells make more lactate when oxygen falls) is
implemented because it is real, but it is *honestly small* at these depths and
the lesson does not lean on it — the cost of volume is direct, not a backfire.
