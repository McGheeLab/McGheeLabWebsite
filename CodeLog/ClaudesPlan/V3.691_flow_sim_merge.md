# V3.691 — §2.1: one picture, and a sim that actually answers the controls

## What Alex reported

> "2.1 does not show the correct simulation for the various changes we ask the
> user to make. and the two plot simulations in this section should be combined
> into one."

Both halves were right, and the first one turned out to have a cause much
bigger than §2.1.

## The root cause: the field restarted on every slider tick

`wireAll()` calls `RESET[widget]()` on **every `input` event** of every slider.
Five widgets in this lesson register a `reset` — `fall`, `flow`, `crowd`,
`wake`, `sweep` — and four of them use it to **seed a continuous tracer
field**. So dragging a slider re-seeded the field on every tick: the animation
never settled, and the moment you let go you were looking at a cold field that
took six seconds to refill. The controls *were* driving the model — the model
was being thrown away faster than it could respond.

Fix, in the framework block:

```js
let RESEED=false;
function keepField(arr){ return !!(arr && arr.length && !RESEED); }
```

`RESEED` is raised only at boot and in `setScen()`. Each continuous-field reset
now opens with `if(keepField(FIELD))return;`. Event-driven resets — `sweep`,
whose reset clears a crossing counter you *want* restarted — simply do not call
it. `resetFlow` additionally seeds in **steady state** (x spread across the room
*and* the chamber, v part-way down) so the first frame is already right; this
is what makes "blower off" show particles landing on the tray immediately,
which is the section's first student task.

This is a house-framework bug, not a §2.1 bug. Any future module built on
`modules/AUTHORING.md` §9 that animates a field and registers a `reset` has it.

## The rest of the controls had nothing to change

Even with a stable field, the tracers were decorative: their count and speed
were hard-coded (`i%9` for the leg, a fixed `0.055+0.05*r` for the rate). Moving
the downflow slider changed two printed numbers and nothing else on screen.

The sim is now driven end to end:

| Control | What now moves |
|---|---|
| Sash | opening height · gauge marker · alarm badge · **inflow tracer count** (volume) · inflow speed *only* once compensation runs out · exhaust bar · split position · breakthrough particles |
| Downflow | **downflow tracer count and speed** · split position · recirculated share · both ledger bars |
| `old` | same tracer count, **slower** — the fixed-blower contrast, visible instead of printed |
| `door` | cross-draft band and arrow in the room, the inflow fan swayed, breakthrough 1.2% → 22% |
| `off` | loop stops, ledger zeroes, particles settle onto the tray and into the dish |

Two model rules make the tracers mean something:

- **Spacing carries volume, speed carries velocity.** A tracer is lit when
  `t.a < dens`, `dens = (qEx+qDown)/qRef`; its rate is the leg's entry velocity.
  This is the whole 1300-Series lesson made visual: raise the sash and the
  inflow gets *denser*, not faster.
- **The split is computed, not drawn where it looks nice.** Both grilles open
  into the same plenum, so the descending air divides where each ends up at the
  same face velocity through its own open area:

  ```
  (qEx + f·qDown)/A_front = ((1-f)·qDown)/A_rear
  ```

  With `A_front/A_rear = 1.5` this puts the split **37% of the way in** at the
  certified point — about where a smoke test finds it — and makes it *move*:
  forward when the sash rises (26% at 12 in), forward when the downflow drops
  (12% at 0.15 m/s), back when it rises (44% at 0.45 m/s). When `f` would go
  negative the split jams against the front edge, which earns its own verdict:
  it is why NSF sets a minimum **downflow** as well as a minimum inflow.

## Two corrections to the content

- **The old face view captioned the front streamlines with the manual's
  smoke criterion — the wrong test.** "No smoke shall billow over the solid work
  surface or penetrate onto it" is the **work-opening-edge** test (smoke released
  1.5 in *in front of* the cabinet). It is a statement about room air, not about
  where the downflow goes. The claim survives in the inflow table row, where it
  belongs; the downflow's own criterion is "smooth downward flow with no dead
  spots or upward flow."
- **Below the mark now has its own verdict.** Dragging the sash under 7.25 in
  used to produce a warning that talked about *raising* the window. The band is
  lopsided on purpose (+0.25 / −0.75); below it, the inflow is still 0.53 m/s
  because the cabinet drops the volume, nothing was certified there either, and
  your forearms block a far larger fraction of a smaller opening. The manual's
  sensors call it an *unsafe intermediate position*.
