# V3.182 — Paper writing: paragraphs, voice &amp; scope

> and [rm/js/training-paper-paragraphs.js](../../rm/js/training-paper-paragraphs.js).
> First entry in the paper-writing series (V3.182–V3.187) that expands
> the V3.163 overview into deep dives.

## Goal

Teach the *sentence-level craft* of scientific writing — the moves that
make a first draft feel polished. Three big ideas:

1. **The paragraph skeleton** — every scientific paragraph has a 3-part
   shape (claim / evidence / link, with section-specific variants).
   Write skeletons first, sentences second.
2. **The four voices** — Reporter (Results), Storyteller (Intro +
   Abstract), Advocate (Discussion), Technician (Methods). Each section
   has a default voice; mixing them is the most common first-draft
   failure mode.
3. **Narrative scope** — broad enough to make readers care, specific
   enough to keep the paper focused. The "zoom out without losing
   focus" move is the discussion-section-rescue tool.

This training is the foundation V3.183–V3.187 reference back to. The
section-specific trainings will assume the student knows the voices and
skeletons defined here.

## Format

Lecture-style page (V3.163 / V3.176 / V3.177 pattern): sticky TOC +
sequential `.training-section` blocks + four interactive widgets.

### Widgets
1. **Skeleton-to-sentence transformer** — cycle through 4 paragraph
   examples (Results / Intro / Discussion / Methods); toggle between
   "skeleton" and "rendered prose" views.
2. **Voice comparator** — same paragraph topic rendered in all 4 voices
   side-by-side; labels show which section each belongs in.
3. **Scope dial** — slider 1–10. At each level, a text example of the
   same project written at that scope ("Cancer kills millions" through
   "Edge density above 0.12 cells/μm² inverted the effect of 1.5 vs
   4.5 kPa matrix stiffness on a 24-hour invasion assay").
4. **Paragraph builder capstone** — form: skeleton type + claim/evidence
   /link inputs + voice + target section. Output: rendered paragraph +
   critique (warns when voice doesn't match section, suggests skeleton
   variant if the chosen one is wrong for the section).

## Sections (10)

1. Why most first drafts fail at the paragraph level.
2. The paragraph skeleton (3-part shape + section-specific variants).
3. From skeleton to sentences (interactive transformer).
4. The four voices of scientific writing.
5. Which voice for which section (mapping table).
6. Active vs passive, "we" / "I" / "the data show."
7. Narrative scope — broad enough to care, specific enough to focus.
8. Zooming out without losing focus (callback / implication ladder /
   comparison anchor).
9. Common voice mistakes.
10. Capstone — paragraph builder.
