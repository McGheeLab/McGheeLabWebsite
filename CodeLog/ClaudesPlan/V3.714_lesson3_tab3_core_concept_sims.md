# V3.714 — Lesson 3, tab 3: the two rules get sims that *are* the concept

Alex: *"tab 3. rethink how we present these two topics to the student. currently
they do not show the core concept at play. it should be an animation of the
fundamental concept please"*

## What was wrong

Tab 3 was cut to its two rules in V3.713 and the prose is right. The **sims** are
not. Diagnosed one at a time:

**`fall` (§3.1) was animated but the animation computed nothing.** Dots fell,
recycled at the top, and never landed; the content lived in two bars and a
big-type ratio beside them. So the moving part was decoration and the concept —
*an open vessel drains a column of air, and the dose is a lottery over sessions* —
never appeared anywhere. A student could watch it for a minute and learn that
particles fall down, which they knew.

**`carry` (§3.2) was not an animation at all**, and it was not the concept
either: it was a *consequence ledger* (a grid of vessels at risk). It asserted
"p ≈ 1" as a premise and then spent the whole widget on the blast radius. The
fundamental thing — what a contact actually hands over, and why one is enough —
was never shown, never computed, and never on screen.

## The concept both sections actually share

Both rules are about **a count of founders**, because of one fact neither section
stated: *one founder is enough*. In a warm flask of medium designed to grow
things, a single organism doubles every half hour, and:

| founders delivered | cloudy after |
|---|---|
| 1 | 13.3 h |
| 10 | 11.6 h |
| 1 000 | 8.3 h |
| 10 000 | 6.6 h |

A **ten-thousand-fold** difference in dose buys **6.6 hours**. So the dose is not
the question; *whether the count reached one* is the question. That reframes the
whole tab:

- **Rule 1** — the count has a **floor you cannot remove** (you have to open the
  vessel to work), it is small, and it is linear in the clock. So the rule is
  *minimise*, and the lever is time.
- **Rule 2** — the count has a **floor of zero** (every contact is optional) and
  its size is set by a surface density spanning three decades on surfaces that
  look identical. So the rule is *never*.

That is the honest reason the two rules have different grammar, and it is
stronger than the old "p ≈ 1" assertion, which was not even true for a light
brush against a wiped deck.

## §3.1 — `fall` rebuilt: the sim *is* eq. 1.1.4

1. **The drained column.** The mouth drains a column of air that grows upward at
   v_s. eq. 1.1.4 read as a volume: N = C · (A v_s t). A 60 mm dish sweeps
   **60 cm in 3.3 min** and drains **2.55 L in five minutes**.
2. **The organisms in the box are drawn honestly.** The drawn box is
   0.36 × 0.60 × 0.36 m = **78 L**. On the bench that box contains **3.1 viable
   organisms**; in the cabinet, **0.0078** — so the cabinet's box is *visibly
   empty*, which is the single best image for why "do it in the hood" outranks
   everything else in the lesson.
3. **The dots landing and the equation are the same statement.** Each drawn
   organism falls at v_s, wraps, and is captured if it crosses the mouth's
   footprint (tested in 3-D, so the 2-D projection does not over-count).
   Expected captures over the play = C·V_box · (v_s t / h_box) · (A / A_floor)
   = **C v_s A t exactly** — the animation evaluates eq. 1.1.4 rather than
   displaying it.
4. **Two acts at two time scales**, because the asymmetry *is* the section's
   point: the open period time-lapsed (×50 at the default), then the reach-over
   at **real time**. Three seconds visibly outweighing five minutes is a picture,
   not a ratio in 23 px type.
5. **The odds are drawn as sessions.** P = 1 − e^(−N) (eq. 3.3.2) over a grid of
   40 sessions like this one; 4 light up on the bench, none in the cabinet, where
   the caption reads "you would need about 3,900".

## §3.2 — `carry` replaced by `seed`: the contact, and the exponential after it

1. **Plan view of the surface, speckled at σ.** The contact patch descends as a
   circle of area A_c and picks up what it covers — so **N₀ = f σ A_c**
   (eq. 3.3.3) is *visible as an area relationship* rather than asserted.
2. **σ is a band, not a number**, and the band is the argument. Four surfaces,
   spanning three decades, with published spreads:

   | what the tip brushed | σ (CFU/cm²) | band |
   |---|---|---|
   | the wiped deck of the cabinet | 1 | 0.03 – 30 |
   | the neck of the bottle | 10 | 1 – 100 |
   | your own glove, mid-session | 50 | 5 – 500 |
   | your sleeve, or anything outside | 700 | 100 – 5 000 |

3. **The odds axis carries the punchline.** On a log axis from 10⁻⁵ to 1, the air
   route (5 min open in a cabinet, 1 in 3,900) sits at the far left; a 1 mm²
   brush of the wiped deck is **12×** worse at its point estimate and **340×** at
   the top of its band; a sleeve is **8,200×** worse and is certain. The deck's
   band alone spans *from better than leaving it open to three hundred times
   worse*, on a surface that looks the same either way — which is exactly why the
   rule cannot be a number.
4. **Growth is the second canvas.** N(t) = N₀ 2^(t/t_d) (eq. 3.3.4) on a log
   axis, with t_vis = t_d log₂(c_vis V / N₀) (eq. 3.3.5) marked, a flask beside it
   filling with turbidity, and the **1-founder curve drawn alongside** so the
   6.6-hour gap between one founder and ten thousand is on the picture. When N₀ <
   1 the two curves coincide — the timeline does not care how you got in.
5. **Mycoplasma keeps its teaching, now derived**: t_d ≈ 3 h, saturates in 3.3
   days, and *no visibility line exists*, so the only marker on the axis is the
   testing interval.

## Decisions taken while building it

**The odds axis became a *founders* axis.** The first build plotted P on a log
axis. A probability axis **saturates**: a sleeve's band runs 78 % to 100 % and
collapsed into a 12-px stub at the right edge, so the most dangerous case was the
least visible one on the picture. The count does not saturate, and it has the
only threshold the section cares about marked on it — **one**. §3.2 now says so
in prose as well, because it is a real reason and not a graphics convenience.

**Everything animated reads "so far".** The first build showed a session-final
headline (98 %) beside bars that were still empty because the reach-over had not
happened yet: two time bases in one panel. The running value now drives the
headline *and the session pips*, with a separate "by the end of this session"
line, so the grid filling as the hand passes over is the payoff.

**Act 3 puts the lid on.** It used to hold the totals with the hand still
hovering over a vessel the caption called CLOSED. Closing it is the rule of the
section, so the lid is drawn and the column stops.

**Compact branches at phone width.** `setupCanvas` derives height from the
rendered width, so a 660×340 canvas is 184 px tall at 390 px and a twenty-row
readout becomes a pile of overlapping text. Both new canvases now have a compact
layout under ~300 px (six rows, label and value on one line; and for `seed`, the
plan view drops and the founders axis survives, because that is the half with the
threshold on it). Stats and verdict were factored into `fallReport` /
`seedReport` so the two widths cannot drift.

⚠ **This is a module-wide pre-existing condition, not something introduced
here.** Every other canvas in all four lessons is equally squeezed at 390 px —
`cv-tou` at 358×168 is already a pile of overprinted text. Fixing the other 15
canvases is a separate job and is not attempted here.

## Safety of the advice (the V3.710 register)

The honest finding — that a light brush on a freshly wiped deck is only ~12× the
air route — could be read as permission. It is not, and the section says why
without arguing with the reader: the band's *width* is the point, you cannot tell
which end you are on by looking, and a contact costs nothing to avoid. The
instruction stays flat: **the tip touches nothing but the medium**. Reasoning
lives here, in the plan doc.
