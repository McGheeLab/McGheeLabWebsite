# V3.188 — Paper writing: peer review &amp; revision

## Goal

Teach the post-submission half of paper writing: what reviewers do, what
you owe them, and how to write a response letter that lands. V3.163's
overview has a short revision-letter section (§ 7); V3.188 goes deep —
both sides of the peer-review transaction, the canonical response
shapes, the tone discipline, and a response-letter builder that
enforces the format.

## Format

Lecture-style page (V3.182/V3.183/.../V3.187 pattern): sticky TOC + 10
sequential `.training-section` blocks + two interactive widgets.

### Widgets

1. **Reviewer-comment classifier** — paste a reviewer comment; regex
   heuristics classify it as one of six types (methodological,
   statistical, literature, interpretation, stylistic, scope-creep) and
   the tool surfaces a response-template suggestion appropriate for
   that type.
2. **Response-letter builder capstone** — form-based: pick the response
   shape (agree / partial / disagree), enter the reviewer's comment
   verbatim + your response + the change made + manuscript reference,
   tool renders the canonical point-by-point block. Tone checker flags
   defensive language. Repeat-able — the rendered blocks stack as a
   reviewer-letter preview.

## Sections (10)

1. Why peer review exists — the system, the social contract.
2. The reviewer's six jobs — rigor / novelty / completeness /
   writing / unstated-limitations / experiments-to-add.
3. What reviewers actually look at when they sit down to read.
4. The six reviewer-comment types (interactive classifier here).
5. The author's job — what you owe the reviewer.
6. The three response shapes — agreement / partial / disagreement.
7. How to disagree with a reviewer (the highest-stakes skill).
8. The editor as your ally — when to escalate.
9. Common author failure modes.
10. Capstone — response-letter builder.
