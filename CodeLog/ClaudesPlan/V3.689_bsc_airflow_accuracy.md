# V3.689 — Cell Culture Lesson 1: the airflow, checked against the actual manual

## Why

Alex read the BSC lesson against the operating manual for the cabinet in our
room (Thermo 1300 Series A2, `7021355-EN Rev 20`) and raised four things. Three
turned out to be accuracy problems in the physics, not presentation problems,
and one of them means the shipped lesson taught **the wrong model for our
cabinet.**

| Reported | What was actually wrong |
|---|---|
| "we don't talk about the air flow at the base of the BSC at all — how is air flowing to and from the rear grille" | the lesson drew two grilles and then stopped. Where the air goes after a grille — most of its journey — was absent |
| "the BSC has a suggested height for the sash marked at the side of the frame and it alarms if it is not there, why would the manufacturer be so strict (see the user manual)" | the lesson's answer was "fixed blower, so raising the sash halves your inflow." **For this cabinet that is false** |
| "in 2.1 it shows the air flow is downwards throughout the cross section — this is not accurate" | correct: seven vertical arrows straight to the work surface. Real downflow splits |
| "3.3 is misleading — we need to distinguish contaminants moving in from outside air, and contaminants dropping from the user's sleeve and gloves. It makes it seem there is no penalty at all to moving parallel" | correct: §3.3 counts one route and its verdict read as though that were the whole account |

Sources: the manual, plus the UNR biosafety manual chapter 8 for the general
Class II picture. Everything asserted below is either quoted from the manual or
arithmetic on its own specification table.

## The finding that changes the lesson

The 1300 Series A2 specification table gives, for a 4 ft cabinet:

| | 8 in work position | 10 in work position |
|---|---|---|
| Downflow volume | 829 m³/h | 829 m³/h |
| Exhaust volume | 468 m³/h | 585 m³/h |
| Inflow velocity | 0.53 m/s | 0.53 m/s |

A cabinet has exactly two openings to the room, so inflow volume = exhaust
volume. Solving eq. 1.2.2 for the width at 8 in gives **w = 1.207 m**; using that
width to *predict* the velocity at 10 in — a genuine prediction, since no 10 in
figure was used to obtain it — gives **0.530 m/s**. And 585/468 = 1.2500 against
10/8 = 1.2500 exactly; the 6 ft models give 878/702 = 1.2507.

**The exhaust volume tracks the opening height, so the velocity is held constant
by design.** This cabinet has SmartFlow compensation and DAVe monitoring; it
raises the volume rather than losing the velocity. The shipped lesson's
fixed-*Q* model — and its worked example computing "0.35 m/s, 69% of the
minimum" — is right for an older single-speed cabinet with a manual damper and
wrong for ours.

Which makes the answer to Alex's second question much better than the one we
shipped: **the position alarm fires while the airflow is still perfectly in
specification.** What is lost off the mark is not velocity, it is *certification*
— every NSF/ANSI 49 test the cabinet has passed was performed at the marked
opening. The manual's own numbers: tolerance **+0.25 in / −0.75 in**, a physical
dimple on the frame, a green LED that turns red, and a certification test that
requires the alarm to sound when the window is raised **one inch**.

## What was built

### §2.1 — two canvases instead of one

- **`cv-flow`, the circuit.** The whole loop as a side section: chamber, both
  grilles, the **drain-pan air duct under the work tray**, rearward along the
  bottom, up the **duct between the inner and outer back wall** (with the
  **paper catch grids** at its foot), into the top plenum, two blowers, two HEPA
  filters, and the split back into supply and exhaust. Tracers walk real
  polylines round it. A ledger column carries the manual's volumes and the
  in = out identity. 64% of what the grilles swallow is recirculated at the 8 in
  position (not the 70% everyone quotes — that is a rule of thumb).
- **`cv-flow-face`, the opening.** Zoomed to ~20 cm of depth, with the sash
  gauge drawn as a real scale showing the mark and the asymmetric tolerance band.
  The downflow streamlines **bend forward into the front grille** and never reach
  the work surface, which is the manual's own smoke-test acceptance criterion:
  *"no smoke shall billow over the solid work surface or penetrate onto it."*
- **The split is drawn and named.** Forward of the dividing streamline the air
  leaves at the front grille; behind it, at the rear.
- **The model compensates.** `vIn(sash)` holds 0.53 m/s until the blower reserve
  runs out near 13 in, then falls as 1/h. A new scenario chip, *an older,
  fixed-blower cabinet*, keeps the old behaviour so the two can be compared —
  because the old model is right for plenty of cabinets and students should know
  which kind they are standing at.
- Prose: a new **"The half of the journey you cannot see"** section with the
  volume table and three consequences (the rear grille is not decoration; the air
  under the tray is moving backwards; the cabinet breathes its own air). The sash
  section is rewritten around the mark and the alarm, with a new worked example
  that *proves the compensation from the data label*. Two new check-yourself
  questions: what blocking the rear grille breaks, and why an in-specification
  anemometer reading at an uncertified opening proves nothing.

### §3.4 (new) — the route that moving parallel does not fix

`shed` widget. §3.3 counts room air through the curtain, which depends entirely
on crossing the sash plane. This counts the other route: gloves and cuffs shed
continuously, the 0.32 m/s downflow carries it **straight down**, and the only
question is what is underneath. A plume model — spread grows with fall height and
with hand speed, fraction into a dish by numerical integration of a 2D Gaussian.

The two scenarios that make the point are mirror images at the same hand speed:

| | room-air route (§3.3) | shedding route (§3.4) |
|---|---|---|
| moving parallel, fast, **over** the dish | **0** | **344 particles/min into the medium** |
| in and out fast, but **beside** it | **567 per job** | 59/min |

The manual supports the distinction without spelling it out: *"do not cause air
turbulence by quick hand, arm or body movements **in the sample chamber** or in
front of the work opening"* — two places, not one. §3.3's parallel verdict was
downgraded from `good` to `warn` and now says explicitly that it counts one route
only, and §3.3's prose gained a scoping paragraph.

Also in §3.4: raising your hand *lowers* the fraction landing in one vessel and
that is not good news — dispersion is not removal.

## Verification

- `check.py`: all four lessons **PASS** (L1 now 11 widgets, 25 sliders).
- Behaviour QA over all five tabs: zero dead sliders, zero dead scenario chips,
  zero blank canvases, zero page errors. One dead control found and fixed —
  `s-flow-down` reported nothing after the stats were rewritten; it now drives the
  downflow volume through a filter-face area calibrated to the manual's own pair
  (829 m³/h at 0.32 m/s).
- The compensation model reproduces the manual exactly: 468 m³/h and 0.53 m/s at
  8 in, 585 m³/h and 0.53 m/s at 10 in.
- The §2.1 glass invariant still holds: **zero** particles drawn past the glass
  plane above the sash, at every sash height.
- Responsive 1200 / 900 / 700 / 640 / 380 px: no overflow, no blank or sub-150 px
  canvases. MathJax 19 containers for 18 equation blocks.
- Screenshots read for every new and changed view; eight drawing bugs fixed that
  way, including streamlines that descended vertically and then skimmed along the
  bench like water in a gutter.
