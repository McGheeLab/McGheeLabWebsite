## The design, as it now is

Two parts, not one. An **aluminium box** that never leaves the incubator, and a thin
**plastic drawer** that slides out of it carrying the dish. The dish comes off the drawer
onto a **37 °C heat plate** in the cabinet, the work happens, and it goes back the same
way. No lids to manage, no filter count to choose — the drawer front closes the box and
that is the end of the subject.

This gives the aluminium **two separate roles**, and the whole section turns on the fact
that they are worth wildly different amounts:

- to **your dish**, while it is in there — a shield against everyone else's door openings;
- to **the chamber** — warm surface and thermal mass against less free air, so the box
  re-warms faster after *every* opening, for every culture in it.

## The structure Alex asked for

**1 · The problem, stated before any solution.** A culture cannot stay in the incubator.
Three separate problems, kept apart because different fixes solve different ones:

| | |
|---|---|
| 1 | the dish is off 37 °C for minutes — a temperature problem |
| 2 | the dish is off 5% CO₂ for the same minutes |
| 3 | the chamber is disturbed by everyone's openings and has to climb back |

And the scale, before ranking anything: somebody else's door opening moves a 35 mm dish by
**0.1 °C**; five minutes on a cold work surface moves it by **eight**. Problem 1 is two
orders of magnitude bigger than problem 3 and is the one nobody talks about.

**2 · Five candidate solutions**, each with what it fixes, what it costs and when it wins:
be quicker · a 37 °C heat plate · a plastic carrier · a resident aluminium box · a warmed,
gassed stage on the microscope. Plus one non-solution (more medium) with §2.3's reason for
why it is not one.

**3 · Measure three of them** — four configurations, the 2×2 of (box, heat plate).

**4 · Conditions for when each is best**, as a rules block keyed to out-time and to whether
the incubator is shared.

## What the sim says

Same five-minute trip, all four configurations:

| | worst T | worst CO₂ | door alone | chamber re-warm τ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| box + heat plate | **0.86 °C** | 1.28% | 0.04 °C | **5.3 s** |
| heat plate only | 0.86 °C | 1.28% | 0.11 °C | 5.9 s |
| box only | 8.07 °C | 1.28% | 0.04 °C | 5.3 s |
| neither | 8.07 °C | 1.28% | 0.11 °C | 5.9 s |

Read down the columns and the ranking is not close:

- **the heat plate is worth ~7 °C** and is worth it with or without a box;
- **the plastic drawer is worth the walk** — 2 mm of polycarbonate is a 4.8-minute lag,
  comparable to the dish's own 5.7, so thirty seconds of carrying costs almost nothing;
- **the aluminium box is worth nothing measurable** to your dish on the trip, and 0.07 °C
  against somebody else's door — a disturbance that was already too small to matter;
- **nothing on the list fixes the CO₂** except being quicker. The dip is 1.28% in all four.

Out-time sweep, which is what the "when is each best" rules are built from:

| out for | with heat plate | bare |
|---|---|---|
| 1 min | 0.40 °C | 0.40 °C (all of it is the walk) |
| 2 min | 0.50 | 3.18 |
| 5 min | 0.86 | 8.07 |
| 20 min | 1.25 | 14.52 |

## The chamber, which is what Alex asked to be surfaced

One 5 mm box adds **0.21 m²** of 37 °C surface and **2.6 kJ/K** of mass, and takes **6 L**
of air out of circulation. On the new **eq. 2.5.2** that is a re-warm τ of **5.3 s** against
**5.9** bare, and a chamber swing of **2.68 °C** against **2.82** — about a tenth off, for
everything in the incubator including cultures belonging to people who never built a box.

Which is the point worth making twice: judged as protection for your own culture the box
loses to a hot plate by two orders of magnitude; judged as an improvement to the shared
incubator it is the only item on the list that does anything at all. §5.2 then does the
arithmetic for a shelf full, where a tenth becomes a half.

## Equation numbering

τ_air = ρcV_free/(hA_warm) is genuinely new and is genuinely written in tab 5, so it takes
**eq. 2.5.2**. It would have been easy — and wrong — to recycle **eq. 2.3.5**, which the
old §5.1 used for gas through a filter and which this rewrite no longer needs there: the
index registers numbers permanently. eq. 2.3.5 instead **moves to §5.2**, where the drawers
genuinely are behind membrane filters and the model genuinely calls `tauGas`. The index
line now says where it is spent, and the "tabs 4 and 5 add no new equations" note becomes
"tab 4 adds none and tab 5 adds exactly one".

## A consistency trap avoided

"No need to talk about lids or filter count" is an instruction about the *lesson*, not
about the *physics*. Modelling the box as open-fronted would have made §5.2's whole
free-volume argument false — eight open boxes displace only their own metal, not 5.9 L
each. So the drawer front still closes the box and the gas coupling is still there in the
model; it is simply not a control and not a topic. With the drawer out, the box's front is
open, so it fills with **chamber** air rather than cabinet air — which also deletes the old
"70-minute climb back through the filters" penalty, because that was an artefact of a
sealed box that this design does not have.

## Third pass — the zoomed panel was lying

Alex: *"5.2 bottom plot is not plotting correctly."* He was right twice over, and both
defects were **created by the zoom** — they had been in the code the whole time and could
not be seen on a four-hour axis.

**1 · Nothing was clipped.** `Chart` clamps Y but not X. On a panel showing a 40-second
window out of a four-hour run, every sample outside that window maps to an x thousands of
pixels away, and the segment joining it to the next paints a horizontal line across the
entire canvas — over the stats panel, the illustration, everything. The burst-shading
`fillRect` did the same. Fixed with a shared `clipPlot(ctx, ch, fn)`, applied to both
widgets. *A chart helper that clamps one axis and not the other is a trap that only springs
when someone zooms.*

**2 · Every record was stamped one step early.** `H.push({t, ...})` ran *after* the state
update, so the record labelled `t` held the state at `t + dt`. On a coarse 15-second step
that put the chamber's cooling fifteen seconds before the door opened, and left a false
discontinuity where the fine sub-steps met the coarse ones. Both `boxRun` and `sysRun` now
stamp `t + dt` and seed an initial record at t = 0.

The corrected panel: flat at 37 °C until 0 s, down to **34.72 °C** exactly at the 8-second
mark, exponential recovery on τ = 4.0 s, both culture traces flat throughout — which is
the section's argument, drawn correctly for the first time.

*Worth keeping: a zoom is a test. Two defects that had survived every numeric check and
every full-axis screenshot showed up the moment the x-range got small enough to resolve a
single step.*

## Fourth pass — scaling, and a name that was already taken

Alex: *"5.2 the plot is not scaled properly on the x and y axis. it should show the
temperature variation and it going back to equilibrium."*

**The window was fixed while the event is not.** 45 s of x-axis for an event that finishes
in 25 at the default dwell and 60 at the longest. Now `dwell + 5·max(τ_air, τ_bare)`,
clamped 22–80 s, so the excursion and the return to equilibrium fill the panel whatever the
dwell. Y gained 10% headroom above 37 so the flat culture traces read as flat rather than as
the top edge — while the *floor* still comes from the bare-shelf dip, deliberately, so that
converting shelves shrinks the drawn excursion instead of rescaling it away.

**A fixed tick step cannot survive an auto-scaled range.** At a 2-second dwell the CO₂ span
is 0.57% and a 0.5 step put one label on the axis. Both widgets now take ticks from the
framework's `niceTicks()` and precision from a new `tickDecimals()`.

**And the metal's benefit is now drawn.** A dashed bare-shelf reference on the zoom shows
the same opening without drawers: deeper dip, slower recovery. It was previously only a
parenthesis in a stat row.

### The one that matters

Writing that, I added `function niceTicks(...)` — **a name the framework already used**, 1170
lines earlier, with a different return type. Function declarations in the same scope
silently shadow, so `drawTherm` and `drawBuff` — tabs 1 and 2, nothing to do with this work
— started receiving an object where they expected an array, drew nothing, and threw **no
console error at all**. Two blank canvases and five dead sliders, on a page reporting
zero problems.

The blank-canvas assertion in the regression harness caught it; no amount of looking at
tab 5 would have. *Before adding a global helper, grep for the name.* And this is the third
member of the same family in this lesson — the `Chart` constructor gaining a side effect,
`isAdmin()` raising inside an `||`, and now a shadowed declaration: **a shared name or a
shared class changing behaviour is invisible at the site you changed and fatal somewhere
you were not looking.**
