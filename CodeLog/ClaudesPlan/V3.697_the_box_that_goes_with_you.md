Both halves of that invalidate what V3.696 shipped.

**The box already has membrane vents.** V3.696's §3.3 was built on a plain-lid box,
where one gap sets both the door-opening damping and the bench-trip recovery and you
cannot tune one without the other — and it landed on the Cell Locker's membranes as
the way out of that trade. **The lab's box already is that design.** The section was
walking the student to a destination they were standing on.

**And it is not a resident, it is a carrier.** V3.696 modelled the box sitting in the
incubator soaking up door openings, with an occasional bench trip. The real use is the
opposite: the box's job happens *outside* the incubator, during the media change, and
the thing it is buffering is not the door — it is the cabinet.

## What the physics says once the workflow is right

Modelled as two lumped masses (box, plate + 19 mL medium) and two interior gas
species through one full cycle, on the equations §3.3 already had — eq. 2.3.4
(`τ = ρct/h`), eq. 2.3.5 (`τ_gas = VL/(D_eff·A)`, now with the filter area) and
eq. 2.3.3 (dew point). Checked in `cycle.py` before any canvas code.

Key environment number: a cabinet is **not** still air. 0.32 m/s of downflow roughly
doubles what a plate loses compared with a bench, so `h_BSC ≈ 14 W/m²K` against 8 in
still room air and 10 in the fan-stirred incubator.

**It works, and the number is about five degrees.**

| | plate at the end of the work |
|---|---|
| bare on a rack, 10 min | **25.6 °C** |
| in the box, 10 min | **30.4 °C** |
| in the box, 4 min | **33.8 °C** |
| in the box, 25 min | 25.4 °C (bare would be 22.5) |

**And there is a bill nobody would guess.** The box comes home cold into a chamber at
93% RH whose dew point is 35.7 °C. Vapour crosses four Ø10 mm filter discs with
τ ≈ 4 min; 2.5 kg of aluminium re-warms with τ ≈ 20 min. **The humidity gets there
first**, so the box is wetter inside than its own surfaces are warm, and it condenses
on the plate for the best part of an hour:

| filters | τ_vap | rains for | water | CO₂ back to 5% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (soaked) | — | not at all | 0 g | **never** |
| 1 | 14 min | 38 min | 0.05 g | 68 min |
| 2 | 7 min | 56 min | 0.21 g | 34 min |
| **4 (ours)** | 4 min | 64 min | **0.54 g** | 17 min |
| 8 | 2 min | 67 min | 1.15 g | 9 min |

So the filters are **not** a compromise between containment and gas exchange — a
0.2 µm membrane already gives both, which is why the lid has them. They are a
compromise between **how fast the CO₂ comes back** and **how much water condenses on
the plate first**, and the referee is **how much heat you let out of the box**.

Which makes *minutes with the lid open* the only control that improves everything at
once — plate warmer **and** less water, because it is the same degrees Celsius doing
both jobs. Wall thickness does not (thicker = warmer culture, longer rainstorm: 10 mm
gives +5.5 °C and 82 min of rain, 1 mm gives +2.7 °C and 41 min). Filter count does
not (it trades one against the other).

Two more results worth keeping:

- **The box reshapes the excursion rather than removing it.** The bare plate recovers
  faster on the way back because it has a tenth of the mass. A deep short chill
  becomes a shallow long one — a good trade for cells, and a trade.
- **"Spray the box, not the vents" is a real failure mode with a real magnitude.** A
  wet hydrophobic membrane passes no gas, which converts the box into the sealed case:
  no rain, and the CO₂ never comes back at all. Same failure as a wet vented flask cap
  in §3.1, and it is now a scenario chip.
