# V3.966 — The Atlas in 3D: nests, with terms and papers orbiting the nest they belong to

**Date:** 2026-11-09 · **Branch:** `scientia/atlas-nests` (on `scientia/quiet-power`, V3.965) · **Gabe's request:** "update the Atlas to be
more user friendly and less clunky and way more modern… Navigating it is a nightmare with clicking and dragging between dense networks.
Assemble three different methods to shape the Atlas and I will tell you which version we will settle with." Three clickable shapes were made
(Constellations, Orbit, Nested — https://claude.ai/artifact/HHMuJC69vtnTbpFacsR1rF); Gabe: "I like nested, let's do that, except that the
nest is in 3D with orbit of terms or papers around the most closely related nest to make the whole atlas more compact at large scale while
very navigatable when focusing on individual nests."

## The shape

- **Nests.** The notebook is a sphere. Projects are spheres packed inside it; each note is a sphere packed inside its project, holding its
  components and structures as dots. Packing in three dimensions (`NEST.pack`, greedy on 34 directions) makes a whole notebook compact from afar.
- **Satellites.** A term, a paper or a person orbits the nest it is most tied to: the note holding at least half of its mentions and
  citations; else the project holding at least half; else the notebook. They sit on a tilted ring just outside the nest (the tilt and spin
  come from the nest's id), each at its own angle. Faint threads from the focused nest's satellites to the components that use them say why
  they orbit there.
- **Flying.** Click a nest to fly into it, one level at a time (`NEST.step`, like the prototype); click the space around the focus to fly
  back out; the crumbs at the top are the levels, ⤢ is the whole notebook. Drag turns the camera round the focus, the wheel (or pinch)
  comes closer, double-click opens. Esc unfocuses, then flies out.
- **Threads.** Hover a thing to see what it is tied to: a term's components, a component's terms, papers, people and wires. The wires inside
  the open note are drawn on their own.
- **Names.** The focus's children, its satellites, the dots of the open note, the hovered and the lit; a name that would cover another is
  left out, the nearer ones first.
- **The camera** (`NEST.cam`): a target point, a zoom (pixels per world unit at the target plane), yaw and pitch; perspective from a fixed
  eye distance (`PF`), things far behind the target fade a little (fog). Nothing animates at rest: the canvas is drawn on input and during a
  flight only.

## Where it lives

- **`scientia/src/14d-nest.js` (`NEST`)**, new. It takes over `ATLAS.render` and, while the Atlas page is on screen, `rebuild`, `search`,
  `focusOn` and `size`; the bar (`ATLAS.bar()`, factored out of `render` in 14b), the kind chips, the lens, the action chips, `/collection`
  (`collRow`), `act`, `panelDraw` and the time slider are the Atlas's own and unchanged.
- **14b's 2D engine stays** (`tick`, `draw`, `fit`, `hit`, `start`): the note-term maps (14c) run on it through `NTERM.as`.
- `22-atlas.css`: the crumbs, the tools, the canvas.
- The graph is `ATLAS.graph()` as before (lens, chips, collection, days); a component's note comes from `M.block(id).docId`, so a collection
  (no note nodes) still nests its components in their notes.
