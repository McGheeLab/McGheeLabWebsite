# V3.842 — Regions around cards (named, coloured, collapsible, displacing), region instances across boards, organise the board by its connections

**Alex (2026-10-22, mid V3.841):** *"we should be able to draw regions around multiple items in the canvas, these regions
should be named and have their own color. regions should also be collapsable in the canvas open it up and items in its
way are smoothly displaced without overlap."* · *"regions from any figureboard or notebook can be added to any other
figureboard as an instance that inherits and syncs between one another."* · *"connections made between items are used
to auto group and reorganize the board."*

Engine work on the plane (`whiteboard.js`, `whiteboard-board.css`, `whiteboard-replay.js`); the hosts gain one tool each.

## 1. Regions — `type: 'region'` items

- **Doc** `{ type:'region', pageId, x, y, w, h, title, color, collapsed, z }` on the board's `items` subcollection
  (same rules as every item). Rendered in the cards layer BELOW the cards (`.wb-region`, z 1): a tinted, bordered box
  with a header — colour swatch (palette of six), the name (an input), ▾ collapse, ⋯ remove (two taps) — and a corner
  grip for its size. Membership is **geometric**: the cards whose centre lies inside the box. Nothing to join or leave.
- **Draw one**: Tools → ▭ Region (engine tool `group`; the figure board's ⬚ "region → panel" stays as it is). Drag a
  box; the region takes the cards inside it, the name "Region N" and the next colour.
- **Move** by its header: the region and its members move together. **Resize** by the grip.
- **Collapse** → the box folds to its header; its members (and their wires) are hidden on every screen. **Open** →
  the members come back, and any other card overlapping the opened box is **displaced** along the shortest way out
  (down or right, in cascades until nothing overlaps), written in one batch, with a 300 ms CSS transition on the
  moving cards.
- Export draws the box + name; recordings carry regions (`itemEventPayload` kind `region`) and replays rebuild them.
- Tidy / avoid-overlap / mirrors ignore regions.

## 2. Instances across boards — `type: 'regionref'` items

- **Doc** `{ type:'regionref', pageId, x, y, w, src: { coll, classId, boardId, regionId }, title }`.
- The instance subscribes to the SOURCE board: the region doc (rect, name, colour), its items (those inside the rect)
  and its strokes (those inside the rect), and renders them read-only, scaled to its own width: compact summary
  cards for the items (kind · title · summary — the source board's full renderers belong to the other host) and the
  ink painted into a canvas. It follows the source live. Header: ⧉ name · "from <board>" · open the source.
- **Add one**: Tools → ⧉ Region from another board: an engine picker lists the user's notebook boards and the
  figure boards of papers they author, then the regions on the chosen board.
- **Sync direction**: source → instance, live. Edits happen at the source (one truth, no conflict rules). Readers
  who cannot read the source board (a co-author looking at an instance of my private notebook) see "no access to
  the source" — the Firestore rules on the source decide, as everywhere.

## 3. Organise by connections

Tools → ⇶ Organise: the wires among cards form a graph; each connected component (≥ 2 cards) is laid out
left → right in layers (longest path from its sources; rows ordered by their parents to limit crossings), placed one
below another from the view's top-left, and wrapped in a region "Flow k" (an existing region that already contains
exactly that flow is reused and resized). Loose cards stack in a column to the right. Wires inside a flow leave
east and arrive west. One batch write, the same displacement transition.

## Decisions
- Geometric membership (no member lists): drawing a box around cards IS grouping; dragging a card out leaves it.
- Regions are plain items: no new rules, exports and replays already iterate items.
- Instances mirror one way. Two-way "sync between one another" needs a merge policy for simultaneous edits; parked
  until the one-way mirror has been used.
