## Three quantities the lesson had never carried

**1 · Displaced volume.** Each drawer takes ~5.9 L of external volume out of
circulation — its interior is behind a 0.2 µm filter and does not join the bulk
exchange. Six drawers take 165 L of free air down to 129 L.

**2 · Which makes the exchange faster, not slower.** The buoyancy flow through the
doorway is a property of *the doorway*, so

```
τ_x = V_free / Q_door
```

and eq. 1.5.1's clock runs **faster** in a full box. Less absolute air comes in (54 L →
52 L) but it is a larger share of a smaller chamber, so **the CO₂ dips further**: 3.36%
in an empty incubator, 3.02% with six drawers, 2.25% with fourteen.

**3 · The bill.** Whatever CO₂ left has to be put back from a cylinder: `V_free × ΔC`.

| | |
|---|---|
| per opening, 8 s dwell | **2.56 L** |
| per day, 10 openings | 26 L |
| per year | ~6,400 L |
| a 9 kg (20 lb) cylinder | **6.3 months** |

That last number is checkable against the cylinder in the corner, which is why it is
worth having.

## The finding

**The gas bill is a door-discipline meter, and the sensitivity is brutal.**

| | cylinder life |
|---|---|
| 4 openings/day, 2 s dwell | **52 months** |
| 10 openings/day, 8 s | 6.3 months |
| 10 openings/day, 30 s | 3.0 months |
| 40 openings/day, 45 s | **0.7 months** |

A **75× range**, entirely from how the door is used. Nothing on the page is about the
incubator's efficiency; it is §4.1 with a price attached.

**And the metal is a straightforward twentyfold win.** Three stainless shelves are
0.8 kJ/K. Six 5 mm drawers are 16.4. The incoming slug has to be warmed to 37 °C and
draws that from whatever mass is present (eq. 2.1.1), so the mass in the chamber moves
**1.31 °C per opening bare** and **0.06 °C with six drawers**.

## Which reverses §3.3's conclusion at a different scale

§3.3 found a drawer to be a poor damper *for the plate inside it*, because the medium's
own 18-minute diffusion lag had already done that job. At chamber scale the same object
is excellent — and **the benefit is collective**: the metal does not know whose culture
it is protecting, so a shelf full of drawers stabilises the flasks of the person who
never made one.

The gas effect is collective too and points the other way: a full incubator dips further
on every opening, and only the cultures behind a filter are shielded from it. **A
half-converted room is the worst of both** — deeper gas dips for everyone, protection
only for the converted. That is not a technical problem and does not have a technical
fix, which is the same shape as §1.2's traffic map.
