## 2. The touch-off error — mine, and it had spread

This is a real correction to a real mistake. The module was teaching *touch off against
the vessel wall*, which is what the general-purpose pipetting literature says (Corning's
"Top Pipetting Tips", cited here for five other habits). **In cell culture it is wrong**:
the wall of an open vessel has been in room air, and a tip that touches it goes on to
touch a stock. The tip contacts nothing but the medium.

It had leaked into five places: §2.4 entirely, §2.1's closing insight, §3.1's aerosol
widget verdict, the tab-4 capstone (two steps and two scored choices), the tab-5 budget's
own checkbox label, and the sources note.

### The physics is better after the correction, not worse

A drop meets the bulk medium at **θ = 0** — a liquid has no contact angle against itself
— so eq. 3.4.2 returns the work of *cohesion*:

`W_medium = γ(1 + cos 0) = 2γ`

which is **the ceiling of eq. 3.4.2**. No solid can reach it. For medium:

| Receiving surface | W (mJ/m²) | vs a fresh tip |
|---|---|---|
| stays on the tip (fresh PP) | 61 | — |
| a TC-treated wall | 103 | 1.68× |
| **the medium itself** | **116** | **1.90×** |

And the transfer mechanism differs in kind, not just degree: a solid transfers by renewing
contact *area* (needs a drag), the bulk liquid **coalesces** (needs only a touch).
`η = 1 − exp(−(W_recv/W_tip)(contact/L₀))` with `L₀` = 0.35 mm for coalescence, 2 mm for a
solid — 0.5 mm of contact gives 93% into the medium against 34% on a wall.

**The aseptic rule and the accuracy rule turn out to be the same rule.** That is a much
stronger section than the one it replaces.

A second contradiction fell out of this: §3.3's `sub` scenario warns about foam while the
prose called submerging the correct move. Both are true of *different actions*, so the
section now separates them explicitly — touching off is the first stop and a coalescence,
blowing out is the second stop and a jet, and under the surface a jet makes foam.

## 4. The three sims that asserted what they did not compute

**`aero` — a magic damping constant.** `damp = wall ? 0.10 : sub ? 0.02 : 1.0`, multiplying
the stopping distance. A surface does not *shorten* a droplet's stopping distance; it
**intercepts** it. Replaced by a gap: the scenario sets the distance to the nearest
surface and one comparison — `s_stop ≥ gap` — decides everything. `gap = 0` means the tip
end is in bulk liquid, so the jet coalesces and no droplet forms at all. Nothing asserted,
one equation. The horizontal scale now follows the gap (a fixed 30 cm axis put a 1 mm gap
in the first 1% of the panel) and the *outcome* is in the stats, not only the verdict —
the stopping distance is a property of the droplet and did not move with the scenario, so
the panel looked inert when the thing that decides had changed.

**`bridge` — a hard-coded `COSTH = 0.7`** for every surface, which made glass and
polypropylene threads behave identically. §2.1 now *computes* θ from surface energy, so
Jurin uses that: a glass neck lifts 112 mm where a polypropylene cap lifts 9 mm. The
material became a real variable and the constant is deleted. Its stat also reported `2r`
under a label the slider used for `r`.

**`path` — split.** Its settling half (eq. 1.1.4 over 96 tip mouths) was honest physics
and becomes the new §3.1 as `fall`. Its crossed-tip corridor was choreography, and its
shed-particle number was one the prose then argued against — both gone.

### New widget `fall`

eq. 1.1.1 into eq. 1.1.4 and nothing else — no fitted constants, no scenario multipliers.
Four vessels, a time, a particle size, and a cabinet checkbox:

| | in the cabinet | on the bench |

## 5. §1.1 animated (widget `piston`)

The claim of §1.1 is *the pipettor moves air and the liquid follows*. A static picture can
show the air column; only an animation shows the **following**. The stroke now plays
itself — rest, push, dip, release, dwell, withdraw — and the liquid is drawn **twice**:
where the air says it should be (dashed) and where it actually is (filled). The gap
between them is the point.

The lag is `τ = R·C` — the same eq. 3.2.3 §2.5 derives, so the numbers here come back later
with a name attached. New liquid slider: PBS τ = 3.4 ms and the liquid is welded to the
piston; **Matrigel τ = 339 ms and it trails by up to 45 µL of a 100 µL draw**, then catches
up during the dwell. The slider is display during play and authority when dragged
(AUTHORING §9), resuming after 3.5 s.

## 6. §2.3 (widget `dep`)

The wetted region was a fixed 3.5 px stroke — it said *where* the film was and nothing
about how thick. Now a band of magnified thickness (×150, stated, capped, dashed when off
scale) that follows the cone's taper, with the µm figure on a leader. The cap is keyed to
the half-width at the middle of the wetted length; keyed to the tip's very end it clipped
every case.

**"Down on the bottom" removed** — scenario, verdict branch and reachable depth. It was an
assertion dressed as a simulation: the widget computes film area and that verdict talked
about throttled inflow, which nothing there models. The rule stays in the prose, pointing
at §2.5 where the 1/r⁴ mechanism actually lives.
