00Design & rationale

Granules (modeled as cubes) are coated on their surface with fibroblasts to make them functional. Coated granules are mixed with bare inert granules at four volume ratios and cultured under passive perfusion. The panel tests the effect of functional fraction and granule size on the culture.

Because F only covers the functional granules, the coating requirement per experiment is

nF = 6 · φ · f · c · Vtot / (LF · AF)

with φ = packing fraction, f = functional volume fraction, c = target surface coverage (1 = 100%), Vtot = granule-bed volume per well, LF = functional granule side, AF = footprint area of one fibroblast. Two consequences drive the whole workflow:

  1. Inert size never enters nF. Any two combinations that share the same functional size and ratio need identical F, regardless of their inert size. At f = 1 there is no inert granule at all, so all-functional combinations of the same functional size are one experiment — which is why the number of distinct experiments is fewer than every F×I×ratio permutation.
  2. Coverage is a fixed F-per-volume loading (6φc/(LF·AF)), independent of ratio. So you coat one master pool of functional granules per size, then split it into any ratio afterward and coverage stays at your target. This is the coat-once, split-later workflow.
Assumptions — confirm before running. (1) The functional and inert granules are Bioconjugated LLS™ and Inert LLS™ microgel respectively, so the mixed granule bed is the LLS bed loaded into the sample well; no separate support LLS is added. If your granules are rigid scaffolds dispersed in separate LLS, tell me and the loading step changes. (2) Fibroblasts coat functional granules only; inert granules stay bare. (3) Ratio f is a volume fraction of the total granule solids. (4) Granules are coated individually in suspension (all 6 cube faces exposed). (5) Vtot is the packed granule-bed volume per well. Because each condition is loaded as a slurry in an equal volume of medium, the ≈2×Vtot suspension must fit the 100 µL sample well — keep Vtot ≲ 50 µL, or aspirate the supernatant after the pack-down spin. Your earlier notes used 20 µL.

03LLS™ equilibration 15–30 min active · >2 h wait

Δ CRITICAL Start ≥ 2 h before sample prep. Equilibrate every pool your plate needs — one per (functional size) and one per (inert size) actually used, listed in the calculator's Master batch table — in separate labeled 15 mL tubes. Hold equilibrated LLS at 37 °C until use.
  1. Centrifuge each LLS™ tube 800 ×g, 5 min, moderate accel/brake.
  2. Confirm separation of clear buffer above semi-transparent LLS™ gel.
  3. Aspirate the excess buffer until only LLS™ gel remains.
  4. Add cell culture medium at 2:1 medium:LLS (e.g. 4 mL medium per 2 mL LLS) and pipette-mix thoroughly with a wide-bore tip.
  5. Hold at 37 °C until proceeding to coating.

04Prepare granule pools 15 min

From the calculator's Master batch table, measure the required bulk volume of each equilibrated pool into a labeled tube. Re-centrifuge 800 ×g, 5 min and aspirate excess medium so you start coating from packed granules.

NOTE Keep the two inert pools bare and set aside at 37 °C — they receive no cells and are added only at the ratio-mixing step.

  1. Add the calculated fibroblast volume (calculator, Cell susp. for that functional pool) to the packed functional granules. Add just enough medium to form a very light LLS — enough that mixing fully resuspends the granules, but not so much that the cells fall straight through; everything should then settle on its own. Pipette-mix gently with a wide-bore tip.
  2. t = 0 — spin. Centrifuge gently 200 ×g, 5 min to settle the cells onto the granules, then gently resuspend to redistribute and re-orient them. Do not remove the excess medium — keeping the light-LLS consistency is what lets every later mix resuspend fully and settle back on its own.
  3. Mix every 20 min for 1 h — at 20, 40, 60 min — by gentle pipette-mixing or simply rolling the tube. Never vortex. Periodic mixing keeps cells from clumping; over-mixing also drives clumping, so mix just enough to redistribute, then let it settle on its own.
  4. After the final mix, gentle spin 200 ×g, 5 min to remove any air bubbles and compact the LLS.
  5. Incubate undisturbed 1 h at 37 °C so cells firmly attach and spread. Total coating time ≈ 2 h.
Δ CRITICAL Fully mix each time without introducing bubbles. Incomplete mixing lets cells clump and leaves granule faces uncoated. Keep to the calculated cell number: excess cells bridge granules into multilayers; too few leaves gaps below 100 % coverage.

06Mix the ratio conditions 20–30 min

For each condition in your plate, combine the coated functional pool with the appropriate inert pool at the calculator's per-well F vol and I vol, scaled by your wells/condition. Pipette-mix gently to a uniform dispersion.

  1. Ratios f < 1 — mix coated functional granules with bare inert granules of the chosen sizes at the volume fractions f : (1−f).
  2. Ratio f = 1 — 100% functional, no inert: the coated pool of that functional size is loaded directly.
  3. Hold each mixed condition at 37 °C if not loading immediately; re-mix gently before loading.
NOTE "F vol" uses the coated functional pool (granules + attached cells); the added cells came along during coating, so no further cell addition is needed here.

08Assemble insert & load <10 min

  1. Inspect the glass-bottom plate for damage. Seat each imaging insert with forceps until it resists — feed-media opening facing down. A loose insert means an incompatible plate.
  2. With a 1 mL pipette, add culture medium to the plate well (feed side, a) around each insert — up to a maximum of 2.0 mL. Dispense delicately.
  3. Resuspend each condition into an equal volume of culture medium and pipette-mix to a uniform slurry, fully resuspending the granules so they load evenly (loaded volume ≈ 2×Vtot).
  4. With a 200 µL wide-bore tip, dispense each resuspended condition into its sample well (b) per the plate map — onto the membrane, not the walls. Keep the slurry ≤ 100 µL per well.
  5. Centrifuge the assembled plate 200 ×g, 5 min (moderate brake/accel). The granules pack into a bed on the membrane, and the membrane is primed for passive perfusion.
  6. Using any tip on an effluent shelf, aspirate any effluent produced during the spin and return it to the feed well.
  7. Transfer to the incubator. Media perfuses and accumulates above the bed passively over ~12 h. Do not jostle the plate.
Δ CRITICAL Do not puncture the membrane with the tip. Adding LLS or media too fast disrupts the bed and loses 3D stability — a reasonable rate is 100 µL in 5–10 s.

09Maintain perfusion culture <5 min / cycle

  1. Remove the plate to a sterile workspace. Place a tip on an insert shelf and aspirate perfused media — up to 375 µL per cycle.
  2. Either return the effluent to the feed well (recycle) or replace with fresh medium (refresh).
  3. Return to the incubator. Exchange medium every 2–4 days, or when the indicator color shifts.

10Collect samples for analysis 30–60 min · optional

  1. Fill a 15 mL tube with 12 mL PBS or medium per condition set. Bring the plate to the bench without agitation.
  2. Lift inserts with forceps; collect each bed (granules + samples) with a wide-bore tip and add it dropwise to the tube.
  3. Settle 5–10 min; aspirate supernatant and excess LLS™, taking care not to draw off the granules.
  4. Resuspend gently in PBS/medium — do not vortex — and repeat the settle/aspirate wash 2–3× to enrich.
  5. Proceed to downstream analysis. Residual LLS™ particles rarely interfere with staining/digestion; complete removal needs a strainer or magnetic sort.
